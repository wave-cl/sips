## Abstract

SIP-60 has a client sign and present a Move naming the exchange it is
at, on first connection, whenever that exchange has no home on record
for the account -- "a signature that changes nothing that day", so that
the home can act when an origin tells it of a channel. The rule as
written moves an account to **any** exchange the client is pointed at
that has never seen it: a wrong `--server-host`, a probe, a second
client run against a test exchange. The Move carries no origins, so
the account's real home is never told, and two exchanges each hold a
signed Move naming themselves. This document keeps the signature where
SIP-60 wanted it and takes it away where it was an accident: a client
presents a Move unasked only where the exchange it is at is the one its
own store records as home, or where its store records none; at any
other exchange it is a **visitor**, says so, and moves only when told
to. A Move a client presents on its own MUST name the home it leaves,
so that the former home is told.

## Motivation

The reference client's store is filed under one exchange -- its home
-- and re-filed when the account moves (SIP-59). The client therefore
knows, before it asks anything, whether the exchange it just connected
to is the one it lives at. SIP-60's rule did not use that: it asked the
*exchange* whether it had a record, and an exchange that had never met
the account said no, which is also what the account's home would say
after losing its state. The two cases are different to the client and
were the same to the rule.

It happened while testing SIP-81: a scratchpad tool connected to a
laptop exchange as an account homed at trunk.exchange, to ask one
question about somebody else's devices, and moved the account to the
laptop on the way in. The laptop was listed nowhere and could reach
trunk.exchange, but the Move named no origins, so trunk.exchange went
on recording itself as home; the account had two homes and one of them
was a machine that would be switched off within the hour. The Move
that undid it was the same one request.

The user's own client has the same rule. `sqex-chat --server-host` at
an exchange one has never used is a Move, silently, with nothing to
say so afterwards but `sqex-chat home` at the right exchange.

## Specification

**Home**, **Move**, **origin hint** and the **former home** are
SIP-59's; a store **filed under** an exchange is SIP-40's and SIP-59's;
`/account/home` and its record are SIP-59's.

### When a client presents a Move unasked

A client holding the account key, on connecting to an exchange `E`,
MAY sign and present a Move naming `E` without being told to, only
where **both** hold:

1. `E` has no home on record for the account (`/account/home` answers
   nothing, or a record with `since = 0`), as SIP-60 requires; and
2. the client's store is filed under `E`, or under no exchange yet.

Where the store is filed under another exchange `H`, the client is a
**visitor** at `E`: it presents no Move, reads and writes at `E` as
whatever `E` lets a member of channels ordered there do, and tells the
person that the account lives at `H` and that this is `E`. A visitor
moves only by an explicit act (SIP-59's Move as the client's `move`
command performs it), which names `H` and every origin the client
knows, as it does today.

A store filed under no exchange is a first run, and the Move it
presents is the account's first "this is my home". A client that can
be told a home some other way -- a restore (SIP-48), a domain the
person gave it -- SHOULD prefer that to the exchange it happens to be
pointed at.

### What an unasked Move names

A Move a client presents on its own (rule 2's first case: the store's
own home has lost the record) names as origins what `move` would name:
the exchange the store was filed under, and every origin of every
channel the client holds. That exchange is `E` itself here, and the
list is what lets `E` gather what SIP-60 promised.

### The residue of a Move by accident

A visitor at an exchange `E` whose record names **`E` itself** as the
account's home -- a Move a client from before this document presented
there, never displaced because nothing told `E` of the later one --
signs a Move naming `H`, the store's home, and presents it at `E`. It
names nowhere the account is not: `H` is what the store is filed under
and what `H` records. `E` then records the account as away at `H` and
serves it as a former home does (SIP-59, SIP-81). The client says it
did this.

### At the former home

Nothing new: a home told of a later Move naming another exchange --
by the client, or by that exchange over `/peer/moved` -- records it
and answers `moved` for the account's home-only routes (SIP-59), and
answers the home's device list (SIP-81). What this document adds is
that a client never causes the case where it is *not* told.

## Rationale

**Why the store's word and not the exchange's.** The exchange can only
say whether *it* has a record. The store says where the account lives,
because SIP-59 re-files it on every Move the account makes from this
client, and SIP-42 and SIP-72 keep a second device's store in step. An
exchange with no record is either a stranger or a home that lost its
state, and only the store can tell which.

**Why a visitor is allowed at all.** A person pointing a client at
another exchange may mean to: to read a public channel there, to ask
where somebody lives (SIP-60), to admit themselves (SIP-24). None of
those is a Move, and refusing to connect would make the client unable
to do the thing SIP-60's locate exists for. Saying so is enough.

**Why the first run still moves.** A fresh store at an exchange the
person typed is the person choosing a home, and there is no other
source to prefer where none was given. It is stated as the case this
document does not close: a fresh store pointed at the wrong exchange
moves there, as it did before, and the person sees the home named on
the first screen.

## Compatibility

Client only. No wire change and nothing at the exchange. A client from
before this document goes on presenting Moves wherever it is pointed;
an account moved that way is recovered as today, by a Move back. The
reference client's `ensure_home` answers which of the three cases it
found, and prints the visitor case.

## Security considerations

**A Move is the account's word.** Nothing here changes who may move an
account: the key holder, by signature. It changes when the reference
client uses the key on the person's behalf, which is the only party
this document binds.

**A stranger exchange learns less.** Under SIP-60's rule an exchange
learned, from any connection by an unfamiliar account, a signed Move
it could present anywhere as the account's current home until a later
one displaced it. A visitor gives it a connection and nothing signed.

## Reference implementation

`sqex` 0.99.1, client only. `Store::filed_under` reads the exchange the
store was claimed under; `Chat::ensure_home` answers `HomeSaid::{
Presented, OnRecord, Visitor { home, told }, NotMine }`, presents a Move
naming this exchange only in the first case and with the origins
`move` would name, and in the visitor case presents one naming the
store's home where this exchange recorded itself; `sqex-chat` prints
the visitor case. Test (`reaching_flow`): a store filed under A,
pointed at B, is a visitor and B records nothing (with SIP-60's rule
alone it moved, the negative control); a record B was given by accident
is displaced on the next visit; an explicit `move` then moves.

Run live on 2026-09-20: the laptop exchange still recorded itself as
`mover-e`'s home from the accident described above, with an `issued`
older than trunk.exchange's; `sqex-chat home` pointed at it as
`mover-e` said "you are a visitor here (this exchange thought it was
home, and has now been told)" and the laptop's record then named
trunk.exchange; a second visit said only "visitor".

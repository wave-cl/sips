## Abstract

A member posts to a channel at the exchange they are connected to, whether
or not that exchange originated it. A replica (SIP-35) accepts the member's
signed post and forwards it to the origin over the peering link it already
holds; the origin verifies it exactly as it verifies a post from a directly
connected member, orders it, receipts it, and answers; the replica hands the
answer back and pulls the entry as it pulls everything else. SIP-35's "one
origin per channel" stands unchanged. What falls is "only the origin accepts
posts": the origin *orders*, and any exchange holding a copy is a way to
reach it.

A copy also carries what is not an entry: a read mark set at a copy
goes to the origin as a post does and every copy pulls the origin's
marks; a signal -- typing, a call's ring state -- sent at a copy goes
to the origin and every copy hands the origin's signals to its own
members as if sent there. And a copy reports a device's chain **as its
own entries show it** when the origin cannot be asked, and serves a
device the heads after each of its positions it holds, so a device can
rebuild its chain from an exchange without a sibling and without a log
of its own.

## Motivation

SIP-35 gave a channel copies that outlive its origin, and SIP-39 gave two
exchanges a way to work for each other's users. Between them a person on
`trunk.exchange` can *read* a channel that lives at `squic.org` from home,
and cannot say a word in it without connecting to `squic.org` -- which is
the model SIP-39's motivation names as the one email spent thirty years
escaping: a client holding a connection to every domain it has something to
say to.

Nothing about ordering needs the member at the origin. SIP-31 made every
entry device-signed and chained, so an entry is a self-contained claim
whoever carries it; SIP-34 made the origin's order a signed commitment; and
SIP-35 made a replica a party the origin already peers with, authenticated
by key and gated by the members' own signed act. A post carried from the
replica to the origin is the same bytes the member would have sent the
origin, with one more hop it cannot alter. What was missing is the hop.

*Added 2026-09-17, was SIP-54.* The sections above carry a member's *acts* to the origin: posts, joins,
leaves, files. The first draft said what it did not carry -- "a signal is ephemeral and a read
mark is soft state; both stay where they were sent" -- and that was the
right first cut, since neither is an entry and nothing verifies them. It
is also, in use, the thing a member at a copy notices first: nobody sees
that they have read, they see nobody typing, and SIP-39 §Group calls across exchanges had to have a
client ring itself from the invitation entry because the `Ringing` event
never reached a copy.

Neither needs signing. SIP-16 already takes a read mark on the connection's
word and a signal on the sender's, delivers both at most once, and stores
neither for long. Carrying them one hop -- copy to origin, origin to
copies -- keeps every property they had, and adds only a pull a replica
was already making.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-77.* SIP-53 §What a client does with a fork found
that a device cannot rebuild its chain from what an
exchange serves: an action's signing input is never transmitted, so the
head after an action is known only to the device that signed it and to
the exchange that receipted it. The document made the client keep every
head it computes, which is right, and does nothing for a device that
kept none -- a store lost, or a device linked after the fact -- at the
one moment it needs a position below the latest.

That moment is a rehome at a copy whose origin is gone (SIP-53). The
copy holds the device's entries and actions up to where its pulls
stopped; it will rebuild every device's chain from them the instant it
orders the rehome; and until that instant it answers `Info` with zero,
because the origin it would have asked is the origin that is gone. The
device signs the rehome at the position it remembers, which is above
what the copy holds, and is refused `broken_chain` by an exchange that
could have told it exactly where to sign.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian. **Origin** and **replica** are
SIP-35's terms. Nothing here changes what either stores. **Chain**, **position**, **head**, `my_chain_seq` and
`my_chain_head` are SIP-31's; "pull" is SIP-35's.

### Where a channel lives

`POST /channel/home`, a SIP-16 `ByChannel` request with type `0x18`, is
answered to any identity that may read the channel:

```
Home | origin[32] | domain_len: u8 | domain |
```

`origin` is the SIP-9 key of the exchange that orders the channel: this
exchange's own key where it is the origin, the pinned key it replicates from
otherwise. `domain` is where the origin is reached by SIP-33, as the operator
recorded it, and empty where none was. As in SIP-39 §The peer directory, the domain is a hint
and the key beside it is what the hint must resolve to; a client that
discovers the domain and finds another key MUST NOT use the listed one.

A channel that is not here is refused as `/channel/info` refuses it.

### What a replica is told

SIP-32's constitution covers a channel's visibility, name and topic in a
digest, which a replica holds and cannot invert; so a replica has written
every pulled channel as private and unnamed, and refused a public room's
would-be joiners as if it were not there. A peering route states them:

```
PullShape | type: u8 = 0x07 | channel[32] |
Shape     | visibility: u8 | name_len: u8 | name | topic_len: u16 | topic |
```

`POST /peer/channel` is gated exactly as `/peer/pull` is. A replica asks
once per channel it holds and records the answer; a private channel's name
and topic are empty here as they are in the origin's own store (SIP-32). An
origin from before this document refuses the route, and the replica's row
stays private and unnamed, which is the reading it had.

A replica told a channel is public serves it to any identified caller, and
needs no constitution to: SIP-16 makes a public channel one anybody may
join and stores it in the clear, so a copy of it is what anybody could
have read by joining. A public roster is also not wholly in the log -- an
exchange seats a newcomer in its welcome channel without a signed entry,
by SIP-31's own rule -- so a replica that read a public channel against a
derived roster refused the members the origin seated. Posting still
requires membership, which the origin checks. A private channel is served
against the derived roster and nothing else, as SIP-35 requires.

### Where a device stands

A replica tracks no device chains, so its `Channel` answer would report a
member's SIP-31 position and SIP-17 counter as zero, and a device with a
fresh store -- the case SIP-31 made `my_chain_seq` for -- would sign from
zero and be refused at the origin. A peering route states them:

```
PullStanding | type: u8 = 0x08 | channel[32] | device[32] |
Standing     | next_chain: u64 | head[32] | msg_seq: u64 |
```

`POST /peer/standing` is gated as `/peer/pull` is. A replica answering
`/channel/info` for a channel it replicates asks the origin and reports the
origin's answer in `my_chain_seq`, `my_chain_head` and `my_msg_seq`; when
the origin cannot be reached it reports its own zeros, which a client takes
as the floor SIP-31 says they are.

### A copy reports what it holds

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-77.* A copy answering `Info` (SIP-16) to a device reports the device's chain
as the origin says it, as §Where a device stands has it. Where the
origin cannot be asked -- unreachable, gone, or from before §Where a
device stands -- the copy MUST
report instead the chain **as its own entries show it**: the position
after the highest `chain_seq` among the device's member entries and the
system entries recording the device's actions that the copy holds, and
the head after it, which is that entry's hash as SIP-34 receipted it
(the origin's `entry_hash`, held with the receipt). A copy holding no
entry of the device's reports zero, as before.

A device takes the greater of this and its own record, as SIP-31 says
of every report. Where the device's own record is higher because of
entries the copy never saw, and the device is rehoming the channel at
that copy (SIP-53 §What a client does with a fork), the copy's report is the position the
rehome MUST be signed at, and the entries between are stranded.

### The heads by position

```
Chain   | type: u8 = 0x24 | channel[32] | from: u64 |     POST /channel/chain
Heads   | count: u16 | count × (chain_seq: u64 | head[32]) |
```

Answered to a member of the channel (SIP-16's reading rule) for **the
calling device**: for each of the device's positions at or above
`from` for which the exchange holds the entry -- a member entry the
device signed, or a system entry recording an action it signed -- the
position and the head after it (the entry's hash as receipted), in
ascending order; and, where the exchange keeps a mark for the device
(an origin, or a copy that has become one), the mark's position and
head last, if higher than any held entry's. At most `MAX_HEADS` per
answer; a caller asks again from the last position returned. A copy
answers from what it holds and does not ask the origin.

A device rebuilding its chain takes the highest position answered, as
SIP-31 has it take the exchange's report; a device stepping back over a
fork (SIP-53 §What a client does with a fork) takes the position at the
fork. The answer is the
exchange's word about its own entries and is verifiable against them:
each head is the hash the receipt on that entry was signed over.

### Posting at a replica

A member posts to a replicated channel with SIP-16's `/channel/post`
unchanged, **signing under the origin**: SIP-31's signing input names the
exchange that orders the channel, so the `exchange` the member binds is
`origin` from `Home`, never the replica's key. A post signed under the
replica's key verifies at no exchange and is refused by the origin as any
forged entry is.

What a device signs for a channel -- its SIP-31 chain position and its
SIP-17 message counter -- is a fact about the origin, whichever exchange it
reached the channel through. A client MUST keep them by the origin's key,
so that one device reaching one channel from two exchanges signs one chain
and one counter; kept by the connection's exchange, the same device would
fork its own chain the first time it posted from the other side.

The replica MUST NOT store the post and MUST NOT assign it a `seq`. It
forwards it:

```
Forward   | type: u8 = 0x06 | device[32] | Post |
Forwarded | status: u16 | body |
```

`POST /peer/forward` is a peering route (SIP-35): gated by the origin's
peer list and, per channel, by a surviving `0x0b` naming the forwarding
replica or by the peer acting for the posting account, and refused
identically to every other peering route otherwise. `device` is the
transport identity the post arrived on at the replica; `Post` is the
request exactly as the member sent it, signature included. `status` and
`body` are what the origin's own `/channel/post` would have answered that
member directly -- a `Posted` on 200, a SIP-21 refusal otherwise.

The origin resolves `device` to an account from **its own** registry
(SIP-22), checks the signature, the epoch, the chain and the membership as
for any post, orders and receipts the entry, and tells its subscribers. It
MUST NOT take the replica's word for who posted: the signature names the
device and the origin's registry names the account, and a replica that lied
about either has sent an entry that fails step 1 or step 2 of SIP-31.

The replica answers the member with `status` and `body` unaltered, so a
member cannot tell from the answer whether the exchange they posted at
ordered the entry or carried it. It SHOULD pull the channel at once rather
than at its next interval, so the member's own message is readable where
they wrote it before they wonder where it went.

### Read marks at a replica

*Added 2026-09-17, was SIP-54.* A replica that receives `POST /channel/cursor` for a channel it does not
order records the mark for its own readers, as it did, **and** forwards it
to the origin as a `ForwardAction` (§A member's own acts) with path
`/channel/cursor` and
the request's own body. The origin honours that path: it resolves the
device to its account as it does for a join, and sets the account's mark
under SIP-16's own rules -- with one adjustment. SIP-16 clamps a mark to
what the exchange has delivered, and the origin delivered nothing to a
member who read at a copy; the copy did. A forwarded mark therefore first
raises the origin's delivery for that member to the mark (bounded by the
channel's top), and is then clamped as any mark is.

Every replica pulls the origin's marks:

```
PullCursors | type: u8 = 0x0a | channel[32] |
```

`POST /peer/cursors`, gated as `/peer/pull` is, answers SIP-16's `Marks`
for every present member, receipts withheld for nobody: the caller is an
exchange that holds the channel whole. A replica merges what it pulls into
its own marks, **higher wins** per account for `delivered` and for `read`,
and publishes SIP-30's `Cursor` event to its members where anything moved.
A mark only ever moves up, so merging two exchanges' views needs no
clock and loses nothing.

### Signals at a replica

A replica that receives `POST /channel/signal` for a channel it does not
order delivers it to its own members at once, as it did, **and** forwards
it to the origin as a `ForwardAction` with path `/channel/signal`. The
origin honours that path as it honours a signal sent to it directly:
queued for its members, published as a `Signal` event, and -- for a
ring state of `ringing` -- as SIP-36's `Ringing` event.

The origin keeps, per channel, a **signal log**: the last `SIGNAL_LOG`
signals it queued, each numbered, for `SIGNAL_TTL`. Every replica pulls it:

```
PullSignals | type: u8 = 0x0b | channel[32] | since: u64 |
Signals     | next: u64 | count: u16 | count × Logged |
Logged      | seq: u64 | account[32] | device[32] | kind: u8 | at: u64 | len: u16 | body |
```

`POST /peer/signals`, gated as `/peer/pull` is, answers the log above
`since`. A replica queues each for its own members exactly as if
`account`'s `device` had sent it there -- SIP-36's per-device rule for a
call state included -- publishes `Signal` and, for `ringing`, `Ringing`,
and remembers `next` for its next pull. **A signal whose `device` is
connected to the replica is skipped**: that device sent it here and it
was delivered here at the time; what the origin has is the copy the
replica forwarded.

A replica pulls both on the same cadence as it pulls entries, and a
replica that forwards a signal SHOULD pull at once afterwards, as
§Posting at a replica has it pull after a forwarded post.

### What is not carried

A signal between two members at one copy reaches the origin and every
other copy, since the origin cannot tell where a member is and a copy
holds the channel whole in any case. A read mark is a fact about a
member, not about where they read; it is the same mark everywhere.
Nothing here is signed, stored beyond `SIGNAL_TTL`, or replicated as an
entry, and an exchange from before these sections (0.71.0) that drops all of
it still conforms to SIP-16.

### When the origin cannot be reached

A post the origin has not ordered is not a post. The replica MUST NOT queue
it: a queue that later fails would have to invent the failure after the
member was told success, and a queue that later succeeds would order the
entry at a time the member did not choose, behind entries they had not
seen. It refuses with SIP-21 code `origin_away` (62) and the member keeps
their draft, exactly as if their own exchange were down -- which, for that
channel, it is.

### A member's own acts

A join and a leave are carried the same way: each is the member's own
SIP-31 action, signed under the origin as a post is, and the origin
applies it as it would from a directly connected member.

```
ForwardAction | type: u8 = 0x09 | device[32] | path_len: u8 | path | body |
```

`path` is `/channel/join` or `/channel/leave` and `body` the request as
the member sent it; the origin honours no other path on this message and
answers a `Forwarded` as for a post. An explicit join writes its `joined`
entry whether or not the caller was already present -- a member the origin
seated by welcome has no signed entry saying so, and this is the one they
can make; and the action carries a chain step the caller has spent, which
an answer of "already here" would drop and leave their next signature a
fork.

### Saying so

A client posting through a copy SHOULD add SIP-19's `Via` part, naming the
exchange it posted through by key. That is the poster's own statement,
under the poster's own signature, sealed with the body; the origin adds
nothing to the entry and the replica cannot. A reader shows it by the domain
its own pin store knows the key under -- *via squic.org* -- or by the key.

### A member's files

A file sent from a copy has to reach the origin, or a message naming it
is a message the origin's members cannot open. SIP-18's writes are carried
the same way, on the same message: `/blob/begin`, `/blob/attach` and
`/blob/detach` name a channel and go to its origin as sent; a `begin`
answered by the origin is handed back to the member with an upload number
of the replica's own, and `/blob/put`, `/blob/commit` and `/blob/abort`
naming that number are carried with the origin's number put back. The
replica stores nothing on the way -- the blob is the origin's, and comes
back to the copy by SIP-35's ordinary blob pull once the origin holds it.
The blob is sealed before it leaves the member's client either way.

### What is not forwarded

Invitation, removal, retention, redaction, rotation and envelopes still go
to the origin, and a replica refuses them naming the origin as SIP-35
requires. They are administrative, and each carries a consequence the
origin should see the actor connected for; posting, joining, leaving,
sending a file, marking read and signalling are what a member does. The
first draft carried neither marks nor signals -- "a signal is ephemeral
and a read mark is soft state; both stay where they were sent" -- and
said a later revision might, once this hop had earned it; §Read marks at
a replica and §Signals at a replica are that revision.

## Rationale

**Why the origin still orders.** SIP-35's rationale against consensus is
unchanged: there is no dispute about who assigns `seq`, so the question is
never "what is the order" but "did the origin commit to this one". A replica
that assigned even provisional numbers would create exactly the disagreement
SIP-35 exists to make impossible. Forwarding keeps one writer and adds
doors.

**Why the member signs under the origin and not the replica.** SIP-31 binds
the exchange into the signature so that an entry cannot be carried into
another exchange's copy of the same identifier and verify there. The origin
is the exchange the entry belongs to; the replica is a courier. Signing
under the courier would make every forwarded entry fail at the one place it
is checked, and re-signing at the replica would be forgery.

**Why the origin resolves the account itself.** A replica holds SIP-22's
registry only as replicated records, and the origin holds the one that
counts. Taking `account` from the replica would let a replica attribute a
device's signed words to another account of its choosing -- the signature
covers the account, so the entry would fail, but only after the origin had
trusted a claim it had no need to.

**Why not queue.** Stated in the specification and repeated because it is
the tempting shortcut. Every other exchange in this stack answers a post
synchronously with the position it gave it; a member relies on that to keep
their SIP-31 chain honest, since a post that was accepted and one that was
not are the difference between advancing the chain and not. A queue breaks
that contract in both directions.

**Why the peering link and not SIP-39's relay link.** The relay link is
dialled by domain when a call is placed and carries live media; the peering
connection is the one a replica already holds to the origin it pulls from,
authenticated and gated by the same list that authorised the copy. A post
is a request with an answer, which is what that connection carries.

*Added 2026-09-17, was SIP-54:*

**Why pull and not push.** A replica pulls from its origin on an interval
already, and the origin cannot push to a replica: SIP-35 is a request from
the copy to the origin, deliberately, so an origin never needs to reach a
copy. A pull every few seconds is the latency a copy's members already
have for entries, and a typing indicator that is a few seconds late is
still a typing indicator; a ring that is a few seconds late is still a
ring, and SIP-36's `ring_secs` is measured in tens of seconds.

**Why the origin does not filter the log by member.** It does not know
where a member is, and it does not need to: a replica holds the channel
under a `0x0b` and learns nothing from a signal it did not already have
the standing to learn. A log per channel is one list, not one per
replica.

**Why the sender's own signal is skipped by the copy it was sent at.**
Otherwise every signal sent at a copy is delivered there twice, once at
the time and once when it comes back around. The device's presence on a
connection is a fact the replica has; a nonce would be a field nobody
else needs.

**Why higher wins for a mark.** A read mark is monotonic by SIP-16's own
rule. The only question two exchanges can disagree on is which is behind,
and the answer is the lower one.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-77:*

**Why the copy's entries are the right answer.** A copy verifies every
entry it holds under the origin's receipt (SIP-35), and the receipt is
signed over the entry's hash -- which is the head the device's next
signature links from. The copy is not asserting a position; it is
reading one off entries the origin signed for. A copy that has just
become the origin rebuilds every device's chain from exactly these
(SIP-53), so the answer a device gets before the rehome is the answer
the new origin will hold after it.

**Why a route as well as the report.** `Info` says where a device
stands now. A device stepping back needs where it stood then; a device
checking its own record needs all of it. Both are one question over the
entries the exchange already holds, and answering it costs a query.

**Why only the calling device's chain.** Another device's chain is
readable from the entries anyway, position by position, by anybody who
may read the channel; but the head after a position is what the next
signature links from, and serving it as a list invites a client to sign
a chain that is not its own. Nothing here stops a member computing it;
nothing here helps.

**What was rejected.** Having the copy rebuild and keep marks for every
device as it pulls: it would be keeping state the origin keeps, for a
question the origin answers, to serve the one case the origin cannot --
and the entries answer that case already.

## Compatibility

Additive. A replica from before this document refuses a post with SIP-35's
`replicated` naming the origin, and a client that implements this document
treats that refusal as it always did. A client from before it never posts
at a replica, because it was refused there before; it also does not ask
`/channel/home`, and an exchange from before it answers that with SIP-21's
`NotFound`, which a client reads as "this exchange is the origin of
everything it serves".

An origin from before this document answers `/peer/forward` with SIP-35's
uniform peering refusal; a replica passes that to the member as
`origin_away`, which is the honest reading -- the origin cannot be reached
for this.

*Added 2026-09-17, was SIP-54.* Additive: two forwarded paths an origin honours, two peer routes. A
replica from before §Read marks at a replica (0.71.0) forwards neither
and pulls neither: its members' marks and signals stay with it, as the
first draft said. An origin from
before it refuses the forwarded paths as it refuses any it does not list,
and the replica's local delivery is what its members get.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-77.* Additive: one route, and an existing report answered where it was
answered zero. A client from before §The heads by position (0.95.0) takes a copy's
non-zero report exactly as it takes an origin's (SIP-31); a client from
after it asking a copy from before it is told zero, and falls back to
its own record. An exchange from before it refuses `/channel/chain`
with `not_found`.

## Security considerations

**A replica cannot forge, still.** It can drop, delay or refuse a post, and
it can forward one to an origin that refuses it. Every one of those was
available to the member's own exchange before this document. What it cannot
do is make the origin store anything a device did not sign, or attribute a
signed entry to another account.

**A replica sees the post in the clear as far as any exchange does.** A
private channel's body is sealed under SIP-17 and the replica holds no key;
the metadata -- who posted, when, how much -- it was already going to pull.
Nothing new is disclosed to the replica; the origin learns which replica a
member posts through, which is one more fact in a peering relationship that
already discloses the channel's shape.

**The origin's peer gate is the whole gate.** A forward from a peer with no
surviving `0x0b` for the channel, and not acting for the poster, is refused
identically to a pull -- the existence-oracle rule of SIP-35 applies
unchanged, since `/peer/forward` is reachable by any peer on the list.

**Replay.** A forwarded post carries the device's chain position; the origin
refuses a position it has already taken as it refuses any broken chain, so a
replica that re-sent a post gains nothing.

*Added 2026-09-17, was SIP-54.* **A replica can forge a mark or a signal for its members.** It always
could, for the members at it: SIP-16 takes both on the connection's
word, and the connection is the replica's to describe. Forwarding widens
who a forged one reaches from the replica's members to everybody, and
the forgery is a false "read" or a false "typing" from an account the
replica already serves. A replica that lies about its members is a
replica an admin withdraws (`0x0c`).

**The origin learns nothing new.** It already held every member's marks
from members at it; marks from members at copies are marks it would have
held had they connected to it. Signals likewise. A copy learns, from the
log, signals between members at the origin and at other copies, which it
did not before; it holds every entry those members write, so the typing
that preceded an entry is not the disclosure.

**A signal is delivered at most once per member, at each exchange that
delivers it.** A member with a stream at the origin and another at a copy
hears a signal twice, once from each, which is what two streams get.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-77.* **An under-reporting copy induces nothing.** SIP-31's rule stands: a
device never signs below its own record on a report alone. The one case
it steps down (SIP-53 §What a client does with a fork) is on its own
evidence of a fork.

**A copy cannot over-report.** Every head it answers is the hash under
a receipt it holds, verifiable by the device; a position it does not
hold an entry for it cannot answer with a head that verifies.

**Disclosure.** A device is told about its own entries, which it may
read, at an exchange that may serve them to it.

## Reference implementation

`sqex` 0.64.0; 0.71.0 (marks and signals); 0.95.0 (chains).
`sqex-proto::channel::{Home, TYPE_HOME}`;
`sqex-proto::peer::{Forward, Forwarded, PullShape, Shape}`;
`refusal::Code::OriginAway`. `sqexd` answers `/channel/home`, forwards a
post for a replicated channel over its own connection to the origin
(`replica::Forwarder`, one per `[[replicate]]` origin, which now takes an
optional `domain`) and nudges the pull loop when the origin took it, asks
`/peer/channel` once per pulled channel, and serves both peering routes
behind the SIP-35 gate. `sqex-chat` asks `/channel/home` with the first
`info` of a channel, signs and verifies under the origin from then on,
files its chain and counter for the channel under the origin's key, and
words `origin_away`. Two things building it turned up and this document
now carries: a client verifying under the key it connected to had called
every entry at a replica forged, so reading at a replica was broken before
posting there was; and the shape route, without which a public room at a
replica was a private one nobody could join.

**Marks and signals** (§Read marks at a replica, §Signals at a replica,
sqex 0.71.0): `sqexd` forwards marks and signals from a replica (after
applying them locally), honours both at the origin, keeps the signal log,
answers `/peer/cursors` and `/peer/signals`, and pulls and merges both on
every pull tick. No client change: a client at a copy asks its exchange
what it always asked. Test: a member at a copy marks read and the origin
shows it (delivered raised to the mark); a member at the origin marks
read and the copy shows it; typing crosses both ways and the sender does

## Abstract

A member posts to a channel at the exchange they are connected to, whether
or not that exchange originated it. A replica (SIP-35) accepts the member's
signed post and forwards it to the origin over the peering link it already
holds; the origin verifies it exactly as it verifies a post from a directly
connected member, orders it, receipts it, and answers; the replica hands the
answer back and pulls the entry as it pulls everything else. SIP-35's "one
origin per channel" stands unchanged. What falls is "only the origin accepts
posts": the origin *orders*, and any exchange holding a copy is a way to
reach it.

## Motivation

SIP-35 gave a channel copies that outlive its origin, and SIP-39 gave two
exchanges a way to work for each other's users. Between them a person on
`trunk.exchange` can *read* a channel that lives at `squic.org` from home,
and cannot say a word in it without connecting to `squic.org` -- which is
the model SIP-39's motivation names as the one email spent thirty years
escaping: a client holding a connection to every domain it has something to
say to.

Nothing about ordering needs the member at the origin. SIP-31 made every
entry device-signed and chained, so an entry is a self-contained claim
whoever carries it; SIP-34 made the origin's order a signed commitment; and
SIP-35 made a replica a party the origin already peers with, authenticated
by key and gated by the members' own signed act. A post carried from the
replica to the origin is the same bytes the member would have sent the
origin, with one more hop it cannot alter. What was missing is the hop.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian. **Origin** and **replica** are
SIP-35's terms. Nothing here changes what either stores.

### Where a channel lives

`POST /channel/home`, a SIP-16 `ByChannel` request with type `0x18`, is
answered to any identity that may read the channel:

```
Home | origin[32] | domain_len: u8 | domain |
```

`origin` is the SIP-9 key of the exchange that orders the channel: this
exchange's own key where it is the origin, the pinned key it replicates from
otherwise. `domain` is where the origin is reached by SIP-33, as the operator
recorded it, and empty where none was. As in SIP-46, the domain is a hint
and the key beside it is what the hint must resolve to; a client that
discovers the domain and finds another key MUST NOT use the listed one.

A channel that is not here is refused as `/channel/info` refuses it.

### What a replica is told

SIP-32's constitution covers a channel's visibility, name and topic in a
digest, which a replica holds and cannot invert; so a replica has written
every pulled channel as private and unnamed, and refused a public room's
would-be joiners as if it were not there. A peering route states them:

```
PullShape | type: u8 = 0x07 | channel[32] |
Shape     | visibility: u8 | name_len: u8 | name | topic_len: u16 | topic |
```

`POST /peer/channel` is gated exactly as `/peer/pull` is. A replica asks
once per channel it holds and records the answer; a private channel's name
and topic are empty here as they are in the origin's own store (SIP-32). An
origin from before this document refuses the route, and the replica's row
stays private and unnamed, which is the reading it had.

### Posting at a replica

A member posts to a replicated channel with SIP-16's `/channel/post`
unchanged, **signing under the origin**: SIP-31's signing input names the
exchange that orders the channel, so the `exchange` the member binds is
`origin` from `Home`, never the replica's key. A post signed under the
replica's key verifies at no exchange and is refused by the origin as any
forged entry is.

What a device signs for a channel -- its SIP-31 chain position and its
SIP-17 message counter -- is a fact about the origin, whichever exchange it
reached the channel through. A client MUST keep them by the origin's key,
so that one device reaching one channel from two exchanges signs one chain
and one counter; kept by the connection's exchange, the same device would
fork its own chain the first time it posted from the other side.

The replica MUST NOT store the post and MUST NOT assign it a `seq`. It
forwards it:

```
Forward   | type: u8 = 0x03 | device[32] | Post |
Forwarded | status: u16 | body |
```

`POST /peer/forward` is a peering route (SIP-35): gated by the origin's
peer list and, per channel, by a surviving `0x0b` naming the forwarding
replica or by the peer acting for the posting account, and refused
identically to every other peering route otherwise. `device` is the
transport identity the post arrived on at the replica; `Post` is the
request exactly as the member sent it, signature included. `status` and
`body` are what the origin's own `/channel/post` would have answered that
member directly -- a `Posted` on 200, a SIP-21 refusal otherwise.

The origin resolves `device` to an account from **its own** registry
(SIP-22), checks the signature, the epoch, the chain and the membership as
for any post, orders and receipts the entry, and tells its subscribers. It
MUST NOT take the replica's word for who posted: the signature names the
device and the origin's registry names the account, and a replica that lied
about either has sent an entry that fails step 1 or step 2 of SIP-31.

The replica answers the member with `status` and `body` unaltered, so a
member cannot tell from the answer whether the exchange they posted at
ordered the entry or carried it. It SHOULD pull the channel at once rather
than at its next interval, so the member's own message is readable where
they wrote it before they wonder where it went.

### When the origin cannot be reached

A post the origin has not ordered is not a post. The replica MUST NOT queue
it: a queue that later fails would have to invent the failure after the
member was told success, and a queue that later succeeds would order the
entry at a time the member did not choose, behind entries they had not
seen. It refuses with SIP-21 code `origin_away` (62) and the member keeps
their draft, exactly as if their own exchange were down -- which, for that
channel, it is.

### What is not forwarded

Only `/channel/post`. Membership actions, retention, redaction, rotation
and envelopes still go to the origin, and a replica refuses them naming the
origin as SIP-35 requires. They are rare, administrative, and each carries a
consequence the origin should see the actor connected for; posting is what
a member does all day. A later revision may forward more once this hop has
earned it.

## Rationale

**Why the origin still orders.** SIP-35's rationale against consensus is
unchanged: there is no dispute about who assigns `seq`, so the question is
never "what is the order" but "did the origin commit to this one". A replica
that assigned even provisional numbers would create exactly the disagreement
SIP-35 exists to make impossible. Forwarding keeps one writer and adds
doors.

**Why the member signs under the origin and not the replica.** SIP-31 binds
the exchange into the signature so that an entry cannot be carried into
another exchange's copy of the same identifier and verify there. The origin
is the exchange the entry belongs to; the replica is a courier. Signing
under the courier would make every forwarded entry fail at the one place it
is checked, and re-signing at the replica would be forgery.

**Why the origin resolves the account itself.** A replica holds SIP-22's
registry only as replicated records, and the origin holds the one that
counts. Taking `account` from the replica would let a replica attribute a
device's signed words to another account of its choosing -- the signature
covers the account, so the entry would fail, but only after the origin had
trusted a claim it had no need to.

**Why not queue.** Stated in the specification and repeated because it is
the tempting shortcut. Every other exchange in this stack answers a post
synchronously with the position it gave it; a member relies on that to keep
their SIP-31 chain honest, since a post that was accepted and one that was
not are the difference between advancing the chain and not. A queue breaks
that contract in both directions.

**Why the peering link and not SIP-39's relay link.** The relay link is
dialled by domain when a call is placed and carries live media; the peering
connection is the one a replica already holds to the origin it pulls from,
authenticated and gated by the same list that authorised the copy. A post
is a request with an answer, which is what that connection carries.

## Compatibility

Additive. A replica from before this document refuses a post with SIP-35's
`replicated` naming the origin, and a client that implements this document
treats that refusal as it always did. A client from before it never posts
at a replica, because it was refused there before; it also does not ask
`/channel/home`, and an exchange from before it answers that with SIP-21's
`NotFound`, which a client reads as "this exchange is the origin of
everything it serves".

An origin from before this document answers `/peer/forward` with SIP-35's
uniform peering refusal; a replica passes that to the member as
`origin_away`, which is the honest reading -- the origin cannot be reached
for this.

## Security considerations

**A replica cannot forge, still.** It can drop, delay or refuse a post, and
it can forward one to an origin that refuses it. Every one of those was
available to the member's own exchange before this document. What it cannot
do is make the origin store anything a device did not sign, or attribute a
signed entry to another account.

**A replica sees the post in the clear as far as any exchange does.** A
private channel's body is sealed under SIP-17 and the replica holds no key;
the metadata -- who posted, when, how much -- it was already going to pull.
Nothing new is disclosed to the replica; the origin learns which replica a
member posts through, which is one more fact in a peering relationship that
already discloses the channel's shape.

**The origin's peer gate is the whole gate.** A forward from a peer with no
surviving `0x0b` for the channel, and not acting for the poster, is refused
identically to a pull -- the existence-oracle rule of SIP-35 applies
unchanged, since `/peer/forward` is reachable by any peer on the list.

**Replay.** A forwarded post carries the device's chain position; the origin
refuses a position it has already taken as it refuses any broken chain, so a
replica that re-sent a post gains nothing.

## Reference implementation

`sqex` 0.64.0. `sqex-proto::channel::{Home, TYPE_HOME}`;
`sqex-proto::peer::{Forward, Forwarded, PullShape, Shape}`;
`refusal::Code::OriginAway`. `sqexd` answers `/channel/home`, forwards a
post for a replicated channel over its own connection to the origin
(`replica::Forwarder`, one per `[[replicate]]` origin, which now takes an
optional `domain`) and nudges the pull loop when the origin took it, asks
`/peer/channel` once per pulled channel, and serves both peering routes
behind the SIP-35 gate. `sqex-chat` asks `/channel/home` with the first
`info` of a channel, signs and verifies under the origin from then on,
files its chain and counter for the channel under the origin's key, and
words `origin_away`. Two things building it turned up and this document
now carries: a client verifying under the key it connected to had called
every entry at a replica forged, so reading at a replica was broken before
posting there was; and the shape route, without which a public room at a
replica was a private one nobody could join.

## Abstract

Three things an exchange without a whitelist needs before it can be
public, none of which the stack had: **rate limits** an exchange applies
per account to what costs it and the members something, answered with
how long to wait; a **mute**, a channel admin's signed entry that leaves a
member reading and stops them posting, enforced by the origin and shown
in the log; and a **report**, a member telling the channel's admins about
an entry, held by the exchange for the admins alone and never in the log.

## Motivation

Every exchange so far has been closed: SIP-8's whitelist, SIP-24's
admission, an operator who knows every key. That is where the design has
been safe to be generous -- 256 members, unbounded posting, a join per
request. An exchange that admits anyone has a different problem: a
stranger who posts a thousand lines a minute, a member who will not stop,
and a room's admins who cannot tell what happened while they were away.

The remedies are old and modest. What this document adds is their shape
in this stack: the limit is the exchange's and is stated in its refusal;
the mute is the admin's and is a signed entry like a removal, so copies
enforce it and members see it; the report is the reporter's word to the
admins and to nobody else, so it is not an entry at all.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian.

### Rate limits

An exchange MAY limit, per **account**, the rate of each of the following,
and MUST answer a request over a limit with SIP-21 code `rate_limited`
(18, SIP-22's, reused) whose detail is the number of seconds after which
the request would be accepted. The request is refused whole and nothing is written.

| What | Counted per | Reference default |
|---|---|---|
| `posts` | account, channel | 30 per minute |
| `signals` | device, channel | 60 per minute |
| `joins` | account | 20 per hour |
| `creates` | account | 10 per hour |
| `uploads` | account | 60 per hour |
| `reports` | account | 20 per hour |

A limit is a token bucket of the stated size refilled at the stated rate;
an exchange states its own values in configuration and MAY set any to
unlimited. A member's act carried to an origin from a copy (SIP-43) is
counted at the origin against the **member's** account, as it would have
been had they posted there; the copy MAY count it too. An admin of a
channel is subject to its limits like anybody.

`rate_limited` is a refusal to *do* and never a refusal to *read*: fetch,
info, the directory and the event stream are not limited by this
document, and an exchange that limited them would be making its members
pay for somebody else's flood.

### Mute

Two events join SIP-16's system space, carrying the actor's SIP-31
action, `arg` empty:

| `event` | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0x0f | `actor` muted `subject` |
| 0x10 | `actor` unmuted `subject` |

The actor MUST hold the admin role. `subject` MUST be a present member
who is not an admin; an admin is demoted first, as for removal. A muted
member **reads and may not write**: the origin refuses their posts,
signals, files and invitations in the channel with SIP-21 code `muted`
(68), and answers their reads, cursors and the event stream as anybody's.
Muting is per channel and per account. It ends with `0x10`, or with the
member being removed, or leaving; a member who leaves muted and joins
again is not muted.

Copies derive the state from the log as they derive membership (SIP-35)
and refuse a muted member's writes themselves before forwarding, with the
same code, so a copy does not carry to the origin what the origin will
refuse. A client shows the two events in the conversation as it shows a
removal; SIP-16's `Member` carries no mute flag, so a client from before
this document sees the entries as unknown events and the refusals as
refusals.

### Reports

```
Report    | type: u8 = 0x1d | channel[32] | target: u64 | reason: u8 | note_len: u16 | note |
Reports   | type: u8 = 0x1e | channel[32] |
Reported  | now: u64 | count: u16 | count × (id: u64 | reporter[32] | target: u64 | reason: u8 | at: u64 | note_len: u16 | note) |
Dismiss   | type: u8 = 0x1f | channel[32] | id: u64 |
```

`POST /channel/report` records a report by a present member of `channel`
about the entry at `target` (0 for the channel itself), with a `reason`
-- 1 spam, 2 harassment, 3 illegal, 4 other -- and a note of at most
`MAX_NOTE` bytes. The exchange keeps it for `REPORT_TTL`, at most
`MAX_REPORTS` per channel (the oldest goes), and tells the channel's
admins with a SIP-30 event:

```
Event Reported | 0x0b | channel[32] |
```

delivered to admins only, as `Admission` is. `POST /channel/reports`
answers the channel's reports to an admin and refuses everybody else as
`not_an_admin`; `POST /channel/dismiss` removes one, admins only. A report
**is not an entry**: it is not in the log, not replicated, not signed, and
not shown to the member reported. Reports made at a copy are carried to
the origin (SIP-43) and held there; a copy holds none.

What an admin does with a report is theirs: redact the entry (SIP-16),
mute or remove the member, or dismiss it. Nothing here does any of that
for them.

### Limits

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| `MAX_NOTE` | 1024 bytes |
| `MAX_REPORTS` | 256 per channel |
| `REPORT_TTL` | 30 days |

## Rationale

**Why the limit says how long.** A refusal that said only "too fast"
leaves a client to guess, and a client that guesses wrong either hammers
or sulks. The number is the exchange's own arithmetic and costs nothing
to state.

**Why per account and not per connection.** A flood from one person is
one person's however many devices they connect. Signals are the
exception because SIP-36 delivers them per device and a typing indicator
from two devices is two indicators.

**Why a mute is an entry.** A removal is, and a mute is a lesser removal:
it changes what a member may do and every copy has to agree on it. An
exchange-side flag would be one exchange's opinion, unenforceable at a
copy and invisible to the members, who are the ones the mute is for.

**Why a report is not.** It is one member's word about another, meant for
the admins; in the log it would be a public accusation, replicated to
every copy, signed for ever. The exchange holds it because the admins may
not be there when it is made, and forgets it because a report that
nobody acted on in a month is not going to be acted on.

**Why no operator-level ban here.** SIP-8 and SIP-24 already give the
operator the door; this document is about what happens inside a room
among people who are all admitted.

## Compatibility

Additive: two events, three routes, one event kind, one refusal code and
one reused. A
client from before this document skips the events as unknown, reads a
`rate_limited` or `muted` refusal as a refusal it does not know the word
for, and never sees a report. An exchange from before it applies no
limits, refuses the mute as an unknown event, and has no reports.

## Security considerations

**A rate limit is a cost the exchange chooses to bear, restated as one it
will not.** It protects the exchange and the members' attention, not the
members' secrets; nothing here reads a body. It can be gamed with more
accounts, which SIP-24 and SIP-8 are for.

**A mute is an admin's power and shows as one.** The entry names the
admin; a room whose admins mute freely is a room whose members can see
it, and leave.

**A report discloses the reporter to the admins** and to nobody else --
not to the reported member, not to the other members, not to a copy. An
admin who tells the member who reported them has done that themselves.
The exchange learns that one member reported another, and the reason
byte; the note is the reporter's words and the exchange stores them in
the clear, as it stores a public channel's names. A private channel's
members should know that a report's note is not sealed, and a client
SHOULD say so where the note is typed.

**Reports are not evidence.** They point at an entry the admin can read
themselves. A report about an entry that was never made points at
nothing, and an admin acts on what they read, not on the report.

## Reference implementation

sqex 0.73.0: `sqexd` limits from `[limits]` in its configuration (the
defaults above; `[0, 0]` unlimited), counted at the origin for acts
carried from a copy; the mute events, enforced on posts, signals and
uploads at the origin and at a copy that derived them; reports held per
channel, told to the admins as an event, read and dismissed by admins
only. `sqex-chat`: `/mute`, `/unmute`, `/report <seq> <why> [note]`,
`/reports`, `/dismiss`. Test: a fourth post in a minute is refused with
the seconds to wait and writes nothing; a muted member reads and cannot
signal or post, at the origin and then at a copy that derived the mute
from the log; a report reaches the admin's stream and list, is refused to
a member, and is not in the log. sigil does not yet show a mute in the
transcript or reports in a channel's admin view.

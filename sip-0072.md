## Abstract

SIP-42 has two devices of one account hand each other the history each
holds, and admits only entries of the incarnation the exchange serves
today. A channel's earlier incarnations -- a direct message destroyed
and rebuilt (SIP-16), a stray folded into the conversation (SIP-71) --
are history a device read and keeps, and the trade cannot carry them:
the sibling refuses them as *an incarnation the exchange does not
serve*, and a device that reset without keeping its signed entries has
nothing to offer. This document has a device keep an ended incarnation
whole -- entries, keys, and what it read -- as a **generation**, offer
its generations to a sibling beside what it holds live, and take a
sibling's as history under the same verification SIP-42 applies to the
live one, with the exchange that ordered the incarnation in place of
the one that serves the channel now.

## Motivation

On 2026-09-18, after three moves and three handovers, an account's two
devices tried to trade and one ended the session with *the sibling's
entries are from an incarnation the exchange does not serve*. The
sibling was not wrong to hold them: it had read that conversation. The
device was not wrong to refuse: SIP-42 says the instance an `Entries`
names must be the one the exchange states today, because merging two
incarnations under one identifier is the fault SIP-16 warns of. What
was wrong was that the only two outcomes were *merge* and *refuse*.

SIP-71 added the third. A device keeps what it read of an ended
incarnation as an earlier copy, shown above the conversation that
continues and never merged into it. That settles what a device does
with its own history; this document settles what two devices do with
each other's. Without it a phone bought after a fold sees the
conversation from the fold on, and the laptop's earlier copy stays on
the laptop -- which is the situation SIP-42 was written to end.

And a device that resets, today, keeps the plaintext it read and
discards the signed entries and epoch keys under them. A sibling can
verify only signed entries (SIP-42 §What a device sends), so the
discarded half is the half that could have travelled.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian. Terms are SIP-42's; a *generation*
is one ended incarnation of a channel as a device keeps it.

### What a device keeps of an ended incarnation

When a device resets a channel's sequence space (SIP-16, SIP-71) it
MUST keep, as a generation: the incarnation's `instance` (SIP-31); the
exchange that ordered it -- the channel's origin as the device knew it
at the reset, SIP-43 -- as the generation's **origin**; every signed
entry it holds of it; every epoch key it holds for it; and what it
read, as SIP-71 already requires. The live tables start empty. Signed
entries of an ended incarnation MUST NOT remain among the live ones: a
sequence number belongs to one incarnation, and an entry left behind
is offered to a sibling as the conversation's.

Generations are shown oldest first, ordered by the time of the first
entry each holds; their numbering is local and never travels.

### Offering

After `Hello` and **before** `Have`, a device that holds generations
sends

```
Earlier     | type = 0x09 | count: u16 | count × Generation |
Generation  | channel[32] | instance[32] | origin[32] | first: u64 | last: u64 | epochs: u16 |
```

one row per generation, in the shape of `Held` with the origin added.
A device holding no generation sends nothing: `Earlier` is optional,
and a sibling that never sends it holds none or predates this document.
It comes before `Have` so that a device answering `Have` already has it
in hand: SIP-42 has each side send `Want` on receiving the other's
`Have`, and a device that had to wait for a possible `Earlier` after it
could not tell a sibling that holds none from one that has not spoken
yet -- and two devices each waiting on the other would never trade.

### Wanting

A device that received `Earlier` decides per row, and sends its answer
**before** `Want`, so that `Want`'s arrival is still the whole of what
it wants (SIP-42 step 3):

```
WantEarlier | type = 0x0a | count: u16 | count × (channel[32] | instance[32] | since: u64) |
```

- A row whose `instance` is the one this device holds **live** is not
  wanted: the sibling has noticed a change this device has not, and the
  device's next poll settles it (SIP-71).
- A row whose `instance` this device holds as a generation is wanted
  from that generation's highest entry, where the row's `last` is
  beyond it or its `epochs` exceed the keys held.
- A row whose `instance` this device does not hold is wanted from 0.
- A row whose `origin` this device does not **know** is not wanted, and
  is never asked for: see Security. A device knows an exchange it has
  connected to, an exchange a channel of its lives at or lived at
  (SIP-43, SIP-53), and their earlier keys (SIP-40, SIP-64).

`WantEarlier` is sent only in answer to an `Earlier`, and only when it
is not empty.

### Serving

A wanted generation is answered as SIP-42 step 4 answers a wanted
channel, with its keys named for the incarnation they belong to:

```
KeysOf      | type = 0x0b | channel[32] | instance[32] | count: u16 | count × (epoch: u32 | key[32]) |
```

then `Entries` (SIP-42, `0x04`), whose `instance` field names the
generation, in pages as SIP-42 has them, then `Blob` for what the pages
name. `Done` is sent once `Want` has arrived and everything in it, and
in any `WantEarlier` that came before it, has been served.

### What a device MUST verify before storing a generation

Exactly SIP-42 §What a device MUST verify, with two substitutions: the
incarnation the `Entries` names MUST be one this device asked for by
`WantEarlier`, in place of the one the exchange states today; and the
receipts verify under the generation's **origin** as this device knows
it, and that origin's earlier keys, in place of the exchange the channel
lives at. Signatures, credentials and chains are checked as they are for
the live incarnation; an entry failing any is refused. A `KeysOf` for an
incarnation not asked for is ignored. `Entries` for an incarnation
neither live nor asked for are refused, as SIP-42 refuses them.

What verifies is kept as a generation -- signed entries, keys, and what
the keys open -- and shown as SIP-71 shows an earlier copy. A generation
imported for an incarnation the device already holds one of is merged
into it by sequence number, which is safe because both are of one
incarnation. Nothing here moves a fetch cursor or touches the live
incarnation.

### Limits

As SIP-42's. A device SHOULD bound the generations it keeps per channel
and drop the oldest past the bound; this document sets no number.

## Rationale

**Why a generation and not a second channel.** An earlier incarnation
is not a different conversation; it is the same two people before
something reset the numbering. SIP-71 chose to show it above the
conversation for that reason, and a sibling should receive it as the
same thing.

**Why the origin travels with the row.** A folded stray was ordered by
the exchange that folded it, which need not be the exchange the
conversation lives at now or the one either device talks to. Receipts
verify under the key that made them, and the importing device has to be
told which. The sibling's word for it is checked against what the
device already knows, not taken.

**Why `Earlier` comes before `Have`.** The other order deadlocks; see
the specification. Putting it first costs an older sibling the same
ended session either way, and costs a newer one nothing.

**Why `WantEarlier` comes before `Want`.** SIP-42 closes the list on
`Want`'s arrival, and a serving side sends `Done` once everything in it
is served. A request arriving after that would be answered by nothing.
Sending it first costs nothing and keeps SIP-42's end condition as it
was, which is why `Done` needed no change.

**Why a device that noticed nothing does not take the sibling's word.**
A sibling offering as *earlier* what this device holds *live* is ahead
of it, not wrong; but resetting on a sibling's say-so is exactly what
SIP-42 forbids for the live incarnation, and the exchange will say the
same thing on the next poll.

**What was rejected.** Bumping `Have` to carry origins and several
incarnations per channel: a device before this document would refuse
nothing and merge nothing, but would want an earlier incarnation as if
it were live (SIP-42 step 3, *a channel this side knows no incarnation
of is wanted as offered*) and store it under the live tables. Separate
messages keep the old path exactly as it was.

## Compatibility

Additive: three SIP-42 message types. A device before this document
that receives `Earlier` ends the session as SIP-42 says of an unknown
type, which is SIP-67's situation exactly: a device that finds the
session ended after sending one learns the sibling is older, and says
so. A device that holds no generation never sends it and trades with an
older sibling as before. `WantEarlier` and `KeysOf` are only ever sent
to a sibling that sent `Earlier` or `WantEarlier`, which an older one
never does.

SIP-42's rule *the instance an `Entries` names MUST be the one the
exchange states today* is amended: or one this device asked for by
`WantEarlier`.

## Security considerations

**The sibling is still a courier.** Nothing is stored on its word:
every entry carries its author's signature and the origin's receipt,
and both are checked. What the sibling adds is the name of the origin,
and a sibling that named a key it controls could sign receipts under
it -- so the name is accepted only for an exchange the device already
knows. A compromised sibling can therefore not manufacture an earlier
copy of a conversation under an exchange the device never met, and
under one it has met it would need that exchange's key.

**Keys of a dead incarnation.** They open nothing that can still be
written to, and travel as SIP-42's keys do: in the clear inside the
session, to a device of the same account.

**Disclosure.** As SIP-42's: two devices of one person, and the history
that person read.

**Denial of service.** A generation is bounded as a channel is; a
sibling offering many is bounded by the device's own limit on what it
keeps.

## Reference implementation

`sqex` 0.90.0, all of it in `sqex-chat`. `store`: `generation`,
`entry_history` and `channel_key_history` beside SIP-71's
`message_history`; `archive_incarnation` at the one place every reset
passes (`reset_sequence_space`), which now also clears the live `entry`
table -- before this document a reset left the signed entries where they
were, numbered against the new incarnation, and a sibling would have
been offered them as the conversation's; `generations`,
`history_entries_after`, `history_keys` and their `put_` halves.
`sync`: `Earlier` (`0x09`), `WantEarlier` (`0x0a`), `KeysOf` (`0x0b`);
`Owed` carries a generation; `serve` reads either table. `client`:
`held_generations`, `knows_exchange`, `import_earlier` -- SIP-42's
checks with the origin named in place of the exchange -- and `earlier`
ordering generations by the time of the first thing each holds.

Tests: a direct message destroyed and rebuilt, the phone keeping the
first incarnation as a generation with its signed entries and keys and
the live tables holding only the new; the laptop, which never saw the
first, receiving it as an earlier copy over a trade, verified, with the
conversation that continues untouched and a second trade carrying
nothing; a courier offering a generation under an origin the device
never met is not asked for, and one under the exchange it knows has its
altered entry refused and the rest kept. Checked by silencing the offer
and watching the laptop hold no earlier copy.

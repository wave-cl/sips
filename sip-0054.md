## Abstract

A read mark set at a copy of a channel is carried to the origin as a
post is, and every copy pulls the origin's marks; a signal -- typing, a
call's ring state -- sent at a copy is carried to the origin, and every
copy pulls the origin's signals and hands them to its own members as if
sent there. A member reading through a copy is seen to have read, sees
the others' marks, sees them typing, and is rung when a call starts at
the origin -- which SIP-43 left to the members at the origin alone.

## Motivation

SIP-43 carried a member's *acts* to the origin: posts, joins, leaves,
files. It said what it did not carry -- "a signal is ephemeral and a read
mark is soft state; both stay where they were sent" -- and that was the
right first cut, since neither is an entry and nothing verifies them. It
is also, in use, the thing a member at a copy notices first: nobody sees
that they have read, they see nobody typing, and SIP-49 had to have a
client ring itself from the invitation entry because the `Ringing` event
never reached a copy.

Neither needs signing. SIP-16 already takes a read mark on the connection's
word and a signal on the sender's, delivers both at most once, and stores
neither for long. Carrying them one hop -- copy to origin, origin to
copies -- keeps every property they had, and adds only a pull a replica
was already making.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian. "Origin", "replica" and "forward"
are SIP-43's; "pull" is SIP-35's.

### Read marks

A replica that receives `POST /channel/cursor` for a channel it does not
order records the mark for its own readers, as it did, **and** forwards it
to the origin as a SIP-43 `ForwardAction` with path `/channel/cursor` and
the request's own body. The origin honours that path: it resolves the
device to its account as it does for a join, and sets the account's mark
under SIP-16's own rules -- clamped to what the origin has delivered.

Every replica pulls the origin's marks:

```
PullCursors | type: u8 = 0x0a | channel[32] |
```

`POST /peer/cursors`, gated as `/peer/pull` is, answers SIP-16's `Marks`
for every present member, receipts withheld for nobody: the caller is an
exchange that holds the channel whole. A replica merges what it pulls into
its own marks, **higher wins** per account for `delivered` and for `read`,
and publishes SIP-30's `Cursor` event to its members where anything moved.
A mark only ever moves up, so merging two exchanges' views needs no
clock and loses nothing.

### Signals

A replica that receives `POST /channel/signal` for a channel it does not
order delivers it to its own members at once, as it did, **and** forwards
it to the origin as a `ForwardAction` with path `/channel/signal`. The
origin honours that path as it honours a signal sent to it directly:
queued for its members, published as a `Signal` event, and -- for a
ring state of `ringing` -- as SIP-36's `Ringing` event.

The origin keeps, per channel, a **signal log**: the last `SIGNAL_LOG`
signals it queued, each numbered, for `SIGNAL_TTL`. Every replica pulls it:

```
PullSignals | type: u8 = 0x0b | channel[32] | since: u64 |
Signals     | next: u64 | count: u16 | count × Logged |
Logged      | seq: u64 | account[32] | device[32] | kind: u8 | at: u64 | len: u16 | body |
```

`POST /peer/signals`, gated as `/peer/pull` is, answers the log above
`since`. A replica queues each for its own members exactly as if
`account`'s `device` had sent it there -- SIP-36's per-device rule for a
call state included -- publishes `Signal` and, for `ringing`, `Ringing`,
and remembers `next` for its next pull. **A signal whose `device` is
connected to the replica is skipped**: that device sent it here and it
was delivered here at the time; what the origin has is the copy the
replica forwarded.

A replica pulls both on the same cadence as it pulls entries, and a
replica that forwards a signal SHOULD pull at once afterwards, as SIP-43
has it pull after a forwarded post.

### What is not carried

A signal between two members at one copy reaches the origin and every
other copy, since the origin cannot tell where a member is and a copy
holds the channel whole in any case. A read mark is a fact about a
member, not about where they read; it is the same mark everywhere.
Nothing here is signed, stored beyond `SIGNAL_TTL`, or replicated as an
entry, and an exchange from before this document that drops all of it
still conforms to SIP-16.

### Limits

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| `SIGNAL_LOG` | 256 per channel |
| `SIGNAL_TTL` | SIP-16's |

## Rationale

**Why pull and not push.** A replica pulls from its origin on an interval
already, and the origin cannot push to a replica: SIP-35 is a request from
the copy to the origin, deliberately, so an origin never needs to reach a
copy. A pull every few seconds is the latency a copy's members already
have for entries, and a typing indicator that is a few seconds late is
still a typing indicator; a ring that is a few seconds late is still a
ring, and SIP-36's `ring_secs` is measured in tens of seconds.

**Why the origin does not filter the log by member.** It does not know
where a member is, and it does not need to: a replica holds the channel
under a `0x0b` and learns nothing from a signal it did not already have
the standing to learn. A log per channel is one list, not one per
replica.

**Why the sender's own signal is skipped by the copy it was sent at.**
Otherwise every signal sent at a copy is delivered there twice, once at
the time and once when it comes back around. The device's presence on a
connection is a fact the replica has; a nonce would be a field nobody
else needs.

**Why higher wins for a mark.** A read mark is monotonic by SIP-16's own
rule. The only question two exchanges can disagree on is which is behind,
and the answer is the lower one.

## Compatibility

Additive: two forwarded paths an origin honours, two peer routes. A
replica from before this document forwards neither and pulls neither: its
members' marks and signals stay with it, as SIP-43 said. An origin from
before it refuses the forwarded paths as it refuses any it does not list,
and the replica's local delivery is what its members get.

## Security considerations

**A replica can forge a mark or a signal for its members.** It always
could, for the members at it: SIP-16 takes both on the connection's
word, and the connection is the replica's to describe. Forwarding widens
who a forged one reaches from the replica's members to everybody, and
the forgery is a false "read" or a false "typing" from an account the
replica already serves. A replica that lies about its members is a
replica an admin withdraws (`0x0c`).

**The origin learns nothing new.** It already held every member's marks
from members at it; marks from members at copies are marks it would have
held had they connected to it. Signals likewise. A copy learns, from the
log, signals between members at the origin and at other copies, which it
did not before; it holds every entry those members write, so the typing
that preceded an entry is not the disclosure.

**A signal is delivered at most once per member, at each exchange that
delivers it.** A member with a stream at the origin and another at a copy
hears a signal twice, once from each, which is what two streams get.

## Reference implementation

Planned: `sqexd` forwards marks and signals from a replica, honours both
at the origin, keeps the signal log, answers `/peer/cursors` and
`/peer/signals`, and pulls and merges both in the replica task; no client
change -- a client at a copy asks its exchange what it always asked.

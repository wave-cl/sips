## Abstract

An account is its key, and the services that are *the key's at an
exchange* -- its device registry, prekeys, mailbox, wake registrations,
backup, endpoint resolution -- live at whichever exchange the person
connects to. Its channels live wherever they were created. This document
lets an account sign, once, the statement **"this exchange is my home"**.
Presented at the named exchange, the home pulls every channel the account
is in from wherever each lives and carries the account's own acts back
(SIP-43). Carried to the exchange the account left, and to every exchange
that orders a channel the account reads, it opens SIP-35's acts-for gate
to the home -- the authorisation SIP-35 calls the recommended upgrade to
an operator's word -- and makes the old home answer, on every service
that was the key's, *moved*, naming the new. The logs are untouched:
membership is in them, and the account is the same key.

## Motivation

SIP-35 lets a peer pull what a set of accounts may read, needing no
per-channel signature, "because those accounts can already fetch every
one of those entries as clients"; and it names the weakness in the same
breath: that the peer really belongs to those accounts is the origin
operator's assertion, and a private group's other members did not agree
to it. "The stronger form is an authorisation signed by the account
itself, the way `0x0b` is signed by a channel admin." This document is
that form, and the thing it makes possible is the one a person actually
wants from it: to leave one exchange for another and take their
conversations with them, without asking every correspondent's admin for
a `0x0b`, and without their correspondents losing them.

Today the alternative is starting over. A client that connects to a new
exchange has no history there, no way to reach the private channels it
was in, and no way to tell anybody where it went: a sender at the old
exchange finds a mailbox that fills and prekeys that run out, and a
caller finds nobody home. SIP-44 moves an account to a new *key* when
the old one is gone; SIP-53 moves a *channel* to a new exchange when its
origin is gone. Neither moves a person who is still here and simply
wants to be somewhere else.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian. Keys are Ed25519 public keys, 32
raw bytes in signing input, base58 in text. **Origin** and **replica**
are SIP-43's; an exchange **orders** the channels it is the origin of.

### The statement

```
HOME_CONTEXT = "sqex-home-v1"   (ASCII, 12 bytes, no terminator)

move_input = HOME_CONTEXT || account[32] || home[32] || issued[8]
Move       | account[32] | home[32] | issued: u64 | sig[64] |
```

`sig` is Ed25519 by `account` over `move_input`. A Move is portable and
self-contained, as a SIP-44 will is: anybody holding it can verify it
with no record of the account, and a hardware-held key signs it as
SIP-58 has one sign a credential. `home` is the SIP-9 key of the
exchange the account lives at from `issued` on.

**A later `issued` replaces an earlier.** An exchange holding a Move for
an account refuses one whose `issued` is not later, with SIP-21 code
`stale_generation` (64). An account moves back by signing a Move naming
the exchange it came from, and moves again by naming a third. A Move
does not expire; it is superseded.

### Presenting

```
Moving | Move | dom_len: u8 | domain
       | count: u8 | count × (origin[32] | dom_len: u8 | domain) |
Moved  | now: u64 | peered: u8 |
```

`POST /account/move` is answered to any identified caller. The statement
carries its own authority; who carries it proves nothing, and the
carrier is not checked. `domain` is where `home` is reached by SIP-33,
a hint as everywhere in this stack: the key beside it is what the hint
must resolve to. The `origins` list is where the account's channels live
as the presenter knows them -- at most 16, each an exchange key and its
domain hint -- and is meaningful only at the home; every other exchange
ignores it. `peered` is 1 where `home` is on this exchange's SIP-35 peer
list and 0 where it is not, so the client learns at once, from the
exchange that will be asked, whether the home's pulls will be served
rather than finding out from a silence.

```
PeerMoved | type: u8 = 0x0f | Move | dom_len: u8 | domain |
```

`POST /peer/moved` is the same statement over the peering link, gated
by the operational peer list and by nothing else, answered with `Moved`.
The home carries a Move it holds to each origin it pulls for the account
from, so that the client presents once. Both routes record the same
fact, and an exchange told twice records it once.

```
Ask    | account[32] |
Homed  | home[32] | dom_len: u8 | domain | since: u64 |
```

`POST /account/home` answers, to any identified caller, the home this
exchange has on record for `account` with the Move's `issued` as
`since`; or this exchange itself, with an empty domain and `since = 0`,
for an account that has registered devices, present memberships or
names here and no Move on record; or SIP-21 `not_found`. What an
exchange discloses here it disclosed already: a channel's home by
SIP-43, an account's devices by SIP-22, its names by SIP-38.

### At the home

An exchange that records a Move naming **itself** holds the account as
homed here, with the `origins` the presenter gave. It MUST pull at once,
and then on an interval no shorter than SIP-35's `PEER_MIN_INTERVAL`,
from each origin: it finds the origin by the domain hint, checks the key
found against the one named, carries the Move (`/peer/moved`), and asks

```
PullMine | type: u8 = 0x0d | account[32] |
Mine     | now: u64 | count: u16 | count × channel[32] |
```

`POST /peer/mine` is gated as `/peer/pull` is: the caller is on the peer
list **and** is the account's recorded home, or is configured as acting
for the account (SIP-35); refused identically to every other peering
route otherwise. It answers the channels this exchange *orders* that the
account is a present member of -- not the ones it holds as copies, whose
origins the home asks itself. The home pulls each as SIP-35 pulls, and
serves them and forwards the account's posts, joins, leaves, cursors,
signals and files exactly as SIP-43 and SIP-54 have a replica do. A
channel whose identifier the home already orders is skipped: a direct
message's identifier is derived from two keys and one may already exist
here (SIP-44's caveat, stated and not solved); the account reads that
one where it was.

The account registers its devices at the home as at any exchange
(SIP-22), publishes prekeys there (SIP-23), registers wakes there
(SIP-45), writes its backup there (SIP-48). Nothing of these is carried
between homes: each is the key's at an exchange, and SIP-35 says why a
prekey pool in two places is a fault and not a copy.

### At a former home, and at every origin the account reads through

An exchange that records a Move naming **another exchange** holds the
account as *away*, at `home`. Two things follow.

**The acts-for gate opens.** `home` may pull and forward for every
channel this exchange orders that the account is a present member of,
exactly as a configured acts-for peer may. SIP-35's per-channel
entitlement is the union of three: a surviving `0x0b` naming the peer;
the peer configured as acting for a present member; and the peer being
the recorded home of a present member. An implementation MUST treat
them as a union, not as modes of a peer.

**The key's services are handed off.** With SIP-21 code `moved` (69,
HTTP 403), whose detail is the home's key in base58 followed by a space
and the domain hint:

- `/mailbox/send` to the account (SIP-5), `/prekey/take` for one of its
  devices (SIP-23) and `/resolve/get` for it (SIP-28) are refused so the
  *sender* learns where to go;
- `/prekey/publish`, `/wake/register`, `/backup/write` and
  `/resolve/publish` from one of the account's own devices are refused so
  a *client* that still points here learns. An account is at one home at
  a time, which is SIP-47's shape for a device.

Channel routes go on serving the account directly: it is still a member,
and this exchange still orders the channel. What the exchange holds for
the account -- mailbox items, a backup, its registry rows -- it keeps and
serves on the reading routes until each expires by its own rule or the
account drops it. Names stay (SIP-38): a name is the domain's, `c@A`
resolves to the key for as long as its holder renews it, and a resolver
that then asks `/account/home` is pointed on.

### A device the origin never saw

SIP-43 has an origin resolve a forwarded post's `device` from **its own
registry**, and refuses to take the replica's word for the account. A
device registered only at the new home is a device the origin has never
seen; it resolves to itself, which is nobody's member, and the forward
fails. The home therefore carries the credential:

```
Carried | type: u8 = 0x0e | cred_len: u16 | credential | inner |
```

on `/peer/forward`, where `inner` is a `Forward` (0x06) or a
`ForwardAction` (0x09) exactly as SIP-43 sends it and `credential` is the
device's SIP-20 credential as the home holds it. The origin MUST verify
the credential under the account it names -- signature, chat scope,
validity at its own clock -- and that its delegate is `inner`'s
`device`. A device it does not know it registers to that account, as
though the account had presented the credential at `/device/register`:
the credential is that same signed act, and SIP-31's step 2 at every
other replica asks the origin's `/device/list`, which must then answer.
A device it knows under another account is refused. The gate is then
SIP-43's, with the account the credential names.

A home MUST wrap a forward for a device it holds a credential for, and
MUST NOT wrap one for an account acting as its own device: there is
nothing to carry. A home forwards `/device/revoke`, carrying SIP-32's
signed `Revocation`, as a `ForwardAction` to every origin it acts at
for the account, and a client SHOULD also revoke at each former home it
can reach; a credential's `not_after` bounds what a revocation nobody
carried costs.

### Calls

The caller's exchange, having resolved `callee@A` to an account (SIP-39),
asks A `/account/home` for it as it already asks A `/name/resolve`. Where
the answer names another exchange this one federates with, it places the
call there, by that exchange's domain, checking the key it finds against
the one named; once, and never chased further. An exchange from before
this document answers `not_found` and the call goes as SIP-39 places it.

### Limits

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| Origins in a `Moving` | 16 |
| Moves recorded per account | 1 (the latest) |
| Pull interval at the home | SIP-35 `PEER_MIN_INTERVAL` or more |

## Rationale

**Why the account signs.** SIP-35 gives the reason and this document
only accepts it. An operator naming a peer as acting for an account is
an operator copying that account's private channels to a machine the
members never agreed to; the members agreed to the account, and the
account's signature is the one that carries. The Move is small, signed
once, and verified by anybody who holds it.

**Why the home carries the statement.** The account's channels may be
ordered by several exchanges, and a client that had to visit each one is
a client holding a connection to every domain it has something to say
to -- SIP-39's motivation names that as the shape to avoid. The home is
already going to reach every one of them to pull; carrying the Move on
the same connection costs one request, and the client presents once.

**Why a Move is replaceable and a will is not.** A succession (SIP-44)
is an ending: the old key is gone, and a second succession would let a
will that leaked after use take the account again. A home is a choice
made while the key is very much present, and the person may make it
again. The later `issued` wins because there is nothing else to settle
it by and every holder computes it alike.

**Why the key's services refuse and do not forward.** A mailbox item
forwarded from A to B is A's assertion of who sent it made twice, which
SIP-35 rules out for signals and cursors on the same ground; a prekey
served from two pools is SIP-23's own defence firing on a normal day; a
backup is sealed to the account and the account can write it anywhere.
Refusing with the home's key costs the sender one round trip and tells
them a fact; forwarding would cost the protocol a rule about which copy
is the real one.

**Why the origin registers a carried device.** The alternative -- verify
per forward and record nothing -- leaves every other replica of the
channel unable to complete SIP-31's step 2, because they ask the
origin's registry and the origin would have nothing to say. The
credential is the account's own signed grant; presenting it is what
`/device/register` is, whoever hands it over.

**Why calls are re-placed by the caller's exchange and not forwarded by
the callee's.** A bridge with a third leg is a relay of a relay; the
caller's exchange already asks the callee's exchange one question before
inviting, and a second question is the cheapest thing that could work.
One hop, because a chain of homes is a loop waiting to happen.

## Compatibility

Additive: four routes, two peering messages, one wrapper, one refusal.
An exchange from before this document refuses `/account/move` and
`/account/home` with SIP-21's `not_found`, refuses `/peer/mine` and
`/peer/moved` as unknown peering messages, and refuses a `Carried`
forward as malformed -- which a home passes to the member as
`origin_away`, the honest reading: that origin cannot be reached for
this. A home sends `Carried` only for a device with a credential, so a
device-less account posting through its home reaches an older origin as
SIP-43 always did. A client from before this document never moves, and
finds a `moved` refusal where it expected a mailbox, which it words as
it words any refusal it does not know.

## Security considerations

**A stolen key moves the account.** A thief who holds the key held the
account already; a Move they sign points senders at an exchange of their
choosing. This document changes nothing about a stolen key, and SIP-44
is the answer to one.

**The home is trusted with what a home always was.** It holds the
account's channels in the clear as far as any exchange does -- sealed
bodies, plaintext graph -- and it holds the device registry. That is
what the account's exchange held before the move; the account chose a
different one.

**The origin's gate is the whole gate, still.** A peer that is not on
the origin's list, or is on it and is neither the home of a member nor
configured for one nor named by a `0x0b`, is refused identically to a
pull for a channel that does not exist. `/peer/mine` is reachable by any
peer and answers strangers as SIP-35 requires it to.

**A carried credential is verified, not trusted.** The origin checks the
account's signature and registers the device to the account the
credential names, never to one the home names. A home that lied about
which device it carries has sent a credential that fails, or one that
registers a device the account really did issue.

**What is disclosed.** The home, to any identified caller of
`/account/home`, and to any peer over `/peer/moved`. That the account
exists at an exchange was disclosed by SIP-22 and SIP-38 before this
document. The `origins` hints tell the home where the account's channels
are, which the home learns anyway by pulling them.

**The domain in every hint is a hint.** The key beside it is what the
hint must resolve to, and a hint that resolves to another key is
discarded.

## Reference implementation

Planned for `sqex` 0.76.0: `sqex-proto::home` (the Move, its
presentation, the answers); `sqexd` records a Move in its registry,
opens the acts-for gate to a recorded home, refuses the key's services
`moved` at a former home, pulls for the accounts homed here from the
origins they named, carries the Move to them, wraps forwards in
`Carried` and registers carried devices, and re-places a call at the
callee's home; `sqex-chat move <domain>` and `sqex home sign`; the store
re-files what it held under the old home under the new, so a moved
client reads its sealed history where it now is.

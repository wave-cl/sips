## Abstract

What a channel forgets is decided by its members -- a message's own
timer, a redaction, the retention an admin set -- and what an exchange
forgets is decided by its operator's disk. SIP-35 let a copy hold more
than its origin, which was right for the second and wrong for the first.
This document separates the two: **policy** binds every holder -- origin,
copy, a client's store, a backup, a sibling handing history over -- and
**capacity** is each holder's own. A redaction made anywhere reaches every
copy; a timed message goes everywhere at its time; a copy keeps no more
of a channel than its admin said the channel keeps.

## Motivation

SIP-16 gave a message a timer and a channel a retention, and gave a
redaction a route. SIP-35 then let an exchange copy a channel and hold it
past the origin's window, on the argument that outliving the origin's
availability is half the reason to copy -- and that argument is about
disk, an origin that pruned because it was full. It was never meant to
let a copy serve for a month what the admin set to keep for a week, or
serve a message its author deleted, and it does both: a redaction is a
change to an entry already held, and nothing carries it to a copy; a
retention is a row at the origin, and a copy has its own row.

Meanwhile SIP-42 and SIP-48 let a person keep their own history past any
exchange's window, which is the point of them, and neither says what that
history does with a message that was meant to disappear.

The line is the one SIP-16 already drew and did not name. A timer, a
redaction and a retention are the members' decisions, signed or made on
their own connection, and they are about the conversation. A byte cap is
an operator's decision about a machine.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian. "Origin" and "copy" are SIP-43's.

### Policy and capacity

A channel's **policy** is: each entry's `expires_after` (SIP-16, signed
into the entry by SIP-31); each redaction made under SIP-16's rules; and
the channel's retention -- `retention_secs` and `max_entries` -- as last
set by its constitution or a signed `0x08`. A holder's **capacity** is its
own bound on bytes or entries per channel.

A holder MUST apply policy to what it holds of a channel, whatever its
capacity: an entry past its timer is deleted, a redacted entry is a
tombstone, and an entry older than the retention is not served as the
conversation. A holder MAY apply its capacity on top, holding *less*
than policy allows; it MUST NOT hold *more*. SIP-35's "a replica may hold
more than the origin does" is narrowed to exactly that: more than the
origin's capacity let it keep, never more than the policy.

### Timers, everywhere

An entry with `expires_after` set is deleted by every holder at
`posted + expires_after` at the latest: an exchange, origin or copy
(SIP-16's backstop, unchanged); a client's store, which SIP-16 lets
delete earlier, at its own read time plus the timer; a SIP-48 backup,
which MUST NOT include an entry with a timer at all -- a backup is read
back after an unknown time, and a message meant to disappear has no
business in one; and a SIP-42 sibling, which MUST NOT hand over an entry
past its timer and MAY hand over one still within it, with the timer
intact.

### Redactions, everywhere

A redaction at a copy is carried to the origin as a post is (SIP-43): a
`ForwardAction` with path `/channel/redact` and the request's body, which
the origin honours under SIP-16's own rule -- the entry's author, or an
admin. The origin records every redaction it makes:

```
PullTombstones | type: u8 = 0x0c | channel[32] | since: u64 |
Tombstones     | now: u64 | count: u16 | count × (seq: u64 | at: u64) |
```

`POST /peer/tombstones`, gated as `/peer/pull` is, answers the positions
redacted at or after `since` (a time), at most `MAX_TOMBSTONES` and the
newest of those. A copy pulls it on its pull tick and tombstones each
position it holds: the body goes, the entry stays with its hash, head and
receipt, exactly as SIP-16's redaction leaves it at the origin -- so the
copy's chain of receipts still verifies, since SIP-31 committed to the
hash and not the bytes. A copy tells its members with SIP-30's `Channel`
event at `last_seq` 0, as the origin does. An entry redacted before a copy
first pulled it arrives as a tombstone and needs nothing.

The origin keeps its record of redactions for at least the channel's
`retention_secs`, after which a redacted entry would have been pruned
anyway.

### Retention, at a copy

A copy applies the channel's retention as its own ceiling: SIP-35's pull
already reports the origin's `window_secs`, and a copy prunes by age at
the lesser of that and its own. `max_entries` likewise, where the copy
learns it (SIP-43's shape). A copy that held an entry the origin's policy
has since passed deletes it on its next sweep.

### Limits

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| `MAX_TOMBSTONES` | 256 per answer |

## Rationale

**Why policy and capacity, and not one rule.** One rule -- "hold what the
origin holds" -- makes a copy useless as a copy: the origin's disk
becomes everybody's disk, and an origin that prunes at 128 MiB prunes
the world. The other one rule -- "hold what you like" -- makes the
members' decisions advisory. The distinction was already in SIP-16, which
prunes by the *lesser* of a channel's retention and an entry's timer
and calls the byte cap a recommended value; this document says it out
loud and applies it to every holder there now is.

**Why a redaction is pulled and not pushed.** A copy pulls; an origin
never reaches a copy (SIP-35). A tombstone list is small and the pull
tick is seconds. Carrying redactions in the entry pull itself would mean
re-serving entries a copy already holds, which the pull is designed not
to do.

**Why a timed message is kept out of a backup.** A backup is opened at a
time nobody chose, by a device that may have been off for a month. A
message with a timer either has to be deleted on restore -- which the
restoring client can do, but only if it is honest and only if the
timer had not long passed anyway -- or kept, which is the message
outliving its author's intent. Not writing it is the only version with
no way to get it wrong.

**Why a sibling may hand over a timed message.** It is still live; the
receiving device is the same person's, and will delete it at the same
time. Refusing would make a phone that woke late show a hole in a
conversation the desktop can still see, for no gain.

**Why the origin's retention and not the admin's entry.** The `0x08`
entry's values ride in `arg`, which is not transmitted (SIP-34), so a copy
cannot read the policy from the log; the origin's pull already reports
its window and that report is the origin's word about its own policy,
which is what a copy trusts for everything else it pulls.

## Compatibility

Additive: one forwarded path an origin honours, one peer route, and a
narrowing of SIP-35 that a conforming copy applies by pruning more. A
copy from before this document keeps serving redacted bodies and holds
past the policy, as it did; an origin from before it refuses the forwarded
redaction and answers no tombstones, and a copy's members redact at the
origin as before. A client from before it puts timed messages into
backups, which the restoring client of this document deletes on reading
as its own store would.

## Security considerations

**A redaction is still not a guarantee.** It never was: every reader
already read it, a client's store may keep it, and SIP-16 says so. What
this document adds is that the *exchanges* -- every copy, not only the
origin -- stop serving it, which is the part a member can actually
expect.

**A copy that ignores policy is a copy that keeps what the members
deleted.** It always could, as any holder of bytes can; the difference is
that it now does so against a stated rule, and an admin who finds it
withdraws it (`0x0c`). Nothing here can make a dishonest copy forget.

**Tombstone lists disclose which positions were redacted** to copies,
which held the bodies anyway. They disclose nothing to anybody else.

**Retention at a copy is the origin's word.** An origin that lies about
its window makes copies keep less or more; less is harmless and more is
what the origin could have served itself.

## Reference implementation

sqex 0.74.0: `sqexd` forwards a redaction from a copy, honours it at the
origin, records every redaction, answers `/peer/tombstones`, applies
pulled tombstones on the pull tick, and prunes a copy by age at the
lesser of its own window and the origin's. `sqex-chat`: `/expire <secs>`
signs a timer into what it sends; a timed message goes from the store
and the timeline at its time, is not folded once past it, is left out of
a backup, and is handed to a sibling only while live. Tests: a redaction
at the origin reaches the copy as a tombstone with the rest intact; one
made through the copy is honoured at the origin and comes back; a
non-author cannot redact through a copy; a copy's window ceiling (unit);
a timed message goes at its time from the sender's store and is never
seen by a reader who comes to it late, and a backup taken while it was
live leaves it out. sigil does not yet offer a timer.

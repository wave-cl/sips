## Abstract

SIP-65 opened calls between exchanges that do not list each other, on
the caller's own signed word and a private membership the callee already
signed for, and left rooms where SIP-49 put them: a `RoomShare` is one
exchange's assertion about who is in a room, an `InviteDevice` rests on
it, and neither had a member's signature to stand on. This document
gives them one. A member who asks its exchange to share a room with
another signs the asking -- the room, the exchange, the time -- and the
share carries that word; an exchange taking calls open honours a share
from an exchange it does not list when the word verifies, the signer is
a member the sender vouches for as its own, and the signer's account
shares a private channel with a member of the room here. A device invite
over such a link is honoured for a device the admitted share named.

## Motivation

SIP-49's live run and every one since had the same shape: two exchanges
that list each other. SIP-63 and SIP-65 made listing unnecessary for
copies and for calls, and SIP-60 made a private channel between two
people at two exchanges ordinary. A call in such a channel (SIP-36) is a
room (SIP-13), and the room is shared across the exchanges by SIP-49 --
which refuses the share the moment one of them is not on the other's
list. So the pair can write to each other, ring each other, and not
speak: the invitation posts, the ring is heard, the room is joined at
each exchange, and each member sees a roster of one.

What SIP-65 said of rooms is the reason: a share was the exchange's word
and nothing else. The fix is the one SIP-65 made for calls, applied to
the act that starts a share -- a member asking for it -- which is the
member's own act and can carry the member's own signature.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian. **Relay link**, **relay peer**,
`JoinShared`, `Homed`, `RoomShare`, `InviteDevice`, `SHARE_SECS` and
`TTL_SECS` are SIP-49's; **open** and **listed** are SIP-65's modes for
calls; the **credential** rule and the **private membership** rule are
SIP-65's ringing rules 4 and 5; **present member** is SIP-16's.

### The member's word

```
ROOM_CONTEXT = "sqex-room-v1"                       (ASCII, no terminator)
room_input   = ROOM_CONTEXT || handle[32] || peer[32] || issued: u64
RoomWord     | issued: u64 | cred_len: u16 | credential | sig[64] |
```

`sig` is Ed25519 by the member's **device** key over `room_input`, where
`handle` is the room's and `peer` is the key of the exchange the member
asks its own to share the room with; `issued` is when it was signed;
`credential` binds the device to its account as SIP-65's `CallWord`
carries one, `cred_len = 0` where the device is the account. The shape
is `CallWord`'s exactly, over a different context.

### Joining with a word

```
JoinShared | type: u8 = 0x05 | handle[32] | proof[32] | count: u8
           | count × (exchange[32] | dom_len: u8 | domain | RoomWord) |
```

SIP-49's shared join, with a domain and a word per exchange named. It is
answered `Homed` as `0x04` is. `0x04` remains valid and names listed
peers only, as SIP-49 says. An exchange taking calls **listed** refuses
`0x05` naming an exchange not on its list (403), as it refuses `0x04`;
one taking calls **open** accepts it, keeps each word beside the room
and the peer it names, finds the exchange by its `domain` (SIP-33),
checks the key, and brings up a link (SIP-39; SIP-65 §The link) where
it holds none. A word that does not verify under the joining device for
its `handle` and `exchange` is refused as malformed, and nothing is
joined. A `domain` may be empty for an exchange on the list, which the
list knows how to find.

A client SHOULD sign a word for every exchange it names, whether or not
it believes that exchange listed: the cost is a signature, and the
client cannot see the lists. A client that is shown, in a `Homed`
answer, a member homed at an exchange it has not named SHOULD join again
naming it: that is how the second side of an unlisted pair comes to hold
a word to share back with.

### Sharing with the word

```
RoomShareSigned | 0x0a | handle[32] | count: u8 | count × (identity[32] | proof[32] | home[32])
                | sharer[32] | RoomWord |
```

Over a link to an exchange **on its list**, an exchange shares as SIP-49
says, with `0x06`. Over a link to an exchange **not on its list** it
sends `0x0a` and nothing else: the share as `0x06` carries it, then the
device whose word it stands on and the word. `sharer` MUST be among the
members shared with `home` zero -- a member of the sender's own -- and
the word MUST be one that device gave the sender for this `handle` and
this peer. Sent when a `0x06` would be, for as long as the sender holds
such a word and the room has members at either end.

### Honouring a share

An exchange **Y** receiving `0x0a` over a link from an exchange **X**
not on its list keeps the share -- as SIP-49 keeps any share, soft
state until the next -- only when all of:

1. Y takes calls open;
2. `sharer` is in the share with `home` zero, and the word verifies
   under `sharer` for `handle` and Y's own key as `peer`;
3. the sharer's account `A'` is `sharer` itself where `cred_len = 0`, or
   the account the credential names, the credential verifying under it
   for scope chat, unexpired, with `delegate = sharer`;
4. Y has a **local member** of `handle` -- a device that joined the
   room here -- whose account and `A'` are both present members of some
   private channel Y holds, shaped where it is a copy (SIP-65 ringing
   rule 5); a room nobody has joined here admits no share;
5. on the **first** share of `handle` over this link, `issued` is within
   `ROOM_WORD_SECS = 120` of Y's clock and `A'` is under the `calls`
   limit (SIP-56, SIP-65). Later shares of the same room over the same
   link stand on the first while the room has members at both ends,
   and their words are checked for signature and not for age: a share
   is repeated every `SHARE_SECS` for as long as a call lasts, and a
   member is not asked to sign every ten seconds.

A share failing any rule is dropped without answer, as SIP-49 answers
no share; the sender goes on sharing and a share that later passes --
rule 4 most often, once a member has joined here -- is kept then. A
`0x06` over an unlisted link is dropped. A `0x0a` over a link to an
exchange on Y's list is verified as above and kept if it passes: a peer
that can sign should not send a word that does not verify.

**Reciprocity.** SIP-49 has the first share of a room from a peer
answered with the receiver's own view. Over an unlisted link Y answers
with `0x0a` when it holds a word from a local member of the room naming
X, and with nothing otherwise; when such a word arrives later -- a
member joining again naming X, as clients are asked to above -- Y
shares then. Until it does, X's members see Y's as absent, which is
what they are to X.

### Device invites

An `InviteDevice` over a link from an exchange not on Y's list is
honoured -- the bridge recorded, the open matched -- when `device` is a
local member of a room for which Y currently keeps a share from that
link naming `caller` among its members. It is answered `Reject
{ refused }` otherwise. SIP-49's rule that the invite rings nobody is
unchanged, and so is the bridge once it stands.

### Limits

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| `ROOM_WORD_SECS` | 120 |
| Exchanges named in one `JoinShared 0x05` | 4, as `0x04` |
| `RoomShareSigned` bytes | `MAX_CONTROL` (SIP-65's 1536) |
| First shares per account | the `calls` limit |

## Rationale

**Why the member signs the peer and not the room alone.** A word over
the handle alone would let X carry one member's wish to be seen at Y
into a share with Z. Binding the exchange named makes the word what the
member actually asked: share this room with that exchange. It is
SIP-65's reason for binding `target`.

**Why the private membership is with a local member of the room.** A
room handle says nothing to an exchange -- it holds no secret and cannot
tie the room to a channel. What Y can know is who has joined the room
here, and whether the person asking to be seen in it is somebody one of
them already talks to. That is SIP-65's consent, found through the room
rather than through the call, and it is why a room nobody has joined
here admits no share: there is nobody whose consent could be read.

**Why a word is not re-signed every share.** A call lasts as long as it
lasts and a share goes every ten seconds; a signature every ten seconds
would be a signature nobody looked at. The first share is where the
question is asked and answered, and the link, the room and the members
at both ends are what keep the answer standing. A word replayed later
over the same link re-admits the same room between the same people,
which is nothing.

**Why the domain travels in the join.** SIP-49 names peers by key
because a listed peer's key is enough to find it. An unlisted exchange
is one the exchange knows nothing about, and the member who wants it
named is the one who knows where it is -- from a `Homed` answer, a
location (SIP-60), or the person who told them.

**Why device invites need no word of their own.** The invite is X's
message, as SIP-65 says of `Invite`; what makes it honourable is that
the caller it names is a member X has already vouched for under a word
Y verified. Adding a second word would prove what the first already did.

**What was rejected.** Letting Y admit a share for a room nobody has
joined here yet, held pending: it would make Y keep strangers' rosters
on their own say-so, which is the disclosure and the cost SIP-49's list
existed to refuse. Signing the roster itself: the roster is the
exchange's knowledge and changes on every join; the member can sign
only what the member did.

## Compatibility

Additive: one join type, one relay control, and one gate opened. An
exchange from before this document refuses `0x05` as malformed, so a
client falls back to `0x04` and the listed behaviour; it drops `0x0a` as
an unknown control, so an open exchange sharing with it is not seen
there, which is where things stood. A listed exchange refuses `0x05`
naming an unlisted exchange exactly as it refuses `0x04`.

SIP-65 §What stays listed is amended: `RoomShare` and `InviteDevice`
are served over an unlisted link on the terms above.

## Security considerations

**Consent** is SIP-65's, read through the room: nobody is shown in a
room here on the word of an exchange alone, and the person whose word
it is must already share a private channel with somebody in the room.

**What a stranger exchange learns.** Nothing before a share is
admitted; after, the same roster SIP-49 discloses to a listed peer --
identities and proofs of the room's members here. It learns that the
sharer's account is known here and shares a channel with a member, which
the admission itself says. A dropped share says nothing.

**Replay.** A word names the room, the exchange and the time; it admits
the same room over a link to the same exchange, and nothing else. Within
the window, X could share a room its member has left: the member's
proof is still in the roster, the member is not, and SIP-13's session
mesh needs the member's own opens.

**Denial of service.** The first share per account is counted under the
`calls` limit, a room admits nothing until somebody here has joined it,
and what is kept is bounded as SIP-49 bounds it.

## Abstract

A channel's origin -- the exchange that orders it (SIP-43) -- can be
succeeded by one of its replicas: an admin posts a signed **rehome** entry
naming the replica, and from that position the replica orders the channel,
receipts it under its own key, and answers `/channel/home` for it. Posted
at the origin, it is a planned move; posted at the replica while the
origin cannot be reached, it is how a channel outlives the exchange it
started on. Readers verify entries under the origin that held the channel
at each position, the way SIP-40 verifies under a key's predecessors.

## Motivation

SIP-43 put a channel's copies on other exchanges and was careful about
what a copy may not do: order. A post the origin has not ordered is not a
post, and while the origin cannot be reached every write is refused
`origin_away`. That is right while the origin is *away*. When it is gone
-- the operator stopped, the domain lapsed, the disk died -- the members
hold complete, verified copies of a conversation nobody can add a line
to, at exchanges that could order it perfectly well, and no signature
exists that would let them. SIP-40 moves an exchange's key by the old
key's own statement, which a dead exchange cannot make.

What can be made is a statement by the channel's own authority. A channel
admin already decides which exchanges may hold a copy (SIP-35's `0x0b`);
deciding which of them orders it is the same authority, exercised once
more, and it is signed into the same log where every member can read it.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian. "Origin" and "replica" are SIP-43's.

### The rehome entry

One event joins SIP-16's system space, carrying the actor's SIP-31 action:

| `event` | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0x0e | `actor` moved the channel's origin to `subject` |

`subject` is the new origin's SIP-9 exchange key. `arg` is
`domain_len: u8 | domain | issued: u64`: where the new origin is reached by
SIP-33 as the actor's client knew it (a hint, as SIP-43's `Home` carries
one), and when the actor signed. The actor MUST hold the admin role.
`subject` MUST be an exchange with a surviving `0x0b` for the channel, or
the exchange the entry is posted at; it MUST NOT be the current origin.

The entry is signed under the **outgoing** origin's key, as SIP-31 binds
every entry to the place it was made: it is the last entry of the old
regime. Every entry after it is signed and receipted under the new.

### Posted at the origin: a planned move

The origin accepts a rehome from an admin as it accepts a `0x0b`, orders
it, receipts it, and from that position **closes the channel to writes**:
every later write is refused with SIP-35's `replicated`, naming the new
origin, and `/channel/home` answers the new origin. It goes on serving
what it holds, and its replicas pull the rehome entry as they pull
anything.

The named replica, on pulling the entry, becomes the origin: it stops
pulling, orders what comes next after the rehome's position, receipts it
under its own key with SIP-34's `head` continuing from the rehome entry's,
and answers `/channel/home` with itself. It accepts SIP-43 forwards from
the channel's other replicas, which it now serves as an origin serves
them, under the `0x0b` entries the log already carries.

### Posted at a replica: the origin is gone

A replica accepts a rehome from an admin **only when all of these hold**:

- `subject` is the replica itself. A replica cannot make another exchange
  the origin; the admin posts there instead.
- The origin has been unreachable from the replica for at least
  `ORIGIN_AWAY_SECS`: every pull and forward in that time failed to
  connect. A replica that reached the origin more recently refuses with
  code `origin_reachable` (66), and the admin's client tells them the
  origin is not gone, only slow.
- The replica can derive the channel's roster (SIP-35) and finds the actor
  an admin in it.
- No rehome was accepted for this channel within `REHOME_MIN_SECS`.

It then orders the rehome entry at the position after the last it holds,
receipts it under its own key with `head` continuing from what it holds,
and is the origin from there, exactly as above.

### What readers verify

A channel has an **origin history**: the exchange that created it, then
each `subject` of each rehome, from the position after that rehome's.
An entry at position `seq` verifies (SIP-31) under the origin that held
the channel at `seq`, and its receipt (SIP-34) under the origin that
ordered it -- which for the rehome entry itself is the *new* origin. A
client holds the history beside the channel and consults it as it
consults a pin's predecessors (SIP-40): a signature that verifies under
no origin in the history is forged, whichever exchange served it.

A client learns the history from the log: the rehome entry is in it. A
client that reads a channel for the first time from its current origin
reads the history from the entries as they come, and MUST verify each
rehome's actor against the roster as derived at that position.

### Telling the others

**Other replicas** of the channel pulled from the old origin. On pulling
a rehome, a replica switches its pull to `subject`, found by SIP-33
discovery of `domain` and checked against `subject`, and verifies what it
pulls there from the position it holds. Where the old origin is gone and
cannot be pulled from, a replica is told by a client:

```
Rehomed | type: u8 = 0x19 | channel[32] | entry (receipted) |
```

`POST /channel/rehomed` carries the rehome entry with the new origin's
receipt. Any identity that may read the channel may present it. The
replica verifies the actor was an admin at that position in the roster it
derives, the entry's signature under the origin it knew, and the receipt
under `subject`; then it switches, and pulls from `subject` from the
position it holds. What it cannot verify it refuses, and stays where it
was.

**The old origin**, if it returns, is told the same way -- by a client, or
by the new origin over the peering link as `/peer/rehomed` with the same
body, gated as `/peer/pull` is. It verifies as a replica does, against its
own log. Where its log ends at the rehome's position it becomes a replica
of the new origin, pulling from it under the `0x0b` the log carries for it
if there is one, and closed to writes otherwise. Where its log goes
**past** that position -- entries it ordered during a partition, which no
replica saw -- those entries are **stranded**: the old origin MUST stop
serving them as the conversation, SHOULD keep them for `STRANDED_SECS` and
serve them on `/channel/stranded` to their own authors, and a client that
finds its own entries stranded SHOULD offer to post them again. Nothing
else can be done with a fork: two orderings of the same positions, and the
members chose one.

### Two rehomes

Two admins at two replicas during one partition may each rehome the
channel to where they stand. Each replica accepted a rehome naming itself
and knows nothing of the other. When either learns of the other -- a
client carrying the other's entry, or the two exchanges meeting over a
peering link -- **the rehome with the earlier `issued` wins**, and on a
tie the lower `subject`; the loser becomes a replica of the winner and
everything it ordered after the fork is stranded, as above. A client that
holds both MUST apply the same rule, so every party settles on the same
history without a third.

### Limits

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| `ORIGIN_AWAY_SECS` | 300 |
| `REHOME_MIN_SECS` | 3600 |
| `STRANDED_SECS` | 7 days |
| Rehomes in a channel's history a client keeps | 16 |

## Rationale

**Why an admin, and not the replica's operator.** The operator holds a
copy because an admin let them (`0x0b`). Letting them also *order* the
channel is more, and it is the admin's to give: an operator who could
promote their own copy could take any channel they replicate the moment
its origin blinked.

**Why the replica must be the subject.** A replica that accepted a rehome
naming a third exchange would be asserting something about that exchange
it cannot know -- whether it holds the channel, whether it will order it.
The admin goes to the exchange they mean and says so there.

**Why an away timer, and not a liveness protocol.** The replica already
knows whether it can reach the origin: it pulls on an interval and
forwards on demand, and both fail or do not. Five minutes of nothing is
what "away" already means to a member refused `origin_away`; the timer
turns the refusal the member has been getting into permission to stop
getting it, and refuses the admin who is merely impatient.

**Why signed under the outgoing origin.** Because that is where the
actor's client believes it is posting, and SIP-31's place-binding is what
stops an entry lifting between exchanges. The rehome is the last entry
made at the old place and the first receipted at the new, and the seam is
exactly one entry wide.

**Why stranded entries are kept and not merged.** A partition produces two
orderings of the same positions. Merging would renumber one side, and a
receipt is a signature over a position: renumbered entries are receipted
entries with the wrong receipts. Their authors can post them again at the
new positions, with new receipts; nothing else is honest.

**Why earlier `issued` wins.** It needs no third party and every holder
computes it alike. A clock is forgeable, so the rule is not fairness, only
agreement; and the admin who forges an earlier time strands the other
admin's side, which is the same thing the other admin could have done to
them. A channel with two admins on two sides of a partition is a channel
whose admins should talk.

## Compatibility

Additive: one event, two routes, one refusal. A client from before this
document sees event `0x0e` as an unknown system entry and, from the next
entry on, signatures under a key it does not expect -- it will show the
channel as forged past that point. That is the correct reading for a
client that does not know origins move, and the reason this document
exists; such a client upgrades. An exchange from before this document
refuses the rehome as an unknown event and the channel stays where it
was.

## Security considerations

**An admin can move a channel anywhere it is already copied.** This is
the `0x0b` trust restated: the admin chose that exchange as a holder; now
they choose it as the orderer. Members who did not want either can leave,
and the log shows them who did what.

**A partition is a fork, and a fork loses something.** Stated above. The
protocol picks a side by a rule everyone can compute, keeps what was lost
where its authors can find it, and does not pretend the lost side was
never written.

**The away timer is the replica's word.** A replica lying about its
reachability lets an admin rehome to it early -- while the origin is
merely slow. The admin can equally wait; the harm is a planned move made
hastily, and the actor is still an admin who could have made it at the
origin. A replica cannot use the timer to take the channel: it still
needs an admin's signature.

**`domain` in `arg` is a hint.** As everywhere: the key beside it is what
the hint must resolve to, and a hint that resolves to another key is
discarded.

## Reference implementation

Planned: `sqexd` accepts and issues the rehome at origin and replica,
`/channel/rehomed`, `/peer/rehomed`, `/channel/stranded`, the away timer
and the tie-break; `sqex-chat` keeps a channel's origin history and
verifies under it, and offers to re-post stranded entries; `sqex-chat
/rehome <exchange>` for an admin; sigil shows the move in the transcript.

## Abstract

A channel's origin -- the exchange that orders it (SIP-43) -- can be
succeeded by one of its replicas: an admin posts a signed **rehome** entry
naming the replica, and from that position the replica orders the channel,
receipts it under its own key, and answers `/channel/home` for it. Posted
at the origin, it is a planned move; posted at the replica while the
origin cannot be reached, it is how a channel outlives the exchange it
started on. Readers verify entries under the origin that held the channel
at each position, the way SIP-40 verifies under a key's predecessors.

Where two exchanges ordered the same positions during a partition, one
ordering wins and the entries the other ordered are **stranded**: kept
for their authors, never served as the conversation. A client that
learns a fork it was on the losing side of files what it holds above the
fork as stranded where its own, drops the rest, reads the winning
history from the fork, continues its SIP-31 chain from its last entry
the winner holds, and offers the person their stranded posts to send
again -- each posted as a fresh entry carrying, in a SIP-19 part, when
it was first said.

## Motivation

SIP-43 put a channel's copies on other exchanges and was careful about
what a copy may not do: order. A post the origin has not ordered is not a
post, and while the origin cannot be reached every write is refused
`origin_away`. That is right while the origin is *away*. When it is gone
-- the operator stopped, the domain lapsed, the disk died -- the members
hold complete, verified copies of a conversation nobody can add a line
to, at exchanges that could order it perfectly well, and no signature
exists that would let them. SIP-40 moves an exchange's key by the old
key's own statement, which a dead exchange cannot make.

What can be made is a statement by the channel's own authority. A channel
admin already decides which exchanges may hold a copy (SIP-35's `0x0b`);
deciding which of them orders it is the same authority, exercised once
more, and it is signed into the same log where every member can read it.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-74.* The first live rehome ended with one entry stranded at the old origin
and its author's client showing it as the conversation, above a cursor the
winning history would never move past. Three things were wrong, and the
first draft had named only the third.

- **The client did not know.** It had posted at its exchange, the
  exchange had ordered the post, and the receipt was good. That the
  position was later taken by another exchange's entry is a fact the
  client learns only by reading the winning log from the fork -- which,
  with its cursor above the fork, it never does.
- **The client could not post again even if it knew.** Its SIP-31 chain
  had advanced over the stranded entry. The winning origin holds no such
  entry, so the client's next signature links to a hash the origin has
  never seen, and SIP-31's rule that a client never takes a lower
  position from an exchange -- written to stop an exchange forking a
  client's chain -- here stops the client mending its own.
- **Nothing offered to post it again**, which is what §Telling the
  others said the client SHOULD do.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian. "Origin" and "replica" are SIP-43's. **Receipt** is SIP-34's; **chain** is SIP-31's;
**part** is SIP-19's.

### The rehome entry

One event joins SIP-16's system space, carrying the actor's SIP-31 action:

| `event` | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0x0e | `actor` moved the channel's origin to `subject` |

`subject` is the new origin's SIP-9 exchange key. `arg` is empty, as
`0x0b`'s is: a system entry's `arg` is never transmitted (SIP-34), and a
rehome must be checkable by everyone who reads the log. *When* is the
receipt's `posted`, which the new origin signed. *Where* the new origin is
reached rides beside the entry, unsigned, as SIP-43's `Home` carries a
domain: in the request that posts it, in `Home` and in `Standing` from
then on, and with the entry when it is carried. The actor MUST hold the
admin role. `subject` MUST be an exchange with a surviving `0x0b` for the
channel, or the exchange the entry is posted at; it MUST NOT be the
current origin. Anything else is refused with SIP-21 code `not_a_replica`
(67).

```
Rehome | type: u8 = 0x19 | channel[32] | subject[32] | dom_len: u8 | domain | action |
```

`POST /channel/rehome` carries the action and the domain hint.

The entry is signed under the **outgoing** origin's key, as SIP-31 binds
every entry to the place it was made: it is the last entry of the old
regime. Every entry after it is signed and receipted under the new.

### Posted at the origin: a planned move

The origin accepts a rehome from an admin as it accepts a `0x0b`, orders
it, receipts it, and from that position **closes the channel to writes**:
every later write is refused with SIP-35's `replicated`, naming the new
origin, and `/channel/home` answers the new origin. It goes on serving
what it holds, and its replicas pull the rehome entry as they pull
anything.

The named replica, on pulling the entry, becomes the origin: it stops
pulling, orders what comes next after the rehome's position, receipts it
under its own key with SIP-34's `head` continuing from the rehome entry's,
and answers `/channel/home` with itself. It accepts SIP-43 forwards from
the channel's other replicas, which it now serves as an origin serves
them, under the `0x0b` entries the log already carries.

### Posted at a replica: the origin is gone

A replica accepts a rehome from an admin **only when all of these hold**:

- `subject` is the replica itself. A replica cannot make another exchange
  the origin; the admin posts there instead.
- The origin has been unreachable from the replica for at least
  `ORIGIN_AWAY_SECS`: every pull and forward in that time failed to
  connect. A replica that reached the origin more recently refuses with
  code `origin_reachable` (66), and the admin's client tells them the
  origin is not gone, only slow.
- The replica can derive the channel's roster (SIP-35) and finds the actor
  an admin in it.
- No rehome was accepted for this channel within `REHOME_MIN_SECS`.

It then orders the rehome entry at the position after the last it holds,
receipts it under its own key with `head` continuing from what it holds,
and is the origin from there, exactly as above. A replica tracked no
SIP-31 chains and no message counters -- the origin did -- and rebuilds
both from the entries it holds before it orders anything: every entry's
hash is the link its device's next step chains from, and the highest
`msg_seq` per device and epoch is what a nonce must not repeat.

### What readers verify

A channel has an **origin history**: the exchange that created it, then
each `subject` of each rehome, from the position after that rehome's.
An entry at position `seq` verifies (SIP-31) under the origin that held
the channel at `seq`, and its receipt (SIP-34) under the origin that
ordered it -- which for the rehome entry itself is the *new* origin. A
client holds the history beside the channel and consults it as it
consults a pin's predecessors (SIP-40): a signature that verifies under
no origin in the history is forged, whichever exchange served it.

A client learns the history from the log -- the rehome entry is in it --
and from `/channel/home`, which carries it: SIP-43's `Home` gains, after
the domain, `count: u8 | count × (ended: u64 | origin[32])`, the exchanges
that ordered the channel before the current one, oldest first, each with
the position of the rehome that ended its regime. Absent from an exchange
before this document. A client reading a channel for the first time from
its current origin needs it, since the entries from before the move come
before the entry that explains them. A client MUST verify each rehome's
actor against the roster as derived at that position.

### Telling the others

**Other replicas** of the channel pulled from the old origin. On pulling
a rehome, a replica switches its pull to `subject`; where `subject` is
reached it learns from the old origin's `/peer/standing`, which gains,
after SIP-43's 48 bytes, `subject[32] | dom_len: u8 | domain` once the
channel has moved; it discovers the domain by SIP-33, checks the key
against `subject`, and pulls from the position it holds. Where the old
origin is gone and cannot be asked, a replica is told by a client:

```
Rehomed | type: u8 = 0x1a | channel[32] | dom_len: u8 | domain | entry (receipted) |
```

`POST /channel/rehomed` carries the rehome entry with the new origin's
receipt and the domain hint. Any identity that may read the channel may
present it. The replica verifies the actor was an admin in the roster it
derives, the receipt under `subject`, and `head` chaining from the entry
before it where held; then it switches, and pulls from `subject` from the
position it holds. What it cannot verify it refuses, and stays where it
was. (A replica cannot check the actor's own signature: a system entry's
`arg` is not transmitted, and SIP-35 has replicas take system entries on
the receipt. The receipt is the new origin's word that it verified the
action; the roster check is the replica's own.)

**The old origin**, if it returns, is told the same way -- by a client, or
by the new origin over the peering link as `/peer/rehomed` with the same
body, gated as `/peer/pull` is, which the new origin sends when the old
one answers its next pull. It verifies as a replica does, against its own
log. Where its log ends at the rehome's position it becomes a replica of
the new origin, pulling from it under the `0x0b` the log carries for it
if there is one, and closed to writes otherwise. Where its log goes
**past** that position -- entries it ordered during a partition, which no
replica saw -- those entries are **stranded**: the old origin MUST stop
serving them as the conversation, SHOULD keep them for `STRANDED_SECS`
and serve them to their own authors:

```
Stranded | count: u16 | count × (seq: u64 | posted: u64 | len: u32 | body) |
```

`POST /channel/stranded`, a `ByChannel` of type `0x1b`, answers the
caller's own. A client that finds its entries stranded offers to post
them again (§Posting again). Nothing else can be done with a fork: two orderings of
the same positions, and the members chose one.

A replica keeps the `0x0b` and `0x0c` entries it pulls as authorisations
of its own, so that having become the origin it serves the channel's
other replicas under them.

### Two rehomes

Two admins at two replicas during one partition may each rehome the
channel to where they stand. Each replica accepted a rehome naming itself
and knows nothing of the other. When either learns of the other -- a
client carrying the other's entry, or the two exchanges meeting over a
peering link -- **the rehome with the earlier receipted `posted` wins**,
and on a tie the lower `subject`; the loser becomes a replica of the winner and
everything it ordered after the fork is stranded, as above. A client that
holds both MUST apply the same rule, so every party settles on the same
history without a third.

### What a client does with a fork

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-74.* A client holds a channel's origin history beside it (§What readers
verify). It learns a fork when the history gains a former
origin whose regime `ended` at a position **below the highest entry the
client holds** -- from a `Home` answer, from a rehome entry it reads, or
by rehoming the channel itself at a replica (below). Position `ended`
is the fork; what the client holds above it was ordered by the former
origin, and the client checks that: an entry above the fork whose
receipt verifies under the former origin and not under the origin that
took over is of the losing regime.

A client SHOULD ask `/channel/home` again whenever an entry's receipt
verifies under no origin it knows: a receipt from a new origin is how a
client with its cursor above the fork first meets it. A client that
carries a rehome to an exchange (`/channel/rehomed`) asks that
exchange again at once: it has just changed what the exchange answers.

A client that reads a channel through more than one exchange holds a
view per connection (SIP-43). The losing side is held in the view that
read it -- the connection to the origin that lost -- and is found there,
when that connection next learns the fork; a stranded post, once found,
is a fact about this device's words and not about a connection, and is
offered and sent from whichever connection can post.

**The losing regime.** Of the entries above the fork that are of the losing regime:

- the client's **own** posts (SIP-19 `Post` bodies signed by this
  device) are kept aside as **stranded**, with the time each was first
  posted and its body as this client wrote it;
- everything else -- other members' entries, and the client's own
  reactions, edits and redactions, whose targets were positions in a
  history that no longer exists -- is dropped from what the client
  shows and holds as the conversation.

They MUST NOT be shown as the conversation, and MUST NOT be kept under
their sequence numbers, which the winning history now uses. A client
that holds none of its own above the fork -- a fresh store -- asks
`/channel/stranded` at the former origin where that is the exchange it
is at, and files what comes back the same way.

The client's fetch cursor goes back to the fork, and the winning history
is read from there as §What readers verify has a client read across a
move: the
former origin's key verifies what came before the fork, the new one what
comes after.

**The chain.** After the fork, the client's SIP-31 chain for the channel continues
from its **last entry the winning origin holds**: the position and head
the origin reports in `Channel` (`my_chain_seq`, `my_chain_head`) are
taken *in place of* the client's own where the client's own is higher
and every entry between is one it filed as stranded. This is the one
case in which a client takes a lower position from an exchange than it
remembers, and it is permitted because the client has verified for
itself that the entries between are of a regime that ended: an exchange
that under-reported here would have to show the client receipts under
the former origin for entries it never made.

A replica that has not yet become the origin reports no chain (a
replica tracked none, and rebuilds from its entries before it orders)
-- or, since SIP-43 §A copy reports what it holds, the chain as its
entries show it, which a client
takes as the origin's report.
The client then rebuilds its own the same way, from the entries it
holds up to the fork: a post of its own links over the entry's terms,
which it can recompute; an **action** of its own -- a system entry
naming its device, whose signing input the exchange never transmits --
links over a head only the device that signed it knows. A client MUST
therefore keep every chain head it computes, by position, for as long
as it keeps the channel; a client that kept only the latest cannot step
back over an action it signed.

A client rehoming a channel at a replica whose origin is gone (§Posted
at a replica: the origin is gone) strands, by that act, everything above the
replica's last position -- including its own. It MUST file those and
rewind its chain as above **before** it signs the rehome, or the rehome
entry itself links to a hash the replica has never seen.

### Posting again

Each stranded post is **offered to the person**, in the order it was
first posted, and sent again only on their say: it is a new entry, and
the person may have said it since, or no longer mean it. A post sent
again is posted as any post is (SIP-16; SIP-43 through a copy), with
one part added:

```
Part | kind: u8 = 0x07 | len: u32 = 8 | said: u64 |
```

`Said` (at most one per post) is when the poster first posted this,
the `posted` of the stranded entry, as the poster's own word about the
poster's own act, signed and sealed with the rest of the body. A
reader that understands it shows the message **at `said`** and marks
it as posted again; a reader that does not skips the part (SIP-19) and
shows the message at its `posted`, which is when it landed the second
time. A `Said` later than the entry's own `posted` is ignored: nothing
was first said after it was posted. The entry's `posted` remains what
orders the log; `Said` is how it reads.

Stranded posts that were replies (SIP-19 `Reply`) keep their `Reply`
part where its target is below the fork, and lose it otherwise: the
message it answered was stranded too. Attachments are kept: a blob is
referred to by its id and is not stranded with the entry (SIP-18),
though a client SHOULD check it is still held before offering.

### Limits

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| `ORIGIN_AWAY_SECS` | 300 |
| `REHOME_MIN_SECS` | 3600 |
| `STRANDED_SECS` | 7 days |
| Rehomes in a channel's history a client keeps | 16 |
| `Said` per post | 1 |
| Stranded posts kept per channel | 256, the oldest dropped past that |

## Rationale

**Why an admin, and not the replica's operator.** The operator holds a
copy because an admin let them (`0x0b`). Letting them also *order* the
channel is more, and it is the admin's to give: an operator who could
promote their own copy could take any channel they replicate the moment
its origin blinked.

**Why the replica must be the subject.** A replica that accepted a rehome
naming a third exchange would be asserting something about that exchange
it cannot know -- whether it holds the channel, whether it will order it.
The admin goes to the exchange they mean and says so there.

**Why an away timer, and not a liveness protocol.** The replica already
knows whether it can reach the origin: it pulls on an interval and
forwards on demand, and both fail or do not. Five minutes of nothing is
what "away" already means to a member refused `origin_away`; the timer
turns the refusal the member has been getting into permission to stop
getting it, and refuses the admin who is merely impatient.

**Why signed under the outgoing origin.** Because that is where the
actor's client believes it is posting, and SIP-31's place-binding is what
stops an entry lifting between exchanges. The rehome is the last entry
made at the old place and the first receipted at the new, and the seam is
exactly one entry wide.

**Why stranded entries are kept and not merged.** A partition produces two
orderings of the same positions. Merging would renumber one side, and a
receipt is a signature over a position: renumbered entries are receipted
entries with the wrong receipts. Their authors can post them again at the
new positions, with new receipts; nothing else is honest.

**Why the earlier `posted` wins.** It needs no third party and every
holder computes it alike. A clock is forgeable, so the rule is not
fairness, only agreement; and the exchange that forges an earlier time
strands the other side, which is the same thing the other could have done
to it. A channel with two admins on two sides of a partition is a channel
whose admins should talk.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-74:*

**Why the client decides what was stranded, and not the exchange.** The
former origin keeps stranded entries for their authors (§Telling the
others), but the
author's client already holds them, in the clear, and holds the
receipts that prove which regime ordered them. Asking the exchange is
the fallback for a client that lost its store; the rule is written so
that a client with one needs no round trip to know.

**Why others' entries are dropped and not kept as history.** They are
not history: they are one side of a fork, and their authors are the
ones who can put them back. Keeping them under their numbers would show
two messages at one position; keeping them aside would be keeping
somebody else's stranded entries, which is the former origin's job and
is bounded there. SIP-60 §The client keeps what it read's earlier copies are different -- a whole
incarnation that ended -- and are kept because nobody can post them
again.

**Why a lower chain position is taken here and nowhere else.** SIP-31
forbids it because an exchange that reported a lower position could
make a client sign twice at one position, and the fork that follows
reads as the client's misconduct. Here the client signs at positions
the winning origin has never seen used, and has checked that the
entries it is stepping back over were ordered by an origin that lost
them. The evidence is the client's own; the exchange's report is only
taken once it is consistent with that evidence.

**Why `Said` and not a re-dated entry.** `posted` is the exchange's
stamp on the position it ordered, and SIP-34 receipts it. A client
cannot set it, and should not: the log's order is the exchange's. What
the client can say is when it said the thing, which is its own fact,
carried the way SIP-43 carries `Via` -- signed by the poster, shown by
the reader as the poster's claim.

**Why offered and not sent.** Time has passed. The person may have
answered again on the winning side, and a message sent twice reads as
a stammer; or the moment has gone. The client knows what was stranded;
only the person knows whether it still wants saying.

**What was rejected.** Re-posting with the original `posted` in place
of a fresh one: a receipt over a position carries the time the origin
ordered it, and a client-chosen time there is a forged receipt. Having
the new origin merge the stranded side: rejected above, since
renumbered entries are receipted entries with the wrong receipts.

## Compatibility

Additive: one event, four routes, two refusals, and two answers that grow
at the end (`Home`, `Standing`) where an older reader stops. A client from before this
document sees event `0x0e` as an unknown system entry and, from the next
entry on, signatures under a key it does not expect -- it will show the
channel as forged past that point. That is the correct reading for a
client that does not know origins move, and the reason this document
exists; such a client upgrades. An exchange from before this document
refuses the rehome as an unknown event and the channel stays where it
was.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-74.* Additive: one SIP-19 part kind, which readers from before §Posting
again (0.92.0) skip; and client rules. Nothing changes on any exchange.
A client from before it goes on showing its stranded entries as the
conversation, as it did.

SIP-31's client-side rule -- never take a lower chain position than
remembered -- is amended by the one case above.

## Security considerations

**An admin can move a channel anywhere it is already copied.** This is
the `0x0b` trust restated: the admin chose that exchange as a holder; now
they choose it as the orderer. Members who did not want either can leave,
and the log shows them who did what.

**A partition is a fork, and a fork loses something.** Stated above. The
protocol picks a side by a rule everyone can compute, keeps what was lost
where its authors can find it, and does not pretend the lost side was
never written.

**The away timer is the replica's word.** A replica lying about its
reachability lets an admin rehome to it early -- while the origin is
merely slow. The admin can equally wait; the harm is a planned move made
hastily, and the actor is still an admin who could have made it at the
origin. A replica cannot use the timer to take the channel: it still
needs an admin's signature.

**`domain` in `arg` is a hint.** As everywhere: the key beside it is what
the hint must resolve to, and a hint that resolves to another key is
discarded.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-74.* **`Said` is the poster's claim.** A poster can post anything with any
`Said`; a reader shows it marked as posted again, under the poster's
name, ordered where the exchange put it. It changes how a message
reads and not where it sits, and it is signed by the same key as the
words.

**The chain rewind cannot be induced.** A client steps back only over
entries it holds with receipts under an origin that its origin history
says ended -- an exchange that wants a client to step back has to show
it a fork that happened, and a fork that happened is exactly when
stepping back is right.

**Stranded bodies are the poster's own.** They were sealed to the
channel's members when first posted and are sealed again when posted
again, under the epoch in force then, which may have moved on; a
member removed in between does not receive the second sending.

## Reference implementation

`sqex` 0.70.0; 0.92.0 (a client and a fork). `sqexd`: the rehome at
origin and replica, the away timer
(`rehome_away_secs`, default 300), `/channel/rehomed`, `/peer/rehomed`
(sent by the new origin when the old one answers a pull again),
`/channel/stranded`, the tie-break, `Home.former`, `Standing.moved`, and
a task that pulls channels whose origin was learned from a rehome. Tests:
a planned move closes the origin, the named replica orders from there,
and a third exchange follows it by the domain; a replica takes a channel
whose origin is gone (and refuses while it is merely slow), and the
origin come back is told, strands what only it ordered, and follows.
`sqex-chat`: former origins kept beside a channel's home and verified
under; a client that read before the move reads on across it, and a
fresh one reads the whole log from `Home.former`; `/rehome <key>
[domain]`. Not yet, then: sigil's transcript line for the move, which is still
sigil's.

**A client and a fork** (§What a client does with a fork, §Posting again,
sqex 0.92.0), in `sqex-proto` (`message::Part::Said`, `PART_SAID`,
`Post::said`, one per post) and `sqex-chat`. `store`: `stranded`
(unscoped on read, as `dm_alias`), `chain_log` (every head, by
position) with `head_after`, `reset_chain`, `truncate_above`,
`entries_anywhere`. `client`: `fork_check` / `fork_found` in the
section's order -- losing side by receipt, own posts kept, the rest
dropped, cursor back, chain back -- called from a `Home` answer's
`former` (the fork being the position before `ended`), from a rehome
entry read (after the batch), from a repudiated receipt (the home asked
again), and from `rehome` at a replica before it signs; `chain_from_held`
rebuilding the chain from own posts and own actions' logged heads where
the origin reports none; `stranded_posts`, `post_again`, `forget_stranded`.
The TUI shows how many posts are stranded, `/repost` and `/unstrand`,
and a message with `Said` at that time, marked *posted again*.

Building it found three things the first draft had not said, all now in
the specification: that a replica-turned-origin reports no chain and the
client rebuilds from what it holds; that an action's link cannot be
rebuilt from the entry the exchange serves, so every head a device
computes is kept; and that a client reading through two exchanges finds
the losing side in the view that read it. Two things beside them were
wrong before it: a client carrying a rehome looked for the
entry only in the view it was connected through, which never held it
(the entry was read at the new origin); and a client with entries of its
own above a fork could not post again at all -- its chain linked to a
hash the winner had never seen, and the rule against taking a lower
position from an exchange, right everywhere else, stopped it mending.

Tests: an origin with a replica, partitioned by coming back where the
replica cannot find it, orders a post the replica never sees; the client
rehomes at the replica -- chaining on from what the replica holds, which
the first draft could not -- and posts on; when the partition heals and
the client carries the rehome, its view through the old origin keeps the
post aside as stranded, shows nothing of the losing side, and the post
is sent again from the winning side, read back saying when it was first
said, with nothing forged anywhere. Checked by never keeping a post
aside and watching the client hold nothing to send.

## Abstract

One new part a SIP-19 post may carry: an account and a serial, naming a post in
somebody else's feed. A quote is a post with words of one's own and that part; a
re-post is the same post without the words. The part carries **no copy of what it
names**, so withdrawing the original withdraws it everywhere it was quoted, and
it names nothing but a key and a number, so a reader verifies the original under
a key it already holds and never has to trust the exchange that served it.

## Motivation

SIP-88 gives an account a feed. Nothing yet lets one person carry another's post
into their own.

The two existing ways of pointing at a message cannot do it. `Part::Reply` and
`Body::Reaction` both carry a `target: u64`, which SIP-19 defines as a SIP-16
sequence number — channel-local by construction, and SIP-19 explains why it is a
sequence number rather than anything a sender chose: an identifier chosen by a
sender "must be qualified by that sender, or one member can mint an identifier
that collides with another's message and quietly redirect replies to it."
Neither can name an entry in a log the reader is not already reading.

SIP-19 anticipated this and said where it goes:

> **What is deliberately not here.** Threads, pinned messages, polls, formatted
> text, forwarding provenance, disappearing messages, per-message expiry. Each
> is a plausible addition and none is needed to hold a conversation. The type
> space is where they go, and the ignore rule is what makes adding them cheap.

This is forwarding provenance, taking the invitation. It follows SIP-43's `Via`
and SIP-53's `Said` exactly: a single-instance part registered in SIP-19's table
by a document of its own, carrying the poster's own word about the poster's own
act, signed and sealed with the rest of the body, which no exchange adds and no
replica can.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian.

### The part

```
Part | kind: u8 = 0x08 | len: u32 | account[32] | serial: u64 |
```

Forty-one bytes of body, naming a post in the feed of `account` at `serial`.
SIP-19's table gains:

| `kind` | Meaning | Body | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0x08 | Quote | `account[32] \| serial: u64` | at most one |

At most one, as `Reply`, `Via` and `Said` are, and for the same reason: a second
changes what the post is *about*, and a post about two things is two posts. It
counts against SIP-19's `MAX_PARTS` like any part. A `Quote` and a `Reply` MAY
both appear — quoting somebody while replying in a channel is coherent, and
their targets are in different spaces, which §Two spaces, one word warns about.

**Nothing but a key and a number.** No author name, no timestamp, no snippet, no
exchange, no domain, no hash. Each was considered and each is rejected in
Rationale.

### What it is not

A re-post is **not a body type.** SIP-19's rules differ in a way that decides
this: an unknown `Body` type MUST be ignored, and an unknown `Part` kind MUST be
skipped using `len` with the rest of the post displayed. A body-typed re-post
would therefore be a **silent hole** in an older client's timeline — and the
posts people re-post are the ones most worth not losing. As a part, an older
client shows a quote's words and skips what it cannot read, and shows a bare
re-post through the rule SIP-19 already has for a post with no parts it
understood: "a client SHOULD say that the message contained something it could
not display".

SIP-19's own test agrees: "A body is all-or-nothing — an unrecognised kind of
message has nothing to show. A part is one element of a message that otherwise
displays fine." A quote is exactly a message that otherwise displays fine, and a
re-post does not become a different kind of message by having less in it.

### Resolving one

A reader holding a `Quote` resolves it in this order, and MUST NOT skip a step.

1. **Discard it** if `account` is the account of the post carrying it and
   `serial` is at or above that post's own serial, which is a post citing itself
   or its own future; or if following it would exceed `QUOTE_DEPTH`.
2. **Find the feed.** Ask `/account/home` (SIP-59) for `account`, which is
   answered to any identified caller. The reader's own exchange MAY proxy, and
   SIP-88 §Where a feed lives governs.
3. **Read the post** with SIP-88's `/feed/read` at `since = serial - 1`,
   `limit = 1`, `dir` forward.
4. **Verify it.** The signature under `device`, the SIP-20 credential binding
   `device` to `account`, and — where the post's `account` is not the feed's
   owner — the seam chain of SIP-88 §Succession.
5. **Display it** in one of the states below, and MUST NOT collapse them.

**The reader never trusts the exchange that served it.** The account key is both
the locator and the verifying key, so a post that verifies is the post that
account signed, whatever path it arrived by. An exchange in the middle can
refuse, delay, or serve nothing; it cannot substitute. That is why this part
carries no exchange identity and no hash: there is nothing for them to protect
against.

### When it cannot be resolved

| what happened | what a reader MUST do | what it MUST NOT do |
|---|---|---|
| the home cannot be reached | show the quoting post, with the quote marked **unresolved**; SHOULD retry with backoff; SHOULD go on showing a copy it already resolved, marked stale | drop the post, or call the quote false |
| `/account/home` answers nothing | show it as **a feed that could not be found** | guess an exchange |
| the post is withdrawn, corroborated | show it as **withdrawn by its author** | keep showing a body it cached — this is what the no-copy rule exists for |
| the post is a tombstone with nothing behind it | show it as **removed by the exchange**, distinguishably, which SIP-32 requires | present it as an ordinary deletion |
| `serial` is below `oldest` | show it as **no longer held** | show it as deleted by anyone, or as never having existed |
| `serial` is above `newest` | show it as **unresolved** and retry later; a feed may simply not have reached it yet | treat it as a fault |
| the signature or credential fails | show it as **forged** | show the body |
| the signing device was revoked since | show it as **unverifiable**, distinctly from forged | report forged — see Security considerations |
| the account has succeeded | resolve at the successor (SIP-88 `state: 0x06`); verify the seam chain | treat the old posts as forged |
| the author has blocked the reader | the feed answers `found: 0`, so: **a feed that could not be found** | infer a block, which SIP-21 forbids stating |
| `QUOTE_DEPTH` reached | render the innermost quote as a labelled, unresolved reference | follow further unasked |

The tone is SIP-19's, which already settled it for a reply whose target is
missing: it "SHOULD still be shown, marked as replying to something
unavailable." **Every row above names its own unavailability, and none of them
is silence.**

### Nothing is copied

**A client MUST NOT copy an `Attachment` part, a preview, a link preview, or
text out of a quoted post into the post that quotes it.**

A SIP-18 `Attachment` carries `key[32]`. Copying one into a feed publishes the
blob's key to everybody who can read that feed, which is what SIP-18 refuses when
it says an `Attach` naming a public channel MUST be refused for a blob any
private channel holds — except performed inside a body no exchange parses, where
no exchange can refuse it. The remedy is SIP-18's own: upload it again, which
"gives the public copy its own key and leaves the private channel's copy alone".

A reader sees the quoted post's pictures by resolving the quote and fetching the
original's own attachment references, under SIP-18's existing authority. A client
SHOULD fetch them only on the person's act, and SHOULD render a quote from its
text alone until then: fetching identifies the reader to an exchange they have no
account at, and one re-post in a widely read feed would otherwise tell that
exchange its whole audience.

**A client MUST NOT compose its own preview of a quoted post.** SIP-19's
link-preview rule transfers exactly — a sender-composed preview is a
sender-chosen claim about something the reader can check for itself, and the
reader must see the real thing or nothing.

### Editing

**An `Edit` MUST be ignored if it adds, removes or changes a `Quote`.**

SIP-19 lets an edit replace every part of a post within `EDIT_WINDOW`. Allowing
it to replace this one would allow quoting somebody agreeable, collecting
whatever a quote collects, and then repointing at somebody else. The words may be
corrected; what the post is about may not.

An `Edit` that changes only other parts is applied as SIP-19 already says.

### Recursion

A quote may name a post that itself carries a quote. It is bounded in three
places.

**On composition, a bare re-post is transparent.** A client composing a quote of
a post whose only understood part is a `Quote` MUST name that post's own target
instead. A pointer to a pointer is the pointer, and the composing client has
already resolved and verified the inner post — it is naming what it actually
read. A **quote with words is not transparent**: there is something of the
quoter's own there, so a quote of it names it.

**On resolution, `QUOTE_DEPTH` is 2.** Each hop is a request to a possibly
different exchange and a disclosure of the reader to it, so depth is an
amplification primitive: one post in a well-read feed becomes readers × hops
requests. A client MUST NOT follow more than `QUOTE_DEPTH` hops from a post it
displays, and MAY follow further **only on the person's own act**, which is not
amplification.

**Cycles** are caught by step 1 of §Resolving one and, failing that, by the
depth bound.

A client SHOULD collapse a chain to the original and the fact that it travelled,
rather than nesting it.

### Two spaces, one word

SIP-19's `Edit`, `Redact`, `Reply` and `Reaction` all carry `target: u64`. In a
channel it is a SIP-16 sequence number; in a feed it is a SIP-88 serial. The two
are different spaces and the word is the same.

A client that renders channels and feeds through one path will resolve one
against the other, silently and plausibly, because both are small integers that
usually exist. An implementation MUST keep them distinct in its types and SHOULD
have a test that fails when a feed serial resolves against a channel.

### Counting

**A client MUST NOT display a count of how many times a post has been quoted.**

No party can enumerate the feeds holding a quote of a post. The author's exchange
never sees them; a reader sees only the feeds it follows; and there is no search.
Any number a client could produce is a count of the feeds it happens to read,
and rendering a sample as a total is the kind of claim SIP-16 forbids elsewhere
when it requires a client with a gap to show the gap rather than present what
remains as the whole.

A client MAY show quotes it has actually seen, as what they are: the ones it
found.

### Limits

| limit | value |
|---|---|
| `Quote` per post | 1 |
| `Quote` body bytes | 40 |
| `QUOTE_DEPTH` | 2 |
| Quotes resolved per displayed post, unasked | 1 |
| `quotes` (SIP-56, per account) | 240 per hour |

## Rationale

**Why a pointer and not a copy.** A copy would resolve offline, survive the
author's exchange going away, and verify with nothing fetched — real advantages,
and SIP-42 already trades signed entries between devices in exactly that shape.
It is refused because of what SIP-88 §Withdrawal cannot then do: a copy under the
quoter's signature, in a log the author has no authority over, can never be
withdrawn. SIP-19 is already honest that "redaction is a request, not erasure"
and that an interface presenting it as deletion "is telling its user something
false". A copy would make that worse by design rather than by circumstance. **An
author's withdrawal must reach the quotes, and only a pointer lets it.**

**Why no author key in the part.** It would be redundant — the post the reader
fetches carries its own `account` — and harmful before it is redundant. A quote
naming an author is a claim a reader renders before it can check anything, so a
citer could name a feed that will never resolve and have every client display
"a post by K, unavailable". That is SIP-19's own objection to a display name
inside a `Mention`: a sender-chosen string where a reader looks for identity. The
reader learns the author by fetching, and where it cannot fetch it names nobody.

**Why no timestamp.** `issued_at` is the author's clock and `received` is
unsigned, so a quoted time would be a claim about a claim. The resolved post
carries both and SIP-88 §What the clocks are governs them.

**Why no hash of what is named.** A hash would let a reader detect that the feed
served something other than what the quoter saw. It is omitted because the
signature already does that work: a feed post is signed by its author over its
own serial, so there is no "something else" at that serial that verifies. A hash
would protect against an exchange substituting a post, which it cannot do, at the
cost of thirty-two bytes and a second thing that can disagree with the first.

**Why no exchange or domain.** Both would be hints, not facts — a feed moves with
its account (SIP-88) and `/account/home` is the answer that stays correct.
Carrying the exchange the quoter happened to read it at would be stale the moment
the author moved home, and a reader that preferred it would read a feed that had
stopped.

**Why quoting a channel entry is not here.** It was specified and withdrawn.
Naming a channel entry needs the channel identifier, and a direct message's
identifier is a hash over its two accounts — so a quote of one lets anybody
holding a candidate pair confirm offline that those two people talk, without
asking any exchange. It also needs SIP-31's `instance`, which SIP-31 requires an
exchange to withhold from a non-member "because a distinguishable value would
make the field an existence oracle for private channels". A part that is safe
only when a client gets two checks right is a part that will be used wrongly.
Somebody who wants to carry a room's post into their feed can say it themselves,
which is what quoting a stranger's words has always meant.

**What was considered and rejected.**

- *A re-post as a body type.* Covered in §What it is not. It degrades to silence
  where a part degrades to a labelled gap.
- *Carrying a snapshot alongside the pointer, marked as the quoter's copy.* It
  reads well and it is the copy problem with a label on it: the snapshot is still
  unwithdrawable, and the marking is the entire mitigation.
- *Exchange-held quote counts.* A route at the author's home recording who quoted
  what, served as a number. It is buildable. It is an index of the opinions of
  non-members, at an exchange none of them chose, attributable, unsigned — so the
  exchange could inflate it and nobody could audit an absence — and it would need
  its own deletion story. Recorded here because the next reader will propose it.
- *Notifying a quoted author.* There is no inbound path to a feed by design
  (SIP-88 §Nothing comes in), and adding one here would be adding it everywhere.
  A quote is visible to its subject only if somebody tells them, which is the
  same as the rest of publishing.

## Compatibility

**Additive, and nothing moves together.** One row in SIP-19's part table, two in
its limits, one in SIP-56's. No existing message changes.

A client from before this document skips the part by `len` and shows the rest of
the post, which is SIP-19's rule and is tested there. A quote therefore reaches an
older client as the quoter's own words with nothing attached, and a bare re-post
reaches it as a post with nothing it understood.

**The honest cost:** those words may read as an original opinion, or as an
attack on nothing. No wire format fixes it — a body type would hide the words
instead, which is worse — so it is stated rather than designed around.

An exchange needs no change at all. This document has no server side, as SIP-19
has none, and for the same reason.

## Security considerations

**A quote is an unverified claim until it resolves, and a client must not render
it as anything else.** Forty-one bytes cost nothing to fabricate, and a fabricated
quote names a feed that will not resolve. Everything a reader is shown before
resolution is the quoter's assertion; the design keeps that surface as small as
it can be — a key and a number, with no name, no time and no words of the
author's — so that there is nothing to put in front of a reader that looks like
the quoted person speaking.

**Resolving a quote discloses the reader to an exchange they have no
relationship with.** The author's home learns that this identity read this post,
and a widely quoted post tells it the audience of whoever quoted it. A client
SHOULD resolve on display and not in advance, SHOULD NOT resolve for posts it is
not showing, and SHOULD let a person turn resolution off, in which case a quote
stays a labelled reference.

**A quote outlives the relationship that made it.** Blocking (SIP-21) does not
unmake one: a blocked reader may still hold the post, and a block makes the feed
answer `found: 0`, which this document renders as "could not be found" precisely
so that the block is not announced. SIP-21's limit applies — the block "is not
undetectable, and MUST NOT be described as though it were."

**Withdrawal reaches the quotes and nothing else.** When the original is
withdrawn, every client that resolves the quote afterwards shows it as withdrawn,
which is the property the no-copy rule buys. It does not reach a reader who
already read it, a cached copy, or a screenshot. **This document makes a feed's
withdrawal work across quotes; it does not make withdrawal work.**

**A quote of a quote is an amplifier.** Without `QUOTE_DEPTH` one post in one
well-read feed becomes a request to every exchange in a chain, from every reader,
on every display. The bound is two and the unasked budget is one, and an
implementation that resolves eagerly or recursively will find that out from
somebody else's exchange.

**An author cannot refuse to be quoted**, and nothing here lets them. The post was
published; a key and a number are public facts about it. What they can do is
withdraw it, which this document makes effective against every quote that resolves
afterwards — and that is the whole of the control they have.

## Reference implementation

None yet.

Where it would attach. `sqex-proto`'s `message` module gains the part in its
`Part` enum, its encoder, its decoder and its `validate`, beside `Via` and
`Said`, with the at-most-one rule tested the way theirs are; the resolution
states belong beside `timeline`'s existing `Verdict` and `Deletion`, which
already carry this shape of three-way answer. `sqex-chat` gains the resolver —
`/account/home`, then SIP-88's `/feed/read`, then verification — and the cache
that stops it asking twice for one display. No exchange code at all.

What an implementation must settle, rather than this document arguing it.

- **Whether the two sequence spaces can be kept apart in one codebase.**
  §Two spaces, one word is the hazard and `sqex-proto`'s `timeline` folds both.
  Build the test that fails when a feed serial resolves against a channel before
  building the feature, because the failure is silent and plausible.
- **What a quote costs to display.** One resolution is a home lookup, a read and
  two signature checks, against another exchange. Measure it on a timeline of a
  hundred posts where a third are quotes, and find out whether the unasked budget
  of one is right or whether a client must resolve lazily on scroll.
- **Whether "unresolved" is legible.** Half of §When it cannot be resolved is
  states a person has never seen in a messenger. Put them in front of somebody
  and find out which ones read as an error in the app rather than as a fact about
  the post.
- **Whether transparent re-posting is what people expect.** §Recursion collapses
  a re-post of a re-post to the original. It is right for the wire and it means a
  client cannot show who re-posted it to you, which is the thing some readers
  most want to know. If that turns out to matter, the answer is a client
  rendering what it already knows from its own feeds, not a chain on the wire.

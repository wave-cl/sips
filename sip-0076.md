## Abstract

SIP-59 has a home pull an account's channels from the origins it has
been told about, and SIP-60 has an origin tell the home when it puts one
of the home's accounts in a channel. The telling is best effort, from
the origin to the home, and an origin that cannot reach the home has
told nobody: the member's client waits, fails, and the copy never comes.
This document adds the member's own word: a device tells its home which
origin to pull from -- `POST /account/hint` -- and a home that carries a
create to an origin on a member's behalf (SIP-60 `create_at`) records
that origin for the member itself, without waiting to be told what it
already knows.

## Motivation

Three live runs on an exchange nobody could reach inbound -- a laptop
behind its own address, taking part as a home -- ended the same way. A
member at the laptop opened a direct message that lived at
`trunk.exchange` (the lower key's home), and trunk, which could not open
a connection to the laptop, could not tell it; the member's client
waited ten seconds for a copy that was never coming and gave up. The
laptop had itself carried the create to trunk moments before. It knew
the origin. It had the member's own signed act naming it. It waited to
be told anyway.

The same happens without a laptop: an origin that is briefly unable to
reach a home, a home behind a firewall that admits only what it dials,
an origin from before SIP-60 that tells nobody. SIP-60 wrote *the
member finds the channel the way they always could* -- by connecting to
the origin -- and SIP-8's whitelist is exactly what stops that.

What is missing is the member's own statement. A home acts for an
account on the account's signature (SIP-59); the account's device is
connected to the home already, authenticated by SIP-3; and what the home
needs to know -- *pull from there* -- is a fact the device holds.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian. **Home**, **origin hint** and the
**home task** are SIP-59's; `create_at` and `/peer/invited` are
SIP-60's; a **device** and the account it registers under are SIP-22's.

### A device hints its home

```
Hint    | origin[32] | dom_len: u8 | domain |          POST /account/hint
Hinted  | now: u64 | pulling: u8 |
```

From any identified caller. The account is the caller's own: the device
itself where it is registered as nobody's, else the account its
registration here names (SIP-22) -- never one the caller asserts. Where
that account's Move names this exchange (SIP-59), the exchange records
`origin` among the account's hints, with `domain` where given, and wakes
its home task, which pulls from the origin at once; `pulling = 1`. Where
the account is not homed here, nothing is recorded and `pulling = 0`: a
home acts for an account on the account's signature, and this device
has not got one. A `domain` may be empty for an origin the exchange
already knows how to reach -- one on its lists, or one it holds a link
or a forwarder to.

A hint is idempotent and adds to the account's hints; it does not
replace them as a Move's `origins` do (SIP-59). An exchange MUST bound
the hints it keeps per account at `MAX_HINTS`, dropping the oldest.

### The home hints itself

An exchange that carries a `create_at` to an origin for an account whose
Move names it (SIP-60 §Where a direct message lives) MUST, on the
origin's `200`, record that origin among the account's hints as if the
account had hinted it, and wake its home task. It knew the origin before
the origin could tell it; SIP-60's telling remains, and is what informs
a home that carried nothing.

### The client

A client SHOULD hint its home:

- after a `create_at` it made, naming the origin it named there --
  belt beside the braces above, for an exchange from before this
  document;
- when it learns, from `/channel/home` (SIP-43), that a channel it is a
  member of lives at an exchange other than its own, once per origin;
- when a person tells it where a channel lives, by hand.

A client that opened a direct message elsewhere (SIP-60) and is waiting
for the copy SHOULD hint before it waits.

### Limits

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| `MAX_HINTS` per account | 32 |
| `domain` bytes | 255 |

## Rationale

**Why the device's word is enough.** The hint causes one thing: the
home asks an origin what channels the account is in there (`/peer/mine`)
and pulls what the origin serves for that account. The origin serves
only what the account may read; the home holds only what it was already
entitled to hold under the account's Move. A device that lies about an
origin makes its home ask an exchange that will answer with nothing.

**Why the home hints itself.** SIP-60 put the telling at the origin
because the origin is the party that writes the membership. But a
`create_at` is the home writing the membership *through* the origin,
and it holds the origin's key in the request. Waiting for the origin to
say what the home just did was the round trip the laptop could never
complete.

**Why not a Move.** A Move carries hints (SIP-59) and replaces them; it
is signed by the account key, which a linked device does not hold; and
signing a new Move to say *also pull from there* is a heavy instrument
for a light fact.

**What was rejected.** Having the client pull the channel itself from
the origin: it cannot connect there, which is the case this document
exists for. Having the origin retry the telling: it should, and SIP-60
says nothing against it, but an origin that can never reach the home
retries forever.

## Compatibility

Additive: one client route and one rule on an existing one. An exchange
from before this document refuses `/account/hint` with `not_found`; a
client that sent it falls back to waiting, as it did. A home from before
this document does not hint itself, and is told by the origin as before.

## Security considerations

**Authority.** A hint names an origin and nothing else, for the caller's
own account, as SIP-22 attributes the caller. It grants the home no
entitlement it did not have.

**Disclosure.** The home learns that its account may have channels at
`origin`, which the account's own client is telling it. The origin
learns, from the pull, that the home acts for the account, which the
Move the home carries already says (SIP-59).

**Denial of service.** Bounded per account by `MAX_HINTS`; each hint
costs the home one peering request per home-task cycle, which SIP-56's
peering limits already bound at the origin.

## Abstract

A SIP-4 read of an **account** is answered from the freshest beat across
the account and every device presently registered to it, and gains one
byte saying whether any of those devices holds a live SIP-45 wake
registration. Before this, a person whose only client was a phone was
absent for ever -- their phone beat under its own device key, which nobody
reads -- and a person whose phone was asleep but reachable in seconds was
shown as gone. The exchange still reports facts and not verdicts: a
timestamp, an interval, and now a bit saying a wake would land.

## Motivation

SIP-4 records a beat against the identity that beat. SIP-22 made that
identity a *device*: a phone paired under SIP-47 connects as its device
key, beats as its device key, and its beats are recorded under its device
key. A consumer showing presence asks about the *account* -- that is what a
contact is, and what a direct message is addressed to -- and finds nothing
there unless the account's own key has beaten. A person with a desktop and
a phone is "there" while the desktop is up and "absent" the moment it
sleeps, however awake their phone is; a person with only a phone is absent
from the day they pair it.

The second half is SIP-45's doing. A phone that has registered a wake
endpoint and gone to sleep is not absent in any sense a caller cares
about: a ring reaches it in seconds, through the wake, and it answers. But
it is not beating, so the dot beside its owner's name says offline, and
the caller who believes the dot does not call. The exchange knows the
endpoint is there. It has no way to say so.

A consumer could reconstruct both from what is already public -- SIP-22's
`List` is answerable to anybody, so it could list a contact's devices and
read each -- at N+1 requests per contact per poll, and it would still not
know about the wake registration, which SIP-45 rightly keeps off every
route. The exchange holds all of it and can answer in one reply.

## Specification

### Reading an account

A SIP-4 `Read` naming a key that is an account with registered devices
(SIP-22) MUST be answered from the set consisting of the account's own
key and every device whose registration currently stands -- not revoked,
credential not expired. The reply's `last_seen`, `interval_secs` and
`flags` are those of the **freshest** beat in that set, and `found` is
`1` if any member of the set has beaten and is not withheld.

A `Read` naming a device key directly is answered as before, from that
key alone: a device is asked about as a device.

### Withholding

A beat with SIP-4's withhold bit from **any** member of the set withholds
the whole account from public readers: the account is one person, and the
one device they set to withhold on speaks for them. Members of the set
themselves are the account's own devices and see it, as SIP-4 lets an
identity see its own withheld beacon.

### Reachable

The reply gains one byte after `flags`:

```
| found: u8 | last_seen: u64 | interval_secs: u32 | now: u64 | flags: u8 | reach: u8 |
```

`reach` bit 0 is set when at least one member of the set holds a SIP-45
wake registration that has not expired, and that device did not ask to be
left out of this (below). Other bits are reserved and MUST be zero. The bit
is a fact -- an endpoint is held -- and not a verdict: whether a person who
is not beating but can be woken should be shown as *reachable* is the
consumer's decision, exactly as staleness is under SIP-4.

`reach` is answered whether or not `found` is set: an account that has
never beaten and holds a wake registration answers `found = 0` with
`reach = 1`, which is the phone-only person's ordinary state. A withheld
account answers `found = 0` and `reach = 0`: withholding withholds.

A consumer that shows presence SHOULD show *reachable* apart from
*active*, *away* and *absent* -- a person who can be woken is not there,
and is not gone.

### Leaving a device out

A device that holds a wake registration but would rather its account not
be reported reachable on its account says so when it registers. SIP-45's
`Register` gains one trailing byte:

```
Register | type: u8 = 0x01 | ttl: u32 | endpoint_len: u16 | endpoint | flags: u8 |
```

`flags` bit 0: **quiet** -- do not count this registration toward `reach`.
Other bits reserved, MUST be zero. The byte is optional: a `Register`
without it means no flags. An exchange from before this document refuses
the longer body as malformed; a device that is refused SHOULD register
again without the byte, and then knows the exchange does not report reach
in any case.

### Interpreting

SIP-4's reading rules stand: staleness is `now - last_seen` against
`interval_secs`, a single missed beat is not absence, and the exchange
computes no verdict. What this adds is that the beat a consumer is reading
may be any device's, so the interval is that device's declared interval and
not the account's -- there is no such thing.

## Rationale

**Why aggregate at the exchange.** Because it holds the registry, the
beats and the wake registrations, and can answer from all three in one
reply, and because a consumer that reconstructed it would still be blind
to the wake registration, which must stay off every route (SIP-45). The
device list is public already; nothing about which devices exist is being
hidden by keeping reads per device, and a reader who wanted per-device
presence still has it by reading a device key.

**Why a fact and not a verdict.** SIP-4's rule, kept: "reporting *up* or
*down* forces the exchange's choice on everyone". A wake registration is a
fact the exchange holds. What it means -- that a call is worth placing,
that a message will be seen soon -- is the consumer's to decide, and the
consumer can weigh it against `last_seen`.

**Why the freshest beat and not all of them.** A reply listing every
device's beat is a device inventory, and a caller does not need one: the
question is whether the person can be reached, and the freshest beat
answers it. Per-device reads remain for a reader who wants them.

**Why withhold is any-device.** The alternative -- the account withheld
only when every device withholds -- means one forgotten laptop makes the
person visible after they set their phone to withhold, and the person does
not find out. A setting that means "hide me" should hide them.

**Why the quiet flag lives on the wake registration.** Because that is
where the fact comes from, and the device that made it is the device to say
whether it may be reported. Putting it on the beat would leave a phone that
never beats -- the ordinary phone -- with no way to say it.

**Rejected: reporting the count of devices, or which one beat.** Both are
inventory. The registry is public but a presence reply that says "three
devices, the phone is the live one" is a different thing from a registry
somebody has to go and read, and nothing a caller needs.

## Compatibility

The reply grows by one byte. SIP-4 already states the rule for that: a
reply is a report, and a consumer reads what it does not know as absent
rather than refusing it, so a consumer from before this document reads a
new exchange's reply as SIP-4 and a new consumer reads an old exchange's
21- or 22-byte reply as `reach = 0`. `Register` grows by an optional byte,
with the refusal-and-retry path SIP-4 established for its own flags. Beats
are unchanged. Reads of device keys are unchanged.

An exchange that implements SIP-22 but not this document goes on answering
account reads from the account key alone, which is what it did.

## Security considerations

**What a stranger learns.** That an account has *some* device beating, and
that *some* device of it can be woken -- not which, not how many. Both are
less than SIP-22's public `List` already lets a stranger reconstruct for
the beats, and the reach bit is the one new fact: that the person carries
a device that sleeps and can be woken. That is a reasonable description of
nearly everybody, and a device that would rather not be described so
registers quiet. A withheld account discloses neither.

**Timing across devices.** A reader who used to see a desktop's beats and
a phone's beats under two keys, and could tell which was up, now sees one
freshest beat under the account and cannot -- unless they read the device
keys, which they still can. The change hides nothing from a determined
reader and discloses less to an idle one.

**The reach bit as a probe.** `reach = 1` says a wake would be sent for an
event, not that one was, and nothing here lets a reader cause one:
sending an event to an account still requires the standing SIP-16 and
SIP-36 give a member. A stranger cannot ring somebody by reading their
beacon.

**Correctness of "currently registered".** A revoked device's beats and
registrations MUST drop out of the set at once, or a phone that was lost
and revoked goes on reporting its owner reachable. Revocation already
resyncs admission (SIP-47); this is one more thing it resyncs.

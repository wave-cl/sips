## Abstract

SIP-39 rings a person for an exchange their operator listed, and named
the way to widen that without discarding the operator's veto: "a
consent-gated open mode, a callee opting in to cross-domain
reachability". This document is that mode, with the consent the corpus
already records. An exchange configured to take **calls openly** dials
out to, and rings its users for, an exchange nobody listed, when the
caller has **signed the call** and the two people **already share a
conversation** -- a private channel this exchange holds in which both
are present members. The caller's signature is over the call itself (the
target, the ephemeral, the moment), carried across the link so the
callee's exchange verifies who is calling rather than taking the far
exchange's word for it; the shared membership is the callee's own signed
act of talking to this person, read from the log. A SIP-56 row bounds
what a caller may ring. Rooms (SIP-49) too: a member who asks its
exchange to share a room with another exchange **signs the asking** --
the room, the exchange, the time -- and the share carries that word; an
exchange taking calls open honours a share from an exchange it does not
list when the word verifies, the signer is a member the sender vouches
for as its own, and the signer's account shares a private channel with a
member of the room here. A device invite over such a link is honoured
for a device the admitted share named.

## Motivation

SIP-35 §Open peering opened every copy to a member's word and left one
gate listed, for
a stated reason: a ring reaches a person, and SIP-56 had no row for it.
The consequence is that two people who talk every day across two open
exchanges -- a direct message that lives at one home and reads at the
other, a group with members at three -- cannot call each other unless
two operators have named each other's key. The conversation was opened
on their own word; the call is refused for want of somebody else's.

SIP-39 was right about the risk. An exchange that rings its users because
an arbitrary domain asked is a spam surface, and SIP-39's list made
federation a decision an operator made. But the list was the only
consent SIP-39 had to hand. Since then the log records a better one: a
person who is a present member of a channel with somebody has, by a
signed entry -- a direct message opened, an invitation accepted, a group
joined -- said they talk to that person. That is the consent a call
should rest on, and it is the callee's, not an operator's.

One thing the list also did, quietly, is vouch for the caller. An
`Invite` names the caller by key and the callee's exchange rings on that
name, trusting the exchange that sent it because that exchange is
listed. From a stranger, the name proves nothing: any exchange could
send `Invite { caller: A }` for an `A` who shares a channel with the
callee, and the callee's phone would show `A`. So the word this document
rests on is not the far exchange's but the caller's own.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-73.* SIP-49's live run and every one since had the same shape: two exchanges
that list each other. SIP-35 §Open peering and the calls half of this document made
listing unnecessary for copies and for calls, and SIP-60 made a private channel between two
people at two exchanges ordinary. A call in such a channel (SIP-36) is a
room (SIP-13), and the room is shared across the exchanges by SIP-49 --
which refuses the share the moment one of them is not on the other's
list. So the pair can write to each other, ring each other, and not
speak: the invitation posts, the ring is heard, the room is joined at
each exchange, and each member sees a roster of one.

What the calls half said of rooms is the reason: a share was the
exchange's word and nothing else. The fix is the one made for calls
above, applied to the act that starts a share -- a member asking for it -- which is the
member's own act and can carry the member's own signature.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian. **X**, **Y**, **A**, **B**, the
**relay link**, `Invite`, `CallOpen` and the **relay-peer allowlist**
are SIP-39's; **present member** is SIP-16's; **credential** is
SIP-20's; **home** is SIP-59's. **Relay link**, **relay peer**,
`JoinShared`, `Homed`, `RoomShare`, `InviteDevice`, `SHARE_SECS` and
`TTL_SECS` are SIP-49's; **present member** is SIP-16's.

### The caller's word

```
CALL_CONTEXT = "sqex-call-v1"                          (ASCII, no terminator)
call_input   = CALL_CONTEXT || eph[32] || tgt_len: u8 || target || issued: u64
CallWord     | issued: u64 | cred_len: u16 | credential | sig[64] |
```

`sig` is Ed25519 by the caller's **device** key over `call_input`; `eph`
and `target` are the `CallOpen`'s own; `issued` is when it was signed.
`credential` is the SIP-20 credential binding the device to its account,
present when the device is not the account itself (`cred_len = 0`
otherwise), exactly as SIP-59 carries one with a forwarded post.

The client sends its exchange a signed open:

```
CallOpen | type: u8 = 0x08 | eph[32] | tgt_len: u8 | target | CallWord |
```

`0x05` is SIP-39's unsigned form and remains valid; an exchange treats
an unsigned open as a call that may go only where the list allows. A
word that does not verify under the device that sent the open is no
word: the open goes on as an unsigned one.

X carries the word to Y whole, in a signed invite:

```
Invite | 0x08 | bridge[16] | caller[32] | caller_eph[32] | account[32]
       | dom_len: u8 | caller_domain | tgt_len: u8 | target | CallWord |
```

`caller_eph` MUST equal the `eph` the word was signed over, and `caller`
the device that signed it. `MAX_CONTROL` (SIP-39) rises to 1536 to hold
it. To a peer not on its list, `0x08` is the only invite X may send
(below). To a peer on its list X sends SIP-39's `0x01` first, since a
listed peer from before this document cannot decode `0x08`; if that
peer answers `Reject { refused }` and X holds a word for the bridge, X
sends the same bridge's invite again as `0x08`, once -- the peer may be
one that does not list X and wants the caller's word -- and a second
refusal stands.

### Open calls

An exchange is configured to take calls **listed** (SIP-39 as written)
or **open**, independently of SIP-35's peering mode. Open changes two
gates:

**Dialling (X).** Having discovered the target's exchange and resolved
the account `B` (SIP-39 §Placing a call, steps 1 and 3; SIP-59's home
followed once), X places the call over the link to an exchange not on
its list when all of:

1. the open is signed, and the word verifies under `caller` for this
   `eph` and `target`;
2. the caller's account `A'` -- the device itself, or the account its
   registration here names (SIP-22) -- and `B` are both present members
   of some **private** channel X holds, ordered here or copied (SIP-35,
   SIP-59) and, for a copy, shaped (SIP-43): a copy not yet told its
   shape is written private by default and cannot vouch;
3. `A'` is under the `calls` limit (below).

Otherwise X answers the caller `rejected { refused }` as SIP-39 does for
an unlisted domain. A listed peer is dialled as before.

**Ringing (Y).** On an `Invite` over a link from an exchange not on its
list, Y rings `account` only when all of:

1. the invite is the signed form, `caller` is the device that signed
   the word, `caller_eph` is the `eph` signed over, and the signature
   verifies;
2. `issued` is within `CALL_WORD_SECS = 120` of Y's clock, and Y has not
   rung on this `(caller, eph)` before within that window -- a word is
   honoured once;
3. `target` names Y: its domain is Y's own (SIP-60's `domain`) where Y
   knows one, and its label is `account`'s key, or a name Y resolves
   (SIP-38) to `account`;
4. the caller's account `A'` is `caller` itself where `cred_len = 0`,
   or the account the credential names, the credential verifying under
   it for scope chat, unexpired, with `delegate = caller`;
5. `A'` and `account` are both present members of some private channel
   Y holds, shaped where it is a copy, as rule 2 of dialling;
6. `A'` is under the `calls` limit.

Otherwise Y answers `Reject { refused }` -- the same coarse reason
SIP-39 gives for everything a stranger may not have, and it stays
coarse: a failed rule discloses nothing about `account`, the channel, or
whether `A'` is known here. A link from an exchange on Y's list is
served as SIP-39 says, whichever form the invite takes; a signed invite
from a listed peer is verified too, and refused if it fails, since a
peer that can sign should not send a word that does not verify.

**The link.** An open exchange accepts a relay link from any identified
exchange (SIP-39 §The relay link, the accepting side), and grants it
nothing by that: every invite on it meets the rules above. A listed
exchange closes an unlisted link at once, as SIP-39 says.

### The member's word

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-73.* *Until 0.91.0 `RoomShare` and `InviteDevice`
were served to listed peers only.*

```
ROOM_CONTEXT = "sqex-room-v1"                       (ASCII, no terminator)
room_input   = ROOM_CONTEXT || handle[32] || peer[32] || issued: u64
RoomWord     | issued: u64 | cred_len: u16 | credential | sig[64] |
```

`sig` is Ed25519 by the member's **device** key over `room_input`, where
`handle` is the room's and `peer` is the key of the exchange the member
asks its own to share the room with; `issued` is when it was signed;
`credential` binds the device to its account as `CallWord` above
carries one, `cred_len = 0` where the device is the account. The shape
is `CallWord`'s exactly, over a different context.

### Joining with a word

```
JoinShared | type: u8 = 0x05 | handle[32] | proof[32] | count: u8
           | count × (exchange[32] | dom_len: u8 | domain | RoomWord) |
```

SIP-49's shared join, with a domain and a word per exchange named. It is
answered `Homed` as `0x04` is. `0x04` remains valid and names listed
peers only, as SIP-49 says. An exchange taking calls **listed** refuses
`0x05` naming an exchange not on its list (403), as it refuses `0x04`;
one taking calls **open** accepts it, keeps each word beside the room
and the peer it names, finds the exchange by its `domain` (SIP-33),
checks the key, and brings up a link (SIP-39; §Open calls, the link) where
it holds none. A word that does not verify under the joining device for
its `handle` and `exchange` is refused as malformed, and nothing is
joined. A `domain` may be empty for an exchange on the list, which the
list knows how to find, or one the exchange already holds a link to --
which is every exchange whose members a `Homed` answer has shown.

A client SHOULD sign a word for every exchange it names, whether or not
it believes that exchange listed: the cost is a signature, and the
client cannot see the lists. A client that is shown, in a `Homed`
answer, a member homed at an exchange it has not named SHOULD join again
naming it: that is how the second side of an unlisted pair comes to hold
a word to share back with.

### Sharing with the word

```
RoomShareSigned | 0x0a | handle[32] | count: u8 | count × (identity[32] | proof[32] | home[32])
                | sharer[32] | RoomWord |
```

Over a link to an exchange **on its list**, an exchange shares as SIP-49
says, with `0x06`. Over a link to an exchange **not on its list** it
sends `0x0a` and nothing else: the share as `0x06` carries it, then the
device whose word it stands on and the word. `sharer` MUST be among the
members shared with `home` zero -- a member of the sender's own -- and
the word MUST be one that device gave the sender for this `handle` and
this peer. Sent when a `0x06` would be, for as long as the sender holds
such a word and the room has members at either end.

### Honouring a share

An exchange **Y** receiving `0x0a` over a link from an exchange **X**
not on its list keeps the share -- as SIP-49 keeps any share, soft
state until the next -- only when all of:

1. Y takes calls open;
2. `sharer` is in the share with `home` zero, and the word verifies
   under `sharer` for `handle` and Y's own key as `peer`;
3. the sharer's account `A'` is `sharer` itself where `cred_len = 0`, or
   the account the credential names, the credential verifying under it
   for scope chat, unexpired, with `delegate = sharer`;
4. Y has a **local member** of `handle` -- a device that joined the
   room here -- whose account and `A'` are both present members of some
   private channel Y holds, shaped where it is a copy (ringing rule 5
   above); a room nobody has joined here admits no share;
5. on the **first** share of `handle` over this link, `issued` is within
   `ROOM_WORD_SECS = 120` of Y's clock and `A'` is under the `calls`
   limit (SIP-56; §Limits). Later shares of the same room over the same
   link **stand on the first** while the link is up: rules 2 and 3 are
   checked on each, and rules 4 and 5 are not repeated. A share is sent
   every `SHARE_SECS` for as long as a call lasts, so a member is not
   asked to sign every ten seconds; and the word a later share carries
   may be another member's -- whichever of the sender's members is still
   in the room -- so a room is not lost when the member who first asked
   for it leaves. The admission ends with the link (SIP-49 §Sharing on
   the relay link), and the next share over a new link is a first share.

A share failing any rule is dropped without answer, as SIP-49 answers
no share; the sender goes on sharing and a share that later passes --
rule 4 most often, once a member has joined here -- is kept then. A
`0x06` over an unlisted link is dropped. A `0x0a` over a link to an
exchange on Y's list is verified as above and kept if it passes: a peer
that can sign should not send a word that does not verify.

**Reciprocity.** SIP-49 has the first share of a room from a peer
answered with the receiver's own view. Over an unlisted link Y answers
with `0x0a` when it holds a word from a local member of the room naming
X, and with nothing otherwise; when such a word arrives later -- a
member joining again naming X, as clients are asked to above -- Y
shares then. Until it does, X's members see Y's as absent, which is
what they are to X.

### Device invites

An `InviteDevice` over a link from an exchange not on Y's list is
honoured -- the bridge recorded, the open matched -- when `device` is a
local member of a room for which Y currently keeps a share from that
link, admitted on a word, naming `caller` among its members. It is
answered `Reject { refused }` otherwise. The placing side is gated the
same way: X sends a device invite to an exchange not on its list when
the device named is among the members of a share X admitted from that
exchange, for a room the caller is a member of here, and refuses the
`CallOpen` otherwise. SIP-49's rule that the invite rings nobody is
unchanged, and so is the bridge once it stands.

### Limits

SIP-56's table gains one row:

| What | Counted per | Reference default |
|---|---|---|
| `calls` | caller account | 20 per hour |

counted at X when it dials for `A'` and at Y when it rings for `A'`,
each against its own bucket; a call over the limit at X is `rejected {
refused }` to the caller with no invite sent, and at Y is `Reject {
refused }`. `CALL_WORD_SECS = 120`. Y keeps `(caller, eph)` pairs for
`CALL_WORD_SECS` and no longer.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-73:*

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| `ROOM_WORD_SECS` | 120 |
| Exchanges named in one `JoinShared 0x05` | 4, as `0x04` |
| `RoomShareSigned` bytes | `MAX_CONTROL` |
| First shares per account | the `calls` limit |

## Rationale

**Why a shared private membership is the consent.** SIP-39 named "a
callee opting in to cross-domain reachability" and left the form open. A
per-person flag ("anyone may call me") is the email model SIP-39
rejected; a per-caller allowlist kept at the exchange is a second contact
list that drifts from the first. A present membership in a private
channel is a fact the callee already signed for, in the log the callee
already reads, revocable the way the callee already revokes things --
leave the channel, remove the member -- and it names exactly the
population "people I talk to". It is the same reasoning SIP-35 gave for
an acts-for peer needing no per-channel signature: the disclosure is one
the person already made.

**Why not a public channel.** Anyone joins one (SIP-16), so two accounts
in the same public channel have said nothing about each other; counting
it would let everyone in a busy room ring everyone else in it, which is
the email model with a room in front of it. The first live run found
exactly this: a caller sharing nothing but a copied `#general` with the
callee was rung through.

**Why the caller signs the call, not the invite.** The invite is X's
message; a signature by X would prove X sent it, which the link already
proves, and X is the party whose word is not enough. The call is the
caller's act, and the caller's device key is the only key that can vouch
for it. Signing over the ephemeral binds the key agreement to the word:
Y verifies that the `eph` the callee will derive against is the one the
caller chose, so an exchange on the path cannot substitute its own and
sit in the SIP-12 key agreement -- a property SIP-39's unsigned invite
never had, and one a listed peer now gets for free.

**Why `target` and not the account.** The client signs before its
exchange has resolved anything; what it knows is what the person typed.
Binding the string binds the domain, so a word for `bob@y.test` cannot
be replayed to ring the `bob` of `z.test`, and Y checks the label
against the account it is asked to ring rather than trusting X's
resolution of it -- which is also the one place SIP-39's name-binding
trust is narrowed: Y rings the account its own resolution names.

**Why a word is honoured once.** X sees the word, and X is the caller's
own exchange; within the window it could ring again on the caller's
behalf. It could always ring on the caller's behalf while the caller was
polling; what it must not do is ring after the caller stopped, and the
window plus the once-rule bounds that to one extra ring at most. Nothing
finer is worth a nonce round trip before every call.

**Why a mode, and why not open peering's.** SIP-35 §Open peering's
argument for a mode holds
here, and the two are separate switches because they cost different
things: a copy costs the exchange storage a member was entitled to; a
ring costs a person's attention, and an operator may open one and not
the other. The default is listed, as open peering's is.

**Why rooms stayed listed at first.** A `RoomShare` is an exchange's own
assertion about who is in a room, and `InviteDevice` rings nobody but
rests on a roster the exchanges exchanged. Neither had a member's
signature to stand on until §The member's word gave them one, the day
after the first live run of calls.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-73:*

**Why the member signs the peer and not the room alone.** A word over
the handle alone would let X carry one member's wish to be seen at Y
into a share with Z. Binding the exchange named makes the word what the
member actually asked: share this room with that exchange. It is
the reason above for binding `target`.

**Why the private membership is with a local member of the room.** A
room handle says nothing to an exchange -- it holds no secret and cannot
tie the room to a channel. What Y can know is who has joined the room
here, and whether the person asking to be seen in it is somebody one of
them already talks to. That is the calls' consent, found through the room
rather than through the call, and it is why a room nobody has joined
here admits no share: there is nobody whose consent could be read.

**Why a word is not re-signed every share.** A call lasts as long as it
lasts and a share goes every ten seconds; a signature every ten seconds
would be a signature nobody looked at. The first share is where the
question is asked and answered, and the link, the room and the members
at both ends are what keep the answer standing. A word replayed later
over the same link re-admits the same room between the same people,
which is nothing.

**Why the domain travels in the join.** SIP-49 names peers by key
because a listed peer's key is enough to find it. An unlisted exchange
is one the exchange knows nothing about, and the member who wants it
named is the one who knows where it is -- from a `Homed` answer, a
location (SIP-60), or the person who told them.

**Why device invites need no word of their own.** The invite is X's
message, as §Open calls says of `Invite`; what makes it honourable is that
the caller it names is a member X has already vouched for under a word
Y verified. Adding a second word would prove what the first already did.

**What was rejected.** Letting Y admit a share for a room nobody has
joined here yet, held pending: it would make Y keep strangers' rosters
on their own say-so, which is the disclosure and the cost SIP-49's list
existed to refuse. Signing the roster itself: the roster is the
exchange's knowledge and changes on every join; the member can sign
only what the member did.

## Compatibility

Additive. `0x05` opens and `0x01` invites are unchanged and a listed pair
behaves exactly as SIP-39 specifies. An exchange from before this
document refuses a `0x08` open as malformed, and a client SHOULD fall
back to `0x05` on that refusal; it also fails to decode a `0x08` invite,
which X sends to an unlisted peer only -- one that, before this document,
would have closed the link on arrival, so nothing that connected before
stops connecting. `MAX_CONTROL` grows; a peer holding the old bound
refuses only the frame it could not have understood.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-73.* Additive: one join type, one relay control, and one gate opened. An
exchange from before §The member's word (0.91.0) refuses `0x05` as malformed, so a
client falls back to `0x04` and the listed behaviour; it drops `0x0a` as
an unknown control, so an open exchange sharing with it is not seen
there, which is where things stood. A listed exchange refuses `0x05`
naming an unlisted exchange exactly as it refuses `0x04`.

## Security considerations

**Caller identity is proven, not asserted.** Over an unlisted link the
only thing Y acts on is a signature by the device it rings for, over the
call it rings; a stranger exchange gains the ability to *carry* a call,
not to invent one. Over a listed link nothing weakens: an unsigned
invite is served as before, and a signed one is checked.

**What an unlisted exchange can do.** Carry calls from its own users to
people they already talk to, up to the `calls` limit per caller, and
bring up a link. It cannot ring anyone its users share no channel with,
cannot name a caller who did not sign, and cannot replay a word past the
window or twice within it. What it learns is SIP-39's coarse `Reject`
and nothing that varies by whether the callee exists, shares a channel,
or is reachable -- every failed rule answers the same.

**The shared channel is disclosed to nobody.** Y checks its own store and
says only yes or no, and no; X likewise. Neither names the channel on
the link.

**The callee's exchange is trusted as it always was.** Y is B's home, by
B's Move or by discovery of B's domain, and holds B's registry; a
dishonest Y could always answer a call with a device of its choosing,
listed or not. This document does not change that trust, which is
SIP-39's and SIP-38's; it removes the operator's vouching for X and
replaces it with A's own signature, which is stronger for the one thing
the vouching was for.

**A stolen device key.** Signs calls as the device, as it signs
everything else; SIP-22's revocation ends it, and until then the thief
can ring the people the victim talks to, which the thief could do from
the victim's own exchange regardless.

**Presence.** `Reject { unreachable }` remains the one thing Y says
about B's presence, to a caller who has already proven a shared
conversation with B -- the same population that can see B's read marks
and typing signals in that conversation (SIP-43 §Signals at a replica). No new disclosure.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-73.* **Consent** is the calls', read through the room: nobody is shown in a
room here on the word of an exchange alone, and the person whose word
it is must already share a private channel with somebody in the room.

**What a stranger exchange learns.** Nothing before a share is
admitted; after, the same roster SIP-49 discloses to a listed peer --
identities and proofs of the room's members here. It learns that the
sharer's account is known here and shares a channel with a member, which
the admission itself says. A dropped share says nothing.

**What one word admits.** The share is the room as the sender has it:
every member there, not only the one whose word it carries. That is
SIP-49's roster, admitted whole once one member of it is somebody a
member here already talks to -- and the roster is bounded by the room's
secret (SIP-13), which nobody joins a room without. A stranger cannot
ride in on another's word without first being in the room.

**Replay.** A word names the room, the exchange and the time; it admits
the same room over a link to the same exchange, and nothing else. Within
the window, X could share a room its member has left: the member's
proof is still in the roster, the member is not, and SIP-13's session
mesh needs the member's own opens.

**Denial of service.** The first share per account is counted under the
`calls` limit, a room admits nothing until somebody here has joined it,
and what is kept is bounded as SIP-49 bounds it.

## Reference implementation

`sqex` 0.82.0–0.82.2; 0.91.0 (rooms). `sqex-proto::session::CallWord`, `CallOpen` in both
forms (`word: Option<CallWord>`, `sign`/`verify_word`), `relay::Control
::Invite` with an optional word and `TYPE_INVITE_SIGNED = 0x08`,
`MAX_CONTROL = 1536`, `CALL_CONTEXT`, `CALL_WORD_SECS`. `sqexd`:
`open_calls` in the configuration (reported at startup beside the
peering mode); `place_call` dials an unlisted exchange on a signed open
and a shared present membership (`Channels::share_membership`), sending
the signed invite, and keeps the word on the bridge to send it again
once when a listed peer refuses the plain one; `serve_relay` accepts
unlisted links when open; `on_control` verifies a signed invite through
the six rules (`word_rings`), with a `(caller, eph)` cache; a `calls`
limit kind at both ends. `sqex-voice` signs every cross-exchange open
and falls back to the plain one at an exchange that refuses it; the
SIP-49 device open stays plain. Tests: two exchanges listing nobody, a
private group at one with a member homed at the other, a signed call
that rings and an unsigned one refused; a caller sharing only a public
channel with the callee refused at her own exchange, and -- where her
exchange lists the callee's and asks nothing -- refused by the callee's;
a word signed by
somebody else treated as none; a callee's exchange keeping to its list
closing the link; a listed pair unchanged. The wire forms round-trip
with and without a credential, and a word verifies under the signing
device alone and only for its ephemeral and target.

Run live on 2026-09-18 between squic.org and trunk.exchange, both open,
with trunk.exchange taken off squic.org's relay list: a signed call from
an account at squic.org to one at trunk.exchange, the two sharing a
direct message, connected and carried Opus both ways with no loss over a
link squic.org never listed. The first run of the control -- an account
sharing nothing private with the callee -- connected too: the two shared
trunk.exchange's public `#general`, copied at squic.org, and 0.82.0
counted it. 0.82.2 counts a private channel only, and the control is
refused. The run also found discovery dead from both servers for an

**Rooms** (§The member's word and after, sqex 0.91.0). `sqex-proto`:
`session::RoomWord` and `ROOM_WORD_SECS`,
`room::JoinSharedWord` (`0x05`), `relay::Control::RoomShareSigned`
(`0x0a`). `sqexd::room`: words kept per room and peer -- one per member
that gave one, the newest from a member still present chosen for a
share -- the domain each was named by, admissions per peer, and
`vouched_pair` for device invites; `relay::word_shares` in the
document's order, `share_control` choosing the form by the list,
`share_back` only where a word is held, `link_to_key` finding an
unlisted peer by the domain a member named; the `/room/join` route for
`0x05`, refusing a stranger where calls are listed and a word that does
not verify as malformed; `place_call` sending a device invite to an
unlisted peer on an admitted share. `sqex-voice`: `--share key` or
`key@domain`, a word signed per heartbeat, `0x05` falling back to `0x04`
and then to `0x01`, and every home a `Homed` answer shows named from
then on.

Building it moved one rule. The first draft had rule 4 -- the sharer's
account shares a private channel with a member here -- checked on every
share; then a second member of the sender's asking for the same peer
replaced the first's word, and the room would have gone dark at the
receiver the moment the first member left, or on the next share if the
second shared nothing with anybody here. Later shares now stand on the
admission, and the sender keeps a word per member.

Tests: two exchanges that list nobody, both taking calls open, with a
private channel between Alice at one and Bob at the other. A plain
shared join naming the stranger is refused; a word signed for the wrong
exchange is malformed and joins nothing. Carol, in the room first and
sharing nothing private with Bob, is not seen on her own word; Alice's
word is, and Carol comes with her, as the room's roster does. Nothing
comes back until Bob names Alice's exchange -- with no domain, the link
being up -- and then she sees him. The pair's bridge comes up over the
unlisted link with the higher's open waiting. Checked by refusing every
word and watching Bob never see Alice.

Run live on 2026-09-19 between `trunk.exchange` (0.91.0) and an
exchange on a laptop that neither lists, both taking calls open, the
laptop reachable by nobody -- every link was the one it opened. A member
at the laptop and one at trunk held a private direct message between
them, ordered at trunk and copied to the laptop. The laptop's member
joined a room naming trunk by `key@trunk.exchange`; within four seconds
trunk's member saw them homed at the laptop, named the laptop in turn
with no domain, and the laptop's member saw them homed at trunk. The
lower placed the bridge at trunk naming the laptop by key and it was
established on the first poll, the higher's open having been waiting
five polls at the laptop -- over a link nobody listed, on two words.

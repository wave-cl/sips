## Abstract

A direct message's identifier is derived from its two accounts, so two
exchanges can each hold a channel of that name for the same pair, each a
complete conversation with its own incarnation, key and log, and neither
aware of the other. SIP-60 says where a direct message lives -- at the
home of the lower key -- and that rule is applied here to the ones that
already exist twice: the copy at the lower key's home is the
conversation, and the other is a **stray**. The home of the lower key,
on learning of a stray, tells the exchange holding it; that exchange
**folds** it -- ends its log, keeps it for both parties to read, points
the identifier at the conversation, and refuses to open another. A
client keeps what it read of the folded copy beneath the conversation
that continues, so nothing either of them wrote is lost -- its files included: the
stray's attachments are kept beside its log for the same window and the
same two people, fetchable by either from the exchange that folded.

## Motivation

SIP-44 stated the problem and did not solve it, and SIP-59 stated it
again: an account moving home may find its new home already ordering a
direct message of the same name, and the home skips the pull, so *the
account reads that one where it was* -- which is two conversations with
one name, found by the same client under the same key, one of them
reachable only by connecting somewhere else.

SIP-60 fixed where a direct message is *opened*. It did not fix the
ones opened before it, the ones opened by a client that could not locate
the other party, or the ones two people opened at their own exchanges
before either exchange federated with the other. On 2026-09-17, during
SIP-60's own live run, two clients that had each derived the same
identifier at two exchanges found two channels, and the client-side
alias that made the conversation findable again was a repair to a
store, not a rule about which channel was the conversation. Every
account that has ever written to the same person from two exchanges has
one of these.

Nothing in the log can settle it. Both channels are well-formed; both
constitutions are signed; both pairs of members are the rightful pair
by SIP-16's arithmetic. The two logs cannot be merged: their positions,
receipts and sealing keys belong to different incarnations, and SIP-44
already declined to rewrite an identifier for less reason. What can be
settled is which one continues, and the answer SIP-60 gives for a new
direct message is the right one for an old one too.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-75.* The first draft of this document said, of attachments: *a blob is
pruned with its channel, and the folded log carries the entries that
referred to them.* That was stated for the sake of stating it, and it is wrong in the way that
matters: the log carries a reference the reader can decode -- the
blob's name, its key, its shape, a preview -- and nothing to fetch. A
photograph sent in a conversation that turned out to be a stray is
shown as a photograph that was sent and cannot be seen. This document's whole
claim was that nothing either of them wrote is lost; a file is a thing
one of them wrote.

SIP-18 already has the shape for keeping a blob past a channel: a blob
belongs to its **attachments**, not to a message, and lives while any
attachment does. What a fold needs is an attachment that outlives the
channel it was made in, for the window the log does, held by the pair
the log is held for.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian. **Attachment**, **blob** and the fetch rules are
SIP-18's; a **generation** and the trade are SIP-42's (§Generations).

### Which copy is the conversation

A **direct message** is a channel whose identifier equals SIP-16's
derivation over its two present members; `first` is the lesser of the
two keys. The channel is **the conversation** exactly when it is
ordered at, or held as a copy by, the **home of `first`** as SIP-59
records it -- that is, the exchange `first`'s own signed Move names.
A channel of the same identifier ordered anywhere else is a **stray**.

Where the home of `first` orders none and holds none, there is no
conversation to prefer and nothing here applies. Where it holds one
already, that one is the conversation, whatever another exchange
holds: a stray that is elder, longer, or busier is still a stray. This
rule needs no clock and no comparison between exchanges, and it is the
rule a SIP-60 client already applies when it opens a direct message.

An account that has not said where it lives has no home to decide, and
its direct messages are left as they are.

### The home learns of a stray

The home of `first` learns that an exchange holds a channel of the
identifier the way SIP-59 has a home learn of anything: by asking an
origin what channels an account of its is in (`/peer/mine`), where the
origin is one the account's Move hinted at, or one that told the home
under SIP-60 (`/peer/invited`). An origin that answers with a direct
message identifier the home already holds -- ordered, or as a copy
from another origin -- is holding a stray, and the home SHOULD tell it
at once and on each later cycle it still answers so:

```
PeerFolded | type: u8 = 0x14 | channel[32] | first[32] | instance[32] | dom_len: u8 | domain |
```

`POST /peer/folded`, over the peering link, gated as `/peer/moved` is
(SIP-59; open peering admits any identified caller, SIP-63). `instance`
is the conversation's SIP-31 incarnation as the home holds it, and
`domain` is where the home is reached by SIP-33, a hint as everywhere.
The home carries `first`'s Move (`/peer/moved`) before it, as SIP-59
has it carry the Move before every act on the account's behalf.

### The stray's exchange verifies, then folds

An exchange receiving `PeerFolded` verifies, in order, and refuses
uniformly with SIP-35's `peering_refused` where any step fails:

1. it **orders** `channel` itself -- a copy it holds is its origin's to
   fold, not the caller's to ask about;
2. `channel`'s present members are exactly two, `first` is the lesser
   of them, and the derivation over the two equals `channel` -- so it is
   a direct message and `first` is its `first`, by arithmetic and not
   by the caller's word;
3. it holds a signed Move for `first` naming the caller: the caller is
   `first`'s home by `first`'s own statement, which is the only
   authority this document rests on;
4. `instance` is not its own -- a home naming the exchange's own
   incarnation is confused, and there is nothing to fold.

It then **folds** the channel, in one transaction:

- the channel's entries are kept as the **folded log** under
  `(channel, instance)` with the stray's own incarnation, for
  `FOLDED_SECS`, together with the two members' keys;
- the channel is destroyed as SIP-16 destroys a channel whose last
  member left -- its row, members, chain positions and envelopes go,
  its attachments are kept (§Folded attachments), and its incarnation
  is retired (SIP-31) so nothing
  signed under it is ever admitted again;
- a **fold record** `(channel, into = caller, domain, at)` is kept for
  `FOLDED_SECS`.

The stray's exchange answers `200`; `409` when it holds nothing under the
identifier at all (folded already, destroyed, never here); the uniform
refusal otherwise, which says no more than the channel itself would.

### What a fold record does

While a fold record stands, the identifier **points at the
conversation**:

- `Create` naming `channel` is refused with SIP-35's `replicated`,
  naming `into` -- the same refusal a copy gives a write, and for the
  same reason: the channel lives elsewhere. SIP-16's rule for a
  returning party does not apply, because there is no channel here to
  return to; a client that wants to reopen the conversation opens it
  where it lives, which is where SIP-60 would have sent it.
- `/channel/home` for `channel` answers `into` with the domain hint --
  **to the two members**, and to anybody else as it answered before the
  fold. A direct message's identifier can be computed by anyone, and a
  fold record disclosed to a stranger would say that two people talk.
- The fold record is an **origin hint** for the other member, beside the
  hints SIP-59 has an account give itself: where that member is homed
  here, the home task pulls the conversation from `into` as it pulls any
  channel of theirs elsewhere, and wakes at once. The record stands
  apart from the account's own hints, because a later Move replaces
  those and must not lose this. A member who is not homed here learns
  from the refusal and the home answer, as they would for any channel
  that lives elsewhere.

Once the record has expired, the identifier is an ordinary one again.
A stray opened after that is folded again when the home of `first`
next sees it; nothing depends on the record outliving its window.

### Reading the folded log

```
Folded  | type: u8 = 0x23 | channel[32] |            POST /channel/folded
```

A `ByChannel` of type `0x23`, answered to **either member** of the
folded direct message -- both could read it while it was a channel,
and both can now -- with SIP-16's `Entries` as a fetch answers them:
every entry the exchange still holds of the most recent folded log for
that identifier, `first` and `last` its bounds, no signals, no tip. A
caller that is neither member, or an identifier with no folded log, is
answered `not_found`, which is what the channel itself would have
said. Attachments are kept with the log, for the same window and the
same two members (§Folded attachments).

### Folded attachments

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-75.* When an exchange folds a stray (§The stray's exchange verifies, then
folds), the channel's attachments are **kept as folded
attachments** -- each blob's name, with the fold's time -- rather than
released with the channel. A folded attachment holds its blob alive as
any attachment does (SIP-18 §Lifetime): a blob is deleted when it has
no attachment and no folded attachment left.

A folded attachment expires with the fold record, at `FOLDED_SECS`
after the fold, and MUST be swept on the same schedule as attachments
are; the blob goes then if nothing else holds it. A second fold of the
same identifier replaces the first's folded attachments as it replaces
its log.

### Fetching a folded attachment

SIP-18's `Head` and `Get` are answered for a blob a folded attachment
names **to the two members of the folded direct message**, as they are
answered to a member of a channel the blob is attached to. To anybody
else a folded attachment grants nothing: it is served on the same terms
as the folded log, to the same two people, and the fold record is
where those two are written.

A folded attachment never widens an audience. A blob a folded
attachment names may still be attached to other channels, whose members
fetch it on those channels' terms; SIP-18's rule that a private
attachment overrides a public one stands, and a folded attachment
counts as private for it.

`Attach` and `Detach` do not apply to a folded attachment: the channel
it belonged to is gone, and there is nobody to answer to.

### A copy of a stray

An exchange holding a copy of a stray (a replica under a `0x0b`, or a
home acting for a member) finds, on its next pull, that the origin no
longer serves the channel -- as it finds any channel its origin
destroyed -- and holds what it has until told otherwise. It is told the
way SIP-59 and SIP-60 tell a home anything: an origin it pulls for a
member of the pair lists the conversation, and it pulls that. A copy
MUST NOT carry held positions into the conversation: the conversation is
another incarnation, and a position carried across is SIP-32's fault
exactly. **A copy whose origin serves another incarnation of a channel
than the one it holds drops what it holds and takes the channel afresh**,
which is the rule for a recreated direct message at a replica as much as
for this; and **a channel an exchange orders is never taken as a copy**,
whatever an origin lists -- the stray is folded first, and only then is
the identifier a copy's to hold.

### The client keeps what it read

A client whose channel changed incarnation under it -- the copy it
read replaced by the conversation, at the same identifier, with a
different `instance` (SIP-31) -- MUST keep the messages it had read
and decrypted, shown before the conversation that continues and
marked as an earlier copy, and MUST NOT delete them. SIP-17 forbids
decrypting a counter twice and the exchange serves an epoch key once,
so a message a client drops is one it can never read again.

A client notices the change by the exchange's word -- `Channel`'s
`instance` against the one it holds -- and not only by SIP-16's cursor
rule, which a conversation longer than the stray never trips, nor by
anything it signs, which a reader never does. Before it resets the
sequence space (SIP-16), it SHOULD ask `/channel/folded` at the exchange
it reads from, and where a log is answered, read what it had not yet
read of it under the keys it still holds, so the kept history is whole.
The incarnation it records after the reset MUST be the one the exchange
serves now, whichever rule noticed: a client that recorded a stale one
reset again on its next poll, and took with it an epoch key it had
collected in between, which the exchange serves once. A client from
before this document deletes, as it did, and the entries stay at the
exchange for the window.

A client whose channel is no longer served at all where it read it --
the exchange folded it and holds no copy of the conversation -- and
that is answered a folded log there MUST keep what it read the same way
(found on the 2026-09-19 live run below: the folding exchange could not
reach the lower key's home, so nothing ever replaced the stray).

A client whose `Create` of a direct message is refused `replicated`
opens it at the exchange named, as SIP-60 has it open one at the lower
key's home, and reads the copy its own exchange pulls.

A client showing an earlier copy shows its files as it shows
a live message's -- described from the reference, fetched on request --
and MAY fetch them from the exchange it reads at, which is the one that
folded, or from its own store where SIP-42 put them.

### Limits

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| `FOLDED_SECS` | 604800 (7 days), the same as SIP-53's `STRANDED_SECS` |
| Folded logs kept per identifier | 1, the most recent |
| Fold tells per origin per channel | 1 per home cycle |
| Folded attachments kept | with the fold record, `FOLDED_SECS` |
| Blobs per folded log | as the channel had, bounded by SIP-18's quota |

## Rationale

**Why the lower key's home decides, and not the elder copy.** An elder
copy is a comparison between two exchanges' clocks, which SIP-69 found
are not close enough to order two events by, and it would need a
tie-break on top. The home of the lower key is one exchange, deciding
about one account of its own, under a rule every SIP-60 client already
applies when it opens a direct message -- so the conversation ends up
where the next `open` would have put it anyway. And the decision costs
nothing: the stray is not lost, only ended, and both parties read all
of it.

**Why one exchange tells and the other acts.** Two exchanges each
deciding which copy is the conversation is SIP-53's *two rehomes* with
a rule needed to make them agree. One decider needs no agreement. The
stray's exchange verifies the decider is who it claims -- the home, by
the account's own signed Move -- and verifies the arithmetic itself;
the one thing it takes on the home's word is that the home holds the
identifier, and a home that lied would only have folded a log into a
conversation that does not exist, where the other member's client
would find nothing and open one, which the home would then hold.

**Why the stray is ended and not carried.** SIP-53 carries a log to a
new origin that continues it; the positions, receipts and head run on.
A stray cannot continue the conversation's log or be continued by it:
two logs, two incarnations, two sets of sealing keys. Rewriting either
into the other's numbering is what SIP-44 refused for identifiers and
would refuse here. What can be done is what SIP-53 does with entries
past a fork -- keep them for the people who wrote them -- and a folded
log is a strand of a whole channel, served to both members because
both were members.

**Why the identifier points on for a window and not for good.** A
pointer kept for good would outlive the conversation it points at:
`first` moves home again, or the pair destroy the conversation and
start over somewhere the pointer does not know. A window covers the
case the pointer is for -- a client that has not yet noticed -- and
after it the rule that made the fold makes the next one.

**Why the client keeps history on every incarnation change and not only
on a fold.** The client cannot always tell the two apart, and it does
not need to: a message this person read is theirs, whichever channel
it was read in, and SIP-16's reset was about sequence spaces, not about
forgetting. Deleting was an implementation's shortcut, and it was
lossy in the one way SIP-17 makes permanent.

**What was rejected.** Letting the *higher* key's home fold its own
stray on learning the lower's home: it can, safely, but two actors need
a rule to agree and one actor needs none. Merging by re-posting the
stray's messages into the conversation: the messages would carry the
wrong author's signature (the re-poster's) and the wrong time. A field
in the `Create` naming the intended home, so a stray could be detected
at creation: an assertion, and SIP-60 already sends the create to the
right place when the home is known.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-75:*

**Why an attachment and not a copy of the file.** SIP-18 keeps a file
alive by attachment and lets a message expire with it; a fold that
copied the bytes into some folded store would be a second lifetime
rule for the same bytes. Keeping the attachment, with a new holder and
a fixed window, is the mechanism already there.

**Why the pair and not the channel's members.** The channel has no
members: it was destroyed. Who may read the folded log is written in
the fold record, and who may fetch the files it names should be the
same two people, for the same reason -- both could while it was a
channel.

**Why the fetch is from the folding exchange.** That is where the blob
is, and where the log is served from. A client that reads the folded
log there reads the files there too; the other member's
client, at the other exchange, reads through its own connection to the
folding exchange as §Reading the folded log has it read the log.

**What was rejected.** Carrying the blobs to the conversation's home:
the conversation's channel is another log, and attaching a stray's
files to it would show files nobody posted there. Keeping the
attachments for good: the window above is the answer -- a client that
has not read its earlier copy in a week has had its chance, and a
sibling that did holds the file.

## Compatibility

Additive: one peering message, one channel route, one refusal on an
existing route for identifiers under a fold record, and one client
rule. An exchange from before this document refuses `/peer/folded` as
an unknown peering message, so a home tells it and nothing happens:
its stray stands, as it did. An exchange that folds while the home of
`first` is from before this document never folds, because nobody tells
it. A client from before this document deletes on an incarnation change
as it did, and is refused `replicated` on a `Create` under a fold
record, which it treats as it treats the same refusal from a copy.

SIP-59's line -- *a channel whose identifier the home already orders is
skipped* -- is amended: the pull is skipped, and the origin is told.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-75.* Additive on the exchange: a fold keeps rows it released before, and two
existing routes answer where they answered *not found*. Nothing changes
on the wire. An exchange from before §Folded attachments (0.93.0) releases a
stray's attachments as it did, and a client asking it for a file of an earlier
copy is told it is not there, which is what it was told before.

## Security considerations

**Authority.** A fold rests on one signed statement, the account's own
Move, and one computation, SIP-16's derivation, both checked by the
exchange that acts. The peering link's admission and rate (SIP-63,
`Kind::Peering`) bound how often anyone can ask.

**What a lying home can do.** An exchange that is `first`'s home by
`first`'s own signature can end the stray whenever it likes, whether or
not it holds a conversation to point at. That is no more than a home
could already do: SIP-59 lets it pull, forward and read everything
`first` is in. The stray is not destroyed -- it is kept and served to
both members for the window -- so the worst outcome is a conversation
that has to be reopened.

**Disclosure.** `/peer/folded` tells the stray's exchange that `first`
is homed at the caller, which `/account/home` answers to any identified
caller already, and that the caller holds a channel of that identifier,
which follows from `first` being a member of it. The folded log is
served to the two members and nobody else, as the channel was.

**Denial of service.** A fold record and a folded log are bounded by
the window and by the one-per-identifier rule, and a fold can only be
caused for a channel the exchange orders whose lesser member has a
Move on record naming the caller -- so the most an attacker can fold
is their own direct messages, from their own home.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-75.* **Audience.** A folded attachment is fetchable by exactly the two
people who could fetch it while the channel stood, for a bounded time.
It grants nothing to a third party, and is never the attachment that
lets a blob be fetched publicly.

**Storage.** A fold that keeps files costs what the channel cost, for a
week at most, and the channel's quota already bounded it. A stray is
made by a member of the pair, and the attachments were theirs.

**A blob shared across channels.** A blob attached to a stray and to a
group stays fetchable by the group's members on the group's terms and
by the pair on the fold's; the fold's expiry releases only the fold's
hold.

## Reference implementation

`sqex` 0.89.0; 0.93.0, 0.93.1 (folded attachments). `sqex-proto::peer::PeerFolded` (`0x14`) and
`channel::TYPE_FOLDED` (`0x23`). `sqexd`: `folded` and `folded_entry`
tables, `Channels::{direct_pair, held_direct_pair, instance_of, fold,
folded_into, folded_hints, folded_log}`, `FOLDED_SECS` and the sweep;
`/peer/folded` in the document's order of checks, `/channel/folded`,
`/channel/home` for a folded identifier to the pair; `Create` refused
`replicated` under a fold record; `replica::tell_folded` from the home
task, which reads fold records as origin hints beside the accounts' own.
`sqex-chat`: `message_history` and `Store::archive_messages` at the one
place every reset passes; `Chat::{folded_entries, earlier}` and the
poll's incarnation check; `open_dm` following a `replicated` refusal;
earlier copies above a divider in the client, readable and nothing else.

Building it found four things that were already wrong, each of which an
independent implementation should check for.

**The home never knew what it ordered.** SIP-59's *a channel the home
already orders is skipped* was implemented against a column the table
does not have; the query failed quietly, the check answered *no* for
every channel, and a home pulled its own channels from any origin that
listed them -- where the replica's `adopt` kept the row and marked it a
copy of the origin. Nothing noticed for twelve documents because
nothing had made an origin list a channel the home ordered until a
direct message did. A copy is now never adopted over a channel the
exchange orders, and a copy held under another incarnation is dropped
and taken afresh, which SIP-32's recreated direct message needed too.

**`replicated` named nobody.** SIP-35 says the refusal names the origin
so a client can go there; the detail was empty. It carries the key now,
which is what a client refused a `Create` under a fold record follows.

**A cached answer outlives the fold.** The client caches a channel's
`info` and its home per connection. Read after a fold, the stale `info`
hid the changed incarnation while the first entries of the conversation
were folded under the stray's keys and verified under the stray's
exchange; refreshed on arrival and re-asked on a change, with the
exchange that ordered the copy that ended kept among the keys the log
may verify under (SIP-53's former origins), the same log reads clean.

**Two resets spent the key.** The cursor rule noticed the restart first,
with a stale incarnation recorded; the fresh one noticed it again on the
next poll, reset again, and deleted the epoch key collected in between
-- and the exchange had served that envelope once. Hence the rule above
that the incarnation recorded is the one served now.

Tests: at the exchange, a stray at the higher key's exchange is folded
on the lower key's home's word, with the telling refused without the
Move, refused naming the higher key as `first`, refused naming the
exchange's own incarnation, and `409` for an identifier it does not
hold; the fold record answers `/channel/home` to the pair and not to a
third party; a `Create` by a member is refused `replicated` naming the
home; the folded log reads to both members and to nobody else, and the
home that folded nothing serves none; the other member's home pulls the
conversation once they say it is their home, and they write into it
there. Checked by disabling the telling and watching the fold never
happen. At the client: a store reset archives what was read as a
generation, twice; and two clients at two exchanges -- a stray with two
messages, a conversation with one -- end with the stray's two shown as
an earlier copy, the conversation continuing, a reply through the
folded side read at the home, and a second `open` finding the copy and
making no second stray.

Deployed on two exchanges on 2026-09-19. A stray made on the day before
the deploy -- a direct message opened at `trunk.exchange` by the higher
key, two messages, the lower key never having connected there under
SIP-60 -- was folded twelve seconds after the lower key hinted its home
at the other exchange, on the home's first `/peer/mine`. The higher
key's client then showed the two as an earlier copy above the
conversation, read the folded log at the exchange that ended it, wrote
into the conversation through its own exchange, and was read at the
home; a third party asking that exchange about the identifier was told
nothing.

**Folded attachments** (§Folded attachments, §Fetching a folded
attachment, sqex 0.93.0). `sqexd::channel`: a `folded_attachment` table written in
`fold` before the channel is destroyed -- which is what keeps `destroy`'s
collection from taking the blobs, since `collect_blob` now counts folded
attachments beside attachments; `may_fetch` grants a blob a folded
attachment names to the fold record's pair, and marks it private;
`sweep` drops folded attachments with the fold record and collects what
nothing names. `sqex-chat`: `/save e<n>.<seq> <path>` reaches a file in
the n-th earlier copy; the download is from the exchange the
client reads at. The SIP-42 §Generations half (a generation carries its blobs) needed
nothing: a generation's pages
already carried the blobs they named, opened under the generation's keys.

Tests: in the fold test, a file uploaded into the stray before
the fold is fetchable by both members afterwards and by nobody else;
checked by keeping no folded attachment and watching the file go with
the channel.

Run live on 2026-09-19 against `trunk.exchange` (0.93.0): a stray with a
file in it, made at trunk by the higher key; the lower key's home was an
exchange on a laptop trunk could not reach, so the conversation never
arrived at trunk and the fold left the higher key's exchange holding no
channel at all under the identifier -- only the folded log and the
file. The higher key's client read the log as an earlier copy and
fetched the file from trunk, 45 bytes, whole.

That run found a client gap beside the folded attachments': a client whose
channel is *gone* at its exchange, rather than replaced under the same
identifier, had nothing that noticed. The client rule above covered a
changed incarnation; a poll refused *not found* for a channel the client
had read now asks `/channel/folded`, and where a log is answered keeps
what was read as an earlier copy (`sqex-chat` 0.93.1).

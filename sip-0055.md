## Abstract

An exchange answers a directory search with its own public channels and
its peers': it reads each peer's directory on an interval, as any client
could, and serves the union with every row naming the channel's **home**
-- the exchange that orders it, by key and domain -- and whether a copy is
held here. A person at one exchange finds a room at another without
knowing which exchange to ask, and is told where to go.

## Motivation

SIP-16 gave each exchange a directory of its public channels. SIP-39 §The peer directory gave
a client the list of exchanges its own federates with, so it could add
them one by one. SIP-43 let a channel be read and joined through a copy.
Between them a room at trunk.exchange is reachable from squic.org -- if
the person knows to look at trunk.exchange, or the copy happens to be
listed locally. Finding a room is still finding an exchange first.

Every exchange already knows its peers (SIP-39 §The peer directory) and every peer's directory
is public to any identified caller (SIP-16). What is missing is an
exchange asking on its members' behalf and saying, for each result, where
it lives -- which the local directory never had to say, since everything
in it lived here.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian.

### Reading the peers

An exchange keeps, for each entry in its peer directory (SIP-39 §The peer directory) that has a
domain, a copy of that peer's public directory: it discovers the domain
(SIP-33), checks the key, connects as itself (SIP-3, its own SIP-9 key),
and reads `/channel/list` page by page up to `PEER_ROWS` rows, every
`DIRECTORY_SECS`. A peer that refuses -- a whitelist that does not admit
this exchange, an older exchange, a peer that is down -- contributes what
it last contributed, for `DIRECTORY_STALE`, and then nothing.

An exchange MUST NOT read a peer's directory more often than
`DIRECTORY_SECS`, and MUST identify itself when it does: the peer may
decline, and should be able to see who asked.

### The search

```
Search | type: u8 = 0x1c | offset: u32 | q_len: u16 | query |
Found  | now: u64 | total: u32 | count: u16 | count × Row |
Row    | channel[32] | instance[32] | home[32] | dom_len: u8 | domain
       | here: u8 | members: u16 | last: u64 | name_len: u8 | name | topic_len: u16 | topic |
```

`POST /channel/search` is answerable to anybody `/channel/list` answers,
and matches `query` against name and topic as `/channel/list` does. It
answers the union of this exchange's own directory and every peer
directory it holds:

- A channel listed here -- ordered here, or held here as a copy (SIP-43)
  -- appears once, with `home` the exchange that orders it, `domain` that
  exchange's domain as recorded (empty for this exchange itself, whose
  name SIP-33 gives to clients), and `here` set. A copy is a row from the
  local directory and not from the peer's, so a channel replicated from a
  peer is not listed twice.
- A channel listed at a peer and not here appears with `home` the peer's
  key, `domain` the peer's, and `here` clear. Its `instance`, `members`
  and `last` are as the peer served them.

Rows are ordered as the local directory orders its own, local rows first,
then each peer's in the order of the peer directory. `total` counts every
match before `offset`; `MAX_DIRECTORY` bounds a page as in SIP-16.

### What a client does with a row

A row with `here` set is joined here, as any listed channel is; the join
is carried to its home where the home is elsewhere (SIP-43). A row with
`here` clear is at another exchange: the client reaches `domain` by SIP-33,
refuses it if the key found is not `home`, and joins there -- the
connection SIP-39 §What a client does with the directory already had it make to add an exchange, now made for a
room it was looking for. A client MUST show which of the two a row is;
"join" and "go there" are different acts.

### Limits

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| `DIRECTORY_SECS` | 60 |
| `DIRECTORY_STALE` | 15 minutes |
| `PEER_ROWS` | 256 per peer |
| Page | `MAX_DIRECTORY` (SIP-16) |

## Rationale

**Why the exchange reads the peers and not the client.** A client that
fanned out to every peer would make one connection per peer per search,
would be refused by any peer whose whitelist does not admit it (SIP-8),
and would disclose its searches to every peer. The exchange reads each
peer once a minute, as one identified caller, and serves searches from
what it holds; a peer sees an exchange reading its public directory, and
nothing about who searched for what.

**Why read the client route and not a peering one.** The directory is
public by SIP-16's own rule, and reading it as a client needs nothing a
peer has not already agreed to. A peering route would let a peer serve
more than its public directory, and there is nothing more to serve.

**Why `home` on every row.** A local row's home was always "here" and
never had to be said. Once rows come from elsewhere, and once a local row
may be a copy of a channel ordered elsewhere (SIP-43), a row without a
home is a room the client cannot find its way to.

**Why not replicate on demand.** A person finding a room at a peer cannot
have their own exchange copy it: a copy is authorised by the channel's
admin (`0x0b`), not asked for by a reader. The row says where the room is;
going there is the reader's, and the admin may authorise a copy later.

## Compatibility

Additive: one route. An exchange from before this document refuses it as
a route it lacks and a client falls back to `/channel/list`, which is the
local half of the same answer. Peers need nothing: they are read as a
client reads them.

## Security considerations

**A peer's directory is the peer's word.** A row from a peer names a
channel, a name, a topic and a count the peer served; a lying peer lists a
room that is not there or misnames one that is. That was true of adding
the exchange by hand (SIP-39 §What a client does with the directory) and is bounded the same way: the client
reaches the home by SIP-33, and what it finds there is what there is.
Nothing here is signed because nothing here is asserted beyond "the peer
said so", and the row says which peer.

**What the peer learns.** That this exchange read its public directory,
once a minute. Not who searched, nor for what.

**What the client learns.** The public directories of every exchange its
own federates with, which it could have read itself one exchange at a
time. Nothing private: a private channel is in no directory, at home or
away.

**An exchange does not extend its whitelist by reading.** It reads as a
client and is admitted or not; a peer that does not admit it is a peer
whose rooms are not found here, which is what the peer chose.

## Reference implementation

sqex 0.72.0: `sqexd` reads each labelled peer's directory every
`directory_secs` (default 60) as an identified client and answers
`/channel/search`; `sqex-chat`'s `/find` uses it, marks a row "at
<domain>, copied here" or "at <domain> -- connect there to join", and
refuses `/join` on the latter, saying where to go; against an exchange
from before this document it falls back to `/channel/list`. Test: two
exchanges, one federating with the other by label, a room at each: the
search at the federating one lists its own room at home and the peer's
rooms at the peer, not joinable; the peer, federating with nobody, lists
only its own. sigil's directory pane does not yet show homes.

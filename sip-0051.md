## Abstract

When a device opens a SIP-12 session toward another device of the **same
account**, the exchange tells the target -- one SIP-30 event, carrying the
opener's key -- and wakes it for that event (SIP-45). Before this, a phone
was handed history (SIP-42) only when its owner happened to have both
devices open at once, because SIP-12 discloses nothing about a pending
open and so nothing existed to wake for. The disclosure is bounded to the
one case where it says nothing new: an account's devices already know each
other by the registry, and one of them wanting the other is a fact the
account itself established.

## Motivation

SIP-42 gives two devices of one account a way to hand each other what the
exchange has forgotten, through a session the exchange cannot read. Both
have to be in the session at once: SIP-12's consent is mutual, and the
session forms when the second `open` arrives. A desktop keeps an `open`
outstanding toward each sibling and renews it; that is what lets a phone
that appears find a session waiting.

A phone does not appear. It is asleep, and SIP-45 wakes it only for a
SIP-30 event -- and SIP-12 raises none. That is deliberate and right:
"waiting / never-seen / nonexistent are one answer so `open` can't probe
for an identity", and an event on `open` would make every `open` a
presence probe against its target. So the desktop's outstanding open sits
there, the phone sleeps, and the sync that SIP-47's pairing ends with --
"both sides open a SIP-42 session toward each other and the phone takes
the history it wants" -- happens only if the person keeps the phone in the
foreground long enough, and never again unless they happen to.

The same gap catches SIP-47's step 4 on first pairing, a SIP-17 reseal to
siblings after a rotation, and any future device-to-device exchange. All
of them need the phone to be there when the desktop is, and nothing tells
it to be.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian.

### The event

SIP-30 gains a kind:

| kind | payload | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `0x0b` | `[32]` device | a device of your account has opened a session toward you (SIP-12) |

It is delivered to the **target device** only -- per device, not per
account: the session is with that device, and the account's other devices
have nothing to do. The payload is the opener's device key, so a target
with several siblings opens toward the right one.

### When it is raised

An exchange MUST raise it when a SIP-12 `Open` is accepted whose caller
and target are both devices presently registered (SIP-22) to the same
account -- or one of them is the account key itself and the other a device
registered to it -- and the target holds no established session with the
caller. It MUST NOT be raised for any other `Open`: SIP-12's rule that
nothing is disclosed about a pending open stands for every pair that is
not siblings.

**Once per open, not per renewal.** SIP-12 answers a repeated `Open` for the
same pair with the same ephemeral idempotently, and a client renews its
open by repeating it. An exchange MUST raise the event for the first
`Open` of a pending pair and MUST NOT raise it again while that open
stands; an `Open` after the previous expired, was closed, or was replaced
by one with a new ephemeral is a new open and is raised for. Without this a
desktop renewing every ten seconds would wake the phone every thirty.

### Waking

The event is not momentary: a device woken for it arrives to find the
open still standing (SIP-12 keeps one for an hour). SIP-45's exchange
MUST wake for it, under SIP-45's ordinary coalescing; it is not a ring and
does not bypass the interval.

### What the woken device does

Woken, it connects and reads the event. It MUST verify that the named
device is presently registered to its account (SIP-22's `List`, which
SIP-47 has it consult already) before opening toward it -- the event is a
hint and the registry is the authority -- and then opens (SIP-12) toward
the named device, the session forms, and SIP-42 runs. The phone's window
ends when the sync completes or its budget runs out; SIP-42 sessions
resume from `since`, so a window that ends mid-sync has lost nothing but
time.

A woken device that finds no open outstanding when it opens -- the
sibling gave up -- treats it as any other wake that found nothing: it
composes nothing and goes back to sleep.

## Rationale

**Why an event, and why on the stream.** Because that is the one path a
sleeping device is woken for, and SIP-45 exists precisely to turn an event
into a wake. Every alternative -- a signal in a channel, a mailbox message,
a beacon flag -- either is momentary and not woken for, is per account
rather than per device, or would need the phone to poll, which is the
problem being solved.

**Why only siblings.** SIP-12 refuses to disclose a pending open for a
reason this document does not weaken: an `open` that produced any visible
effect at its target would let anybody test whether a key is present. For
two devices of one account the disclosure is empty. The registry already
lists them to anybody; the account signed both credentials; that one wants
the other is a fact about the account's own devices, told to one of them.
Nothing is learned that the account did not itself arrange.

**Why the opener's key is in the payload.** A SIP-30 event is a hint and
never carries content; a device key is the same kind of thing the
membership event's account key is -- who, not what. A target with three
siblings needs to know which one, or it opens toward all three and two of
them are not there.

**Why once per open.** Stated in the section; the failure without it is a
wake storm the person would experience as their phone waking every
coalescing interval for as long as their desktop is up.

**Rejected: raising the event for every open, per account.** A single
"somebody opened toward you" without saying who, sent to the account,
would leak presence for every open to the target's other devices and
would still not say which sibling to open toward.

**Rejected: having the exchange complete the session on the target's
behalf.** It cannot: SIP-12's key agreement needs the target's static
private key at DH1 and DH2, and the exchange holding neither is the whole
design.

## Compatibility

Additive: one SIP-30 kind, which a client from before this document
ignores, as SIP-30 requires. An exchange from before it raises nothing and
a phone syncs as it did, when both happen to be there. SIP-12's wire is
unchanged; the exchange consults the registry it already holds at the
moment it already handles an `Open`.

## Security considerations

**Who can wake your phone.** Any device registered to your account, at will,
subject to coalescing. Such a device holds a credential your account
signed, and could already read your channels, post as you, and revoke
your other devices' juniors; waking your phone is the least of what it
has. A device that should not be able to is one to revoke (SIP-22), which
also stops the event: revocation removes it from the set this document
consults.

**What the exchange learns.** Nothing. It already saw the `Open`, already
resolved both keys against the registry to route it, and already knows
which of your devices holds a stream.

**What the distributor learns.** One more kind of wake, indistinguishable
from the others: four bytes, coalesced.

**A forged event.** SIP-30 events are hints, and the woken device checks
the registry before it acts; an exchange that lied about a sibling
would cause one `open` toward a device that is not there, which is what an
`open` toward an absent sibling always costs.

## Reference implementation

None yet. Planned: `sqex-proto::events::KIND_SIBLING` (`0x0b`) and
`Event::Sibling { device }`; `sqexd::session::open` consulting the device
registry and publishing through the route layer (never from inside the
session lock -- SIP-30's deadlock); `sqexd::wake::wakes` admitting it; the
phone's window in `sigil-phone` opening toward the named sibling.

## Abstract

SIP-40 rotates an exchange's key and says which holders of the old key
follow: a client's pin, a relay-peer entry, a client's store. Since then
an exchange holds other exchanges' keys in four more places -- the
channels it copies for its members (SIP-35, SIP-43), the origins an
account named when it moved here (SIP-59), the homes its accounts'
correspondents live at (SIP-59, SIP-60), and the lineage it learned
(SIP-64) -- and none of them follows. When such an exchange rotates,
every pull from it fails the pin check, every telling of it fails the
key check, and the account whose Move named it as home is homed, as far
as its old exchange can see, at a key nobody answers for. This document
has an exchange **follow a rotation wherever it holds the key**: when
the domain it holds a key for is found publishing another, it takes the
successor only on the retiring key's own signed word -- SIP-40's
handover, from the zone while the window is open or from the successor's
lineage (SIP-64) after it -- and re-keys what it holds, an account's
signed Move included, with the signature cleared as SIP-62 clears one
the account did not sign.

## Motivation

The SIP-64 live run put a laptop exchange in the position of a home that
had met trunk.exchange after trunk.exchange rotated, and gave it the
past. It did not ask what happens to a home that met the origin
*before* the rotation. That home holds the origin's old key in its
`home_origin` hints and in `replicated` for every channel it copies, and
pins it on every connection; the next pull finds a domain answering as
a key it does not hold and stops ("a pull reported an origin we did not
pin"). The account's copy goes stale, the account's posts through the
home are refused, and nothing in the log says why -- the same silence
SIP-40 fixed for the client store, one layer out.

The old home is worse off. It holds the account's signed Move naming
the home by key, and opens SIP-35's acts-for gate to that key alone. The
home's successor pulls and is refused as a stranger, and nothing but a
fresh Move from the account -- who may be on a phone that followed the
pin silently and sees nothing wrong -- reopens it. SIP-40 made a
rotation invisible to a client; SIP-59 made the client's home an
exchange; this is where the two meet.

SIP-40 §Consumers gives the rule: a key held for a domain SHOULD follow a
valid handover for that domain. It did not say how a holder that is not
looking at the zone at the right moment learns of one, and SIP-64 has
since answered that: the successor serves the chain the retiring key
signed, for as long as the history it covers. What is left is to apply
the rule at each place, and to say what following means for a record
the account signed.

## Specification

**Origin**, **replica** and **peer** are SIP-35's; **home**, **Move** and
**hint** are SIP-59's; **lineage** and **predecessor** are SIP-64's.

### What an exchange holds another exchange's key for

An exchange holds a key `K` *for a domain `D`* in each of these, and
each is a **holding**:

1. a channel it copies, whose origin is `K`, reached at `D` (SIP-35's
   `[[replicate]]`, SIP-43's shape, SIP-53's rehome hint);
2. an origin hint an account gave when it moved here: `(account, K, D)`
   (SIP-59 `Moving.origins`, SIP-60 `/peer/invited`);
3. an account's recorded home: `(account, K, D)` -- by the account's
   signed Move, or learned from the domain's exchange (SIP-60);
4. the lineage it learned for `K` (SIP-64);
5. the way it reaches `K`: an address and the domain, brought up on
   first contact.

A holding with no domain -- a key known by itself -- cannot follow, since
nothing says where to look; an exchange SHOULD record the domain
wherever it learns a key.

### Recognising a rotation

Whenever an exchange resolves `D` (SIP-33) to reach `K` and finds the
domain publishing a different key `K'`, it does not fail at once. It
takes `K'` as `K`'s successor when **either**:

- the resolution itself moved the pin from `K` to `K'` on a handover in
  the zone (SIP-40 §Resolution: `from = K`, `to = K'`, valid, within its
  window), or
- `K'`'s lineage (SIP-64, asked at `K'`) verifies for `K'` and `D` and
  names `K` among `K'`'s predecessors.

Both rest on the same two facts SIP-40 requires: the zone says `K'` is
live for `D`, and `K` signed the way to it. The lineage adds only that
the second fact is fetched from the successor rather than read from the
zone, which is what makes a rotation followable after the window. An
exchange MUST NOT follow on the zone's word alone -- a domain that
merely publishes a new key has changed hands, not rotated -- and MUST
NOT follow on a lineage that does not end at the key the zone publishes.

A chain of rotations is followed in one step: a lineage `K → K1 → K'`
names `K` among `K'`'s predecessors, and the holding moves to `K'`.

### Following

On recognising `K'` as `K`'s successor for `D`, the exchange re-keys
every holding of `K` for `D` -- and, where the holding carries no
domain, every holding of `K` -- to `K'`:

- **Copies.** The channel's origin becomes `K'`; the earlier origin `K`
  joins the channel's origin history (SIP-53), so entries the copy
  already holds go on verifying under it. The exchange asks `K'`'s
  lineage (SIP-64) and keeps it, so what `K` receipted before verifies
  at a copy that never held it either.
- **Hints.** `(account, K, D)` becomes `(account, K', D)`.
- **Learned homes.** `(account, K, D)` becomes `(account, K', D)`.
- **Signed homes.** The account's Move named `K`; the record becomes
  `(account, K', D)` **with its signature cleared**, exactly as SIP-62
  clears the signature of a record it re-keyed on a handover the account
  did not sign: the exchange acts on it -- SIP-35's acts-for gate opens
  to `K'`, `/account/home` answers `K'` with `since = 0` -- and does not
  carry it (`/peer/moved` carries only a Move the account signed). The
  next Move the account signs, naming `K'` or anything else, replaces
  it.
- **Lineage.** What was learned for `K` is dropped; `K'`'s is learned.
- **Reach.** The way to `K` is dropped; `K'` is reached at the address
  the zone gave.

The follow is logged once with `D`, `K` and `K'`: it is the one time an
operator can see it happen.

### What does not follow

- A configured origin (`[[replicate]]`) is the operator's, and SIP-40's
  `predecessors` is how the operator says a rotation happened; the
  exchange logs the mismatch and goes on refusing the pull until the
  configuration says otherwise.
- A relay-peer entry follows as SIP-40 §Consumers already says, on the
  zone's handover only; this document does not extend it to the
  lineage, since a relay peer is an operator's listing and a listing
  is not a holding for an account's sake.
- A transport whitelist entry, as SIP-40 says.

## Rationale

**Why the lineage counts as a handover.** SIP-40's resolution rule needs
a handover in the zone because that is where a client, on its own,
could find one; it withdraws after the window because a pin must not
move on a stale record. A holding an exchange keeps for an account is
not a pin: the zone has already been resolved and says `K'`, and the
question is only whether `K'` is the same exchange. SIP-64's chain
answers that with `K`'s own signature, which is the signature SIP-40's
record carries, and the zone's currency is checked by the resolution
that raised the question. Nothing is trusted that SIP-40 would not
have trusted; the window's purpose -- bounding what a stolen pre-signed
record is worth -- is served by rule three of SIP-64 (the chain must end
at the key the zone publishes), which a thief with a retired key cannot
satisfy.

**Why a signed Move is re-keyed rather than left.** The account said
"this exchange is my home" and named it by the key it then had; the
exchange rotated, as SIP-40 lets it, and the account's client followed
the pin without a word, as SIP-40 requires. If the old home did not
follow too, the account's own choice would be undone by a rotation the
account never saw, and the only remedy would be a Move signed from a
client that believes nothing is wrong. Clearing the signature is SIP-62's
precedent for exactly this shape -- a record the exchange acts on and
does not vouch for as the account's word -- and it means the re-keyed
record is never carried as if the account had signed it.

**Why not re-sign at the home.** The home could ask its accounts, on
their next connection, to sign a fresh Move naming the new key, and a
client SHOULD do that when it notices (SIP-59's `ensure_home` sees a
home record with `since = 0` and presents one). But the origins hold
the old record now, and a client that is not connected does not sign
anything; following at the origin is what keeps the account's copies
live in the meantime.

**Why one step for a chain.** A holding is looked at when it is used,
which may be long after several rotations; SIP-40's one-step rule is for
a pin moving in the zone, where each step is a key that was live. The
lineage is the whole chain, checked from the end, and there is nothing
to gain from walking to an intermediate key nobody answers for.

## Compatibility

Additive. An exchange from before this document fails as it fails today
-- the pin check, the key check -- and an origin or home from before
SIP-64 serves no lineage, so a rotation after the window cannot be
followed to it, which is today's behaviour. During the window, the
zone's handover suffices and a SIP-64-less successor is followed too.
Nothing on the wire changes.

## Security considerations

**What can move a holding.** A valid SIP-40 handover from the held key,
and nothing else: the zone publishing `K'` without one is treated as a
different exchange and refused, as SIP-33 refuses a changed key. A thief
with a retired key can sign a link and cannot make the zone publish
their key; a thief with the zone can publish a key and cannot sign a
link from the retired one. Both together is SIP-40's total compromise,
unchanged.

**A re-keyed signed home opens the acts-for gate to `K'`.** That is the
point, and it is bounded as the original was: `K'` may pull what the
account may read, and the account chose `K`, which vouched for `K'`. The
record is marked as re-keyed (signature cleared) and is not carried,
so no other exchange takes it as the account's signature.

**A lineage is asked of the successor over a connection pinned to
`K'`.** The chain verifies under `K`'s signature and ends at `K'`; the
transport authenticates `K'`. A lineage served by anybody else does not
verify.

## Reference implementation

`sqex` 0.83.0. `sqexd`: `Server::reach_by` follows a rotation where a
domain names another key -- on the discovery's `moved_from`, or on the
successor's lineage naming the held key -- through `follow_exchange
(from, to, domain)`, which re-keys the registry's `home` (signature
cleared), `home_origin` and `learned_home`, the channel store's
`replicated` (with an `origin_history` row) and `lineage`, and the
forwarder; the home task and the telling use it through `reach_by`. A
configured origin is not followed. Tests: a home learned by hint goes on
pulling after the origin rotates and the window has closed (the lineage
path), and reads what the old key receipted; an origin holding an
account's signed Move admits the home's successor and answers
`/account/home` with the new key and `since = 0`; a domain that changed
hands without a handover is refused; a configured origin is not
followed.

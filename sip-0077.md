## Abstract

SIP-31 has an exchange keep, per device and channel, the highest chain
position it accepted and the head after it, and report the pair in
`Info`; a device resumes from the greater of that and its own record. A
**copy** keeps no such mark: it reports the origin's, and where the
origin cannot be asked it reports zero, which a device that has lost
its record -- or must step back over a fork (SIP-74) -- has nothing to
put beside. This document has a copy report the device's chain **as its
own entries show it** when the origin cannot answer, and adds a route
that serves a device the heads after each of its positions the exchange
holds, so a device can rebuild its chain from an exchange without a
sibling and without a log of its own.

## Motivation

SIP-74 found that a device cannot rebuild its chain from what an
exchange serves: an action's signing input is never transmitted, so the
head after an action is known only to the device that signed it and to
the exchange that receipted it. The document made the client keep every
head it computes, which is right, and does nothing for a device that
kept none -- a store lost, or a device linked after the fact -- at the
one moment it needs a position below the latest.

That moment is a rehome at a copy whose origin is gone (SIP-53). The
copy holds the device's entries and actions up to where its pulls
stopped; it will rebuild every device's chain from them the instant it
orders the rehome; and until that instant it answers `Info` with zero,
because the origin it would have asked is the origin that is gone. The
device signs the rehome at the position it remembers, which is above
what the copy holds, and is refused `broken_chain` by an exchange that
could have told it exactly where to sign.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian. **Chain**, **position**, **head**,
`my_chain_seq` and `my_chain_head` are SIP-31's; **copy** and **origin**
are SIP-35's; a copy asking the origin for a device's standing is
SIP-43's.

### A copy reports what it holds

A copy answering `Info` (SIP-16) to a device reports the device's chain
as the origin says it, as SIP-43 has it. Where the origin cannot be
asked -- unreachable, gone, or from before SIP-43 -- the copy MUST
report instead the chain **as its own entries show it**: the position
after the highest `chain_seq` among the device's member entries and the
system entries recording the device's actions that the copy holds, and
the head after it, which is that entry's hash as SIP-34 receipted it
(the origin's `entry_hash`, held with the receipt). A copy holding no
entry of the device's reports zero, as before.

A device takes the greater of this and its own record, as SIP-31 says
of every report. Where the device's own record is higher because of
entries the copy never saw, and the device is rehoming the channel at
that copy (SIP-74 §The chain), the copy's report is the position the
rehome MUST be signed at, and the entries between are stranded.

### The heads by position

```
Chain   | type: u8 = 0x24 | channel[32] | from: u64 |     POST /channel/chain
Heads   | count: u16 | count × (chain_seq: u64 | head[32]) |
```

Answered to a member of the channel (SIP-16's reading rule) for **the
calling device**: for each of the device's positions at or above
`from` for which the exchange holds the entry -- a member entry the
device signed, or a system entry recording an action it signed -- the
position and the head after it (the entry's hash as receipted), in
ascending order; and, where the exchange keeps a mark for the device
(an origin, or a copy that has become one), the mark's position and
head last, if higher than any held entry's. At most `MAX_HEADS` per
answer; a caller asks again from the last position returned. A copy
answers from what it holds and does not ask the origin.

A device rebuilding its chain takes the highest position answered, as
SIP-31 has it take the exchange's report; a device stepping back over a
fork (SIP-74) takes the position at the fork. The answer is the
exchange's word about its own entries and is verifiable against them:
each head is the hash the receipt on that entry was signed over.

### Limits

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| `MAX_HEADS` | 256 |

## Rationale

**Why the copy's entries are the right answer.** A copy verifies every
entry it holds under the origin's receipt (SIP-35), and the receipt is
signed over the entry's hash -- which is the head the device's next
signature links from. The copy is not asserting a position; it is
reading one off entries the origin signed for. A copy that has just
become the origin rebuilds every device's chain from exactly these
(SIP-53), so the answer a device gets before the rehome is the answer
the new origin will hold after it.

**Why a route as well as the report.** `Info` says where a device
stands now. A device stepping back needs where it stood then; a device
checking its own record needs all of it. Both are one question over the
entries the exchange already holds, and answering it costs a query.

**Why only the calling device's chain.** Another device's chain is
readable from the entries anyway, position by position, by anybody who
may read the channel; but the head after a position is what the next
signature links from, and serving it as a list invites a client to sign
a chain that is not its own. Nothing here stops a member computing it;
nothing here helps.

**What was rejected.** Having the copy rebuild and keep marks for every
device as it pulls: it would be keeping state the origin keeps, for a
question the origin answers, to serve the one case the origin cannot --
and the entries answer that case already.

## Compatibility

Additive: one route, and an existing report answered where it was
answered zero. A client from before this document takes a copy's
non-zero report exactly as it takes an origin's (SIP-31); a client from
after it asking a copy from before it is told zero, and falls back to
its own record. An exchange from before this document refuses
`/channel/chain` with `not_found`.

## Security considerations

**An under-reporting copy induces nothing.** SIP-31's rule stands: a
device never signs below its own record on a report alone. The one case
it steps down (SIP-74) is on its own evidence of a fork.

**A copy cannot over-report.** Every head it answers is the hash under
a receipt it holds, verifiable by the device; a position it does not
hold an entry for it cannot answer with a head that verifies.

**Disclosure.** A device is told about its own entries, which it may
read, at an exchange that may serve them to it.

## Reference implementation

`sqex` 0.95.0. `sqex-proto::channel::{ChainAsk, Heads, MAX_HEADS}`.
`sqexd::channel`: `held_heads` reads a device's positions and heads off
the entries as `rebuild_standing` does -- a system entry's actor and step
from its body, its `entry_hash` as the head after it; `heads_for` adds
the mark last where higher; `chain_from_entries` is the copy's report.
`server`: `/channel/info` at a copy falls back to `chain_from_entries`
where the origin's standing cannot be had; `/channel/chain`. `sqex-chat`:
`Chat::chain_heads`, asked before the client rebuilds from its own
entries (SIP-74) when an exchange reports zero.

Tests: in SIP-53's exchange test, the replica whose origin has gone now
reports the admin's chain exactly as the test had rebuilt it by hand
from the replica's entries, and serves the heads ascending with that
position last -- checked by having the copy report nothing and watching
the assertion fail; SIP-74's client test, which rehomes at such a
replica, passes on the copy's report alone.

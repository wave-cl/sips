## Abstract

SIP-63 has an exchange answer a peer that is over its peering-write
limit with `rate_limited` and the seconds to wait, and says nothing
about what the peer does with that. The reference peer did nothing: it
logged the code where nobody looks, went on writing into a closed
window, and when a member's own act was refused behind it, told the
member the origin was away. This document says what a peer MUST do
with a limit: hold its writes to that origin for the seconds given,
pass a limit on a member's act to the member as a limit, say so where
an operator looks, and answer `/status` with what each origin last
refused and why.

## Motivation

The live run of SIP-60 §A device hints its home (2026-09-19). A home
carried a Move it had carried a hundred times
before; the origin, over the home's hour of writes, answered
`rate_limited`; the home's log said, at a level the journal did not
keep, *the origin did not take the move*; the home carried the create
behind it; the origin refused the create for want of the Move, with the
uniform refusal, which the home relayed to the member as `origin_away`.
The member's client said the origin could not be reached. The origin
was reached four times in that second.

Every party did what its document said. SIP-63 gave the limit a
distinguishable answer *so that* a peer could act on it, and no
document said what acting on it is. SIP-56 gave a client the seconds to
wait for its own acts, and no document carried that number across the
hop a copy adds. Three live runs read the symptom as a client timeout
at a different layer.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian. **Peering writes** and the
`peering` limit are SIP-63's; `rate_limited` and its detail are
SIP-56's; a **forwarded act** is SIP-43's (a post, an action, a create
carried to the origin for a member at a copy or a home); the **home
task** is SIP-59's.

### Holding off

A peer answered `rate_limited` on a peering write to an origin MUST
make no further peering write to that origin until the seconds in the
detail have passed, measured from the answer; its reads -- pulls,
waits, `/peer/mine`, standing -- are not limited (SIP-56 §Rate limits)
and continue. Writes it would have made in the window are made after
it, where they still apply: a Move is carried when its turn next comes,
a telling that was due is told. A peer that cannot parse the detail
holds off for `HOLD_DEFAULT_SECS`.

### Passing a limit through

An exchange forwarding a member's act (SIP-43, SIP-60) that is answered
`rate_limited` by the origin MUST answer the member `rate_limited` with
the same detail, as SIP-56 has the member's own exchange answer, and
MUST NOT answer `origin_away`, which is for an origin that did not
answer. The member's client shows the wait. The peer holds off its own
peering writes as above only where the limit was the *peering* one;
the origin's limits on the member's acts (`posts`, `creates`, ...)
are the member's and hold nothing at the peer.

An exchange forwarding a member's act refused for want of something the
peer failed to carry -- the uniform refusal after a Move the origin
would not take -- answers `origin_refused` (SIP-21 code 70), a refusal
distinct from `origin_away`: the origin answered, and said no.

### Saying so

A peer that is answered `rate_limited` by an origin, or whose write to
an origin is refused, MUST record for that origin the last refusal --
the path, the code, the wait where given, and when -- and MUST log it
at a level an operator's journal keeps, once per origin per window,
not once per request.

`GET /status` (SIP-9) gains:

```
"origins": [ { "key": ..., "domain": ..., "reached": <secs ago | null>,
               "held_until": <unix | null>,
               "last_refused": { "path": ..., "code": ..., "wait": ..., "at": ... } | null } ]
```

one row per origin the exchange forwards to or pulls from, in no
particular order. `reached` is when the origin last answered anything
(SIP-53 keeps it); `held_until` is the end of a hold in force;
`last_refused` is the record above, and stays until replaced -- a
refusal is a thing to have seen, not a thing to have been cleared.

### Limits

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| `HOLD_DEFAULT_SECS` | 60 |
| Origins in `/status` | 256 |

## Rationale

**Why hold off rather than retry.** The origin said when. A peer that
retries before then spends the answer it was given and, under SIP-63's
bucket, pushes the window further out; a peer that waits exactly as
long as it was told wastes nothing.

**Why the limit is passed through unchanged.** The number is the
origin's, about the origin's bucket, and the member is the only party
that can act on it -- by waiting, or by not sending. Turning it into
`origin_away` told the member to look for a network fault that was not
there, which is the failure the motivation records three times.

**Why `origin_refused` is a new word.** SIP-21's `origin_away` was
written for an origin that does not answer, and a client is told to
treat it as a copy that cannot reach its origin -- to wait, to try
elsewhere. An origin that answers *no* is a different situation with a
different remedy, and a client that cannot tell them apart cannot
choose it.

**Why the record stays.** A refusal that clears itself on the next
success is a refusal an operator sees only if they were watching at
the time. The status line is read afterwards.

**What was rejected.** Having the origin lift its limit for listed
peers: SIP-63 already lets an operator set it to unlimited, and the
peer's behaviour must be right under any limit. Retrying the write
behind the refused one with the Move re-carried first: the peer does
not know which write the origin wanted, and a Move it carried once is
recorded (SIP-60's reference implementation, 0.94.1, made the carry
once per Move).

## Compatibility

Additive: two status fields, one refusal code, and rules for the peer.
An origin from before SIP-63 answers the uniform refusal for
everything, and a peer holds off for nothing it cannot recognise. A
client from before this document treats `origin_refused` as a refusal
it does not know the word for, which SIP-21 tells it to show as such.

## Security considerations

**A lying origin can hold a peer off.** An origin that answers
`rate_limited` with a large wait stops a peer writing to it for that
long. It could refuse every write outright already; the wait is a
courtesy about its own state, capped at the peer's discretion.

**`/status` discloses which origins refused this exchange.** As it
discloses its peers already (SIP-9); the paths and codes are about
this exchange's own requests.

## Reference implementation

`sqex` 0.96.0. `sqex-proto::refusal::Code::OriginRefused` (70).
`sqexd::replica::ForwardFailed` -- `Unreachable`, `Limited(secs)`,
`Refused(code)` -- returned by `Forwarder::{forward, action}` in place
of a string, with `refused_forward` reading the wait off a 429;
`Server::forward_failed` maps it for a member at every forward site
(`/channel/post`, `/channel/create_at`, the carried actions), recording
the refusal; `note_origin_refusal` and `origin_held`; `run_homed`
records what `/peer/moved` and `/peer/folded` refuse and makes no
peering write to a held origin; `/status` gains `origins`. The chat
client says what `origin_refused` means. `HOLD_DEFAULT_SECS = 60`; a
stated wait holds for at most `MAX_HOLD_SECS = 3600`.

Tests: an origin whose peering limit is one an hour; the home carries
one account's Move and is refused the next with `rate_limited`, records
it under `/status` with the wait and a hold in force -- checked by
swallowing the refusal as before and watching nothing be recorded --
and a create carried for the refused account, which the origin refuses
for want of the Move, is answered `origin_refused`.

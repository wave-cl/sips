## Abstract

SIP-88 and SIP-89 refuse reactions, counts and a feed directory, and give
reasons. This document takes each refusal seriously enough to price it: what
the wire would have to carry, what the exchange would have to hold, and what
the objection would cost to overcome. Two of the three turn out to be
obtainable at a price worth paying, and **neither of those two is the design
the objection was written against**. The third is obtainable only by building
a transparency log, and is not recommended. One new SIP-19 part kind and one
optional field on a SIP-21 profile are proposed; **no new exchange route is**.

## Motivation

A reader arriving from any other publishing system looks for three things a
feed does not have, and SIP-88 §Nothing comes in and SIP-89 §Counting say why
each is absent. Both documents argue well, and both argue against **one
specific construction** of the thing — which is not the same as arguing that
nothing of the kind can exist.

This matters because the refusals are load-bearing and will be re-litigated.
SIP-89 says as much of exchange-held counts: "Recorded here because the next
reader will propose it." A refusal that records only the rejected design
invites the next reader to propose the same design again, or worse, to
conclude that the whole category is closed and build something off-spec.

So this document separates three questions that §Nothing comes in answers as
one:

1. May a reader say *this is good* about somebody's post? — **Yes, and it
   costs one part.**
2. May anybody state how many readers said so? — **No, and the price of yes
   is a transparency log.**
3. May a stranger find a feed they do not already hold the key for? — **Yes,
   if the author asks to be findable.**

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian.

### A reaction is a post in the reactor's own feed

SIP-19's part table gains one kind:

| `kind` | Meaning | Body | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0x09 | Regard | `emoji: utf8` | at most one |

A **regard** is a `Quote` (SIP-89, `0x08`) and a `Regard` in one post, in the
reactor's own feed, with no `Text` part. The quote names what is being
regarded; the regard says how. `emoji` is a single Unicode grapheme cluster,
at most 16 bytes, validated as SIP-19 validates a `Reaction`'s emoji today.

A post carrying a `Regard` and no `Quote` is malformed: a regard with no
subject is a mood.

**There is no new route and no new exchange behaviour.** A regard is appended
to the reactor's own feed by `/feed/append`, which already exists, and is read
by whoever follows the reactor, which already works. The exchange parses
nothing: SIP-19's body is as opaque as it has always been.

### What a client does with one

A client reading a post MAY show the regards it has **actually seen**, from
the feeds it follows, and:

- MUST name whose regard each is, which it knows because it read it in their
  feed;
- MUST NOT present them as a total, a score, or a number standing for anybody
  it did not read — SIP-89 §Counting, unchanged;
- SHOULD render a feed of nothing but regards as what it is, so that somebody
  who follows a prolific reactor is not reading a wall of other people's
  opinions about posts they have not seen;
- MUST resolve the quote to show what was regarded, under SIP-89 §Resolving
  one, with `QUOTE_DEPTH` and the unasked budget unchanged.

A regard is withdrawn by withdrawing the post that carries it, which is
SIP-88 §Withdrawal and needs no new rule.

### A directory nobody is in without asking

SIP-88's `Set` — the author's own feed policy — gains one field:

```
Set | type: u8 = 0x06 | retention_secs: u32 | max_posts: u32 | listed: u8 |
```

`listed` is 0 (the default) or 1. `Headed` gains the same byte, so an author
can read back what they asked for.

An exchange MAY serve a directory of the accounts it holds feeds for **whose
feed policy sets `listed`**, and MUST NOT include an account that does not.
The listing carries the account key and nothing else: no last-activity time,
no post count, no topic.

**Not the SIP-21 profile, which the first draft of this document proposed.**
That was wrong twice over. SIP-21's profile refuses unknown flag bits
outright — "a reserved profile flag set" is a decode error, which is the same
rule SIP-88 applies to its own `flags` and for the same reason — so a flag
bit would break every existing reader of *any* profile for a feature only
feed-aware clients want. And a trailing field fares no better where a caller
requires the buffer consumed.

The feed's own policy record has neither problem: everything that reads it
already knows what a feed is, so both ends move together by construction.
It is also the better home on the merits. SIP-88 says of `Set` that it is
"transport-authenticated and unsigned, by SIP-32's own test: pruning is the
exchange's act, nothing a reader repeats depends on the policy, and a signed
policy would be a portable statement whose enforcement is still entirely the
exchange's." Being listed is exactly that shape — one exchange's act, in one
exchange's directory, which no reader repeats.

```
Listed   | type: u8 = 0x01 | since: u64 | limit: u16 |
Listing  | now: u64 | count: u16 | count × (account[32] | listed_at: u64) |
```

`POST /feed/listed`, answered to any identified caller, refused to a caller
the account has blocked exactly as `/feed/read` is.

**No peer mirroring.** A directory is answered by the exchange that holds the
feed, for the accounts that asked it to. An exchange MUST NOT mirror another's
listing, which is what keeps this from reassembling the census SIP-88 refused.

### Counts are not specified here

No construction in this document produces one, and §Rationale explains what it
would take.

## Rationale

### Why a regard is not the thing SIP-88 refused

SIP-88 §Nothing comes in refuses a reaction in three sentences, and each is
about a different design:

> A reaction would have to land in the author's log, which only the author may
> append to; or in the reactor's, where nobody can count it, since no party can
> enumerate the feeds that hold one.

The second clause is not a refusal. It is a description of where a reaction
*can* go, with an objection attached — and the objection is to **counting**,
not to the reaction. This document takes the second clause as the design and
abandons the count, which the first clause never offered either.

> Making the exchange accept reaction-only entries from non-authors would
> require it to parse a SIP-19 body to decide what to store, which SIP-19
> forbids in as many words.

A regard requires nothing of the exchange at all. It is an ordinary post in an
ordinary feed, and the exchange cannot tell it from any other.

**What is lost is the total, and what is gained is that every regard has a
name on it.** "Ada, Bram and Chi thought this was good" is a fact a reader can
check: each of those is a signed post in a feed they follow, verifiable under
a key they hold. "1,247 likes" is a claim nobody can check, including the
person making it. The honest rendering is the one that was always available.

**It is also the only form that survives federation.** A regard travels as a
feed post travels — self-contained, signed, verifiable with no reference to
any exchange, equally valid on a USB stick, which is SIP-27's property and
SIP-88 §What the signing input deliberately omits. Any exchange-held reaction
needs a peer route, a signature scheme so a remote reactor cannot be forged,
and a blocking rule applied at both ends.

### What an exchange-held reaction would actually cost

Priced, because the next reader will propose it (SIP-89's own phrasing):

- **A new table of who reacted to what**, attributable and in the clear. That
  is a per-post list of readers, which is a *partial reading graph* — the one
  thing SIP-88 §The follow list is built to keep off the exchange, and which
  SIP-16 §The graph itself already calls "strictly more disclosure than
  anything else in the stack" for the narrower fact of who talks to whom.
- **An inbound path from strangers**, which SIP-88 says is "the whole of the
  abuse-resistance story". Under SIP-3 an identity is free to mint, so SIP-56's
  limits bound cost and not harassment: a thousand identities each within their
  rate is a thousand reactions.
- **Four to six routes**: react, unreact, read, a peer pair for federation, and
  a deletion cascade when the post is withdrawn.
- **Signed reactions**, not transport-authenticated ones, or a remote exchange
  can invent them.
- **A blocking rule at write and at read**, since SIP-21's block must not be
  announced and a blocked reader's reaction appearing would announce it.

None of that is impossible. It is a document of its own, and it buys a number
that §Counting still says may not be displayed.

### Why a count needs a transparency log

SIP-89 rejected exchange-held counts on four grounds, of which one is
structural: the count is "attributable, unsigned — so the exchange could
inflate it and nobody could audit an absence".

To overcome that, a count must be *checkable*. The shape is known: the
exchange commits to the set of reactors per post as a Merkle root, signs the
root, and serves inclusion proofs. A reader verifies their own regard is
counted; an auditor verifies the root matches the leaves it is served. That is
a transparency log, it is a SIP of its own at least the size of SIP-31, and it
answers only the inflation objection — the disclosure objection above is
untouched and gets worse, because now the set is committed to and published.

**A count that is worth having costs more than everything else in this
document put together.** The recommendation is to show what was seen and say
so: "regarded by 3 people you follow" is true, checkable, and does not need
the exchange to hold anything.

### Why opt-in defeats the census objection

SIP-88 refuses a directory in one sentence:

> One feed per account, listed with a last-activity time and mirrored to every
> peer every sixty seconds, is a timestamped census of every active account.

Every clause of that is a property of the *construction*, and three of the four
are removable:

| the objection | what removes it |
|---|---|
| one feed per account | the listing holds only accounts that asked |
| a last-activity time | the listing carries no time but when it was listed |
| mirrored to every peer | no peer mirroring; each exchange answers for its own |
| every sixty seconds | nothing is pushed; a caller asks |

What remains is a list of accounts that **published a request to be found**,
which is the same act as publishing a profile and is bounded by the same
thing: SIP-21 already lets an account choose to be visible, and an account
that sets `listed` has made a statement about itself as deliberately as one
that publishes a name.

**This is the one place SIP-88's reasoning was stronger than it needed to be.**
The census argument is correct against a directory nobody opted into, and it
does not reach one everybody in it asked for.

### What was considered and rejected

- *A regard as a `Body` type.* SIP-89 §What it is not settles this: an unknown
  body is ignored and an unknown part is skipped with the rest of the post
  shown, so a body-typed regard is a silent hole in an older client's timeline.
  A part degrades to a labelled gap.
- *Allowing a `Regard` beside `Text`.* It reads as a cheap way to say two
  things, and it makes "what is this post" unanswerable: a post with words and
  a regard is an opinion about a post, which is a quote, which already exists.
  A regard is the wordless form on purpose.
- *A regard naming a channel message.* SIP-89 §Why quoting a channel entry is
  not here applies unchanged: a direct message's identifier is a hash over its
  two accounts, so naming one lets anybody holding a candidate pair confirm
  offline that those two people talk.
- *A `listed` flag on the SIP-21 profile.* Proposed by the first draft of
  this document and withdrawn while implementing it: the profile decoder
  refuses reserved flag bits by design, so the change is breaking for every
  profile reader in the network rather than additive — and it would be
  breaking them for a feature none of them has. §A directory nobody is in
  without asking has the argument. A feed still has no *metadata*: SIP-88's
  `Metadata` body remains ignored, and a policy record is not metadata.
- *Serving the directory to unidentified callers.* SIP-88 §Routes already
  settles the shape: an identity is required so the exchange has something to
  apply SIP-56 and blocking against, and an implementation "MUST NOT present
  it as access control".

## Compatibility

**The two halves differ, and the first draft of this document got the second
one wrong.**

The `Regard` part is additive and nothing moves together: one row in SIP-19's
part table, skipped by `len` by any reader that does not know it.

The directory is **not** additive. `Set` grows by a byte and `Headed` with
it, so a client and an exchange must agree about the length — which costs
nothing in practice, because everything that speaks these routes at all
learned them in the same version. What it is not is a change an old peer can
ignore, and saying otherwise would be the kind of claim this section exists
to prevent.

A client from before this document skips a `Regard` by `len` and shows the
rest of the post, which is SIP-19's rule — so a regard reaches an older client
as a post carrying only a citation, which SIP-89 already defines as a bare
re-post. **That is a real misreading**: a regard would read as "carried this
into my feed without comment" rather than as an opinion about it. The cost is
stated rather than designed around, as SIP-89 states the equivalent cost for a
quote.

An exchange that does not serve `/feed/listed` answers as it answers any
unknown path, and a client MUST treat that as "this exchange has no directory"
rather than as an empty one — SIP-88's rule for its own routes, for its own
reason.

## Security considerations

**A regard is a public statement with a name on it, and permanently so.** It
is a feed post: signed, attributable, carried away by strangers, and covered
by SIP-88 §Security considerations' sentence that every post "is permanent in
practice". Somebody regarding a post is publishing an opinion about somebody
else's words to everybody who follows them. A client MUST make that as plain
as it makes it for an ordinary post, and the composer warning SIP-88 requires
covers this unchanged.

**A regard discloses what its author reads.** A feed of regards is a reading
list, published by the reader, about feeds they chose to follow. That is the
reading graph SIP-88 works to keep off the exchange — volunteered rather than
collected, which is a different thing, but the data is the same data and a
client SHOULD say so where a regard is composed.

**`listed` cannot be withdrawn from those who already read it.** Clearing the
flag stops the exchange serving the listing and does not reach any client that
has it. SIP-21's profile has exactly this property and states it; this
inherits it.

**A directory is an enumeration surface, bounded by consent rather than by
obscurity.** Combined with SIP-38's `Resolve` and `Reverse`, both public to any
identified caller, a listing yields names for keys. Everybody in it asked to be
found, which is the whole of the protection — and an account that sets
`listed` should be told, where it sets it, that this is what it means.

**Nothing here creates an inbound path.** A regard lands in its author's own
feed. SIP-88 §Nothing comes in is unchanged: there is still no reaction, no
reply, no comment and no route by which anything reaches a feed but its
owner's devices.

## Reference implementation

None yet, and deliberately so: this document exists to be judged before
anything is built. `sigil` holds the client half of SIP-88 and SIP-89 as of
2026-10-07 and would be where a regard is drawn; the resolver it already has
for a citation is the whole of what reading one needs.

**Both halves are small.** A regard is one part kind in `sqex-proto`'s message
encoder and one branch in a client's post renderer; the directory is one
profile field and one route. Neither needs an exchange to understand anything
it does not already understand, which is the test this document was written
against.

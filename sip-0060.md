## Abstract

Alice, whose home is `squic.org`, wants to write to Bob, whose home is
`trunk.exchange`. Today she can call him (SIP-39) and nothing else: a
direct message or an invitation she makes at her exchange lives there,
and Bob learns of it only by connecting to an exchange that is not his.
This document closes the gap with four small pieces, each on a mechanism
that already exists. Alice's exchange **locates** Bob -- finds his
exchange by the domain, resolves the name, learns his home and his
devices -- and remembers where he lives. It **proxies** the one-time
things she needs from his home, a device list and a prekey, so she
seals to him as she seals to anyone. When she puts him in a channel it
**tells** his home, and his home, holding his own statement that it is
his home (SIP-59), carries that statement to hers and pulls the channel
for him; from then on he reads and writes it where he is, by SIP-43.
And a **direct message lives at the home of the lower key**, so the two
of them never make two.

Two more pieces came from running it. The member's own word: a device
tells its home which origin to pull from (`/account/hint`), and a home
that carried a create to an origin records that origin for the member
itself, without waiting to be told what it already knows. And the Move a
client presents unasked is bounded to the exchange its store is filed
under, or none yet; anywhere else the client is a **visitor** -- it
presents nothing, says so, and moves only when told -- so a wrong
address is not a Move.

## Motivation

SIP-39 put the shape of federation in one sentence: a client holds one
connection, to its own exchange, and its exchange talks to the others.
Calls have had that since; conversations have not. SIP-43 let a member
post into a channel from a copy, SIP-59 let a home hold copies of
everything its accounts are in and carry their posts back -- but a home
pulls only the channels it has been told about, by the account, and the
account can only tell it about channels it already knows. A channel
somebody *else* makes for you, at an exchange you have never connected
to, is exactly the one nobody can tell your home about. That is every
first message from a stranger, and every invitation.

The other half of a first message is the key. A private channel's epoch
key reaches a member sealed to a prekey of each of their devices (SIP-17,
SIP-23), and those prekeys are published at the member's home. Alice
cannot take one from a pool at an exchange she is not a client of;
SIP-35 says why the pool must not be copied. What she can do is ask her
own exchange, and her exchange can ask Bob's -- one request, one prekey,
served once, as it would have been served to her directly.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-76.* Three live runs on an exchange nobody could reach inbound -- a laptop
behind its own address, taking part as a home -- ended the same way. A
member at the laptop opened a direct message that lived at
`trunk.exchange` (the lower key's home), and trunk, which could not open
a connection to the laptop, could not tell it; the member's client
waited ten seconds for a copy that was never coming and gave up. The
laptop had itself carried the create to trunk moments before. It knew
the origin. It had the member's own signed act naming it. It waited to
be told anyway.

The same happens without a laptop: an origin that is briefly unable to
reach a home, a home behind a firewall that admits only what it dials,
an origin from before §Telling the home that tells nobody. §Telling the home wrote *the
member finds the channel the way they always could* -- by connecting to
the origin -- and SIP-8's whitelist is exactly what stops that.

What is missing is the member's own statement. A home acts for an
account on the account's signature (SIP-59); the account's device is
connected to the home already, authenticated by SIP-3; and what the home
needs to know -- *pull from there* -- is a fact the device holds.

*Added 2026-09-20, was SIP-82.* The reference client's store is filed under one exchange -- its home
-- and re-filed when the account moves (SIP-59). The client therefore
knows, before it asks anything, whether the exchange it just connected
to is the one it lives at. The rule as first written (§A client says
where it lives) did not use that: it asked the *exchange* whether it had
a record, and an exchange that had never met
the account said no, which is also what the account's home would say
after losing its state. The two cases are different to the client and
were the same to the rule.

It happened while testing SIP-81: a scratchpad tool connected to a
laptop exchange as an account homed at trunk.exchange, to ask one
question about somebody else's devices, and moved the account to the
laptop on the way in. The laptop was listed nowhere and could reach
trunk.exchange, but the Move named no origins, so trunk.exchange went
on recording itself as home; the account had two homes and one of them
was a machine that would be switched off within the hour. The Move
that undid it was the same one request.

The user's own client has the same rule. `sqex-chat --server-host` at
an exchange one has never used is a Move, silently, with nothing to
say so afterwards but `sqex-chat home` at the right exchange.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian. **Home** is SIP-59's: the exchange
an account signed as its own. **Origin** is SIP-43's. The **home task** is SIP-59's; a
**device** and the account it registers under are SIP-22's; a store
**filed under** an exchange is SIP-40's and SIP-59's.

### Locating

```
Locate  | len: u16 | target |
Located | account[32] | home[32] | dom_len: u8 | domain | Devices |
```

`POST /account/locate` is answered to any identified caller. `target`
is `label@domain`, UTF-8, where `label` is a SIP-38 name or a base58
account key and `domain` is where an exchange is found by SIP-33. The
exchange finds the domain's exchange -- discovering it or using the
link it holds, checking the key it finds against the one it has pinned
-- resolves the label there where it is a name (as SIP-39 resolves a
callee), asks it `/account/home` (SIP-59) and follows the answer once
where it names a third exchange, and asks the home `/device/list`
(SIP-22). `Devices` is the home's answer as SIP-22 lays it out,
credentials included (SIP-32), so the caller verifies each device
against the account itself and takes nothing on the exchange's word. A
domain this exchange does not federate with is refused
`not_authorised`; a domain that cannot be reached, or a label that
resolves to nothing, `not_found`.

The exchange records the answer as the account's **learned home**: the
home's key, its domain, and when it was learned. A learned home is the
domain's exchange's word about who lives there -- the same trust a
SIP-38 resolution places in it, and no more. It is kept apart from a
signed Move (SIP-59) and is never mistaken for one: a learned home makes
this exchange **proxy** and **tell**, below; it never opens the acts-for
gate, which stays on the account's own signature. Where the exchange
holds both for one account, the signed one is what it goes by.

### Proxying the key's services

For a device whose account's home -- signed or learned -- is another
exchange, this exchange answers two routes by asking the home as a
client and handing back what it was given:

- `/prekey/take` (SIP-23): one prekey, taken at the home, served here
  once. The pool is not copied and nothing is kept; the take is the
  home's and the answer is its answer. A home from before this
  document, or one that will not admit this exchange, is `no_prekey`.
- `/device/list` (SIP-22), for an account with no devices registered
  here: the home's list, credentials included.

The home admits the asking exchange's key as SIP-39 already requires it
to for name resolution; an operator who federates with an exchange
lists its key.

### Telling the home

Whenever this exchange writes a present membership for an account whose
home is another exchange -- a `Create` naming it an invitee, an `Invite`,
a direct message opened with it, a forwarded create (below) -- it tells
the home:

```
PeerInvited | type: u8 = 0x10 | account[32] | channel[32] | dom_len: u8 | domain |
```

`POST /peer/invited` is a peering route (SIP-35), gated by the peer list
and answered with SIP-16's `Ack`; `domain` is the origin's own, the hint
the home finds it by. Sent once per membership written, best effort: an
origin that cannot reach the home has told nobody, and the member finds
the channel the way they always could -- or tells its home itself, and a
home that carried the create hints itself (§The home hints itself). Refused uniformly like every
peering route.

The home, on `/peer/invited` for an account that holds a Move naming
this exchange (SIP-59), records the origin among the account's hints,
carries the Move to it (`/peer/moved`), and pulls at once. It does no
more than that, and for an account with no Move here it does nothing:
a home acts for an account on the account's signature, and the
invitation was the origin's word on the strength of a learned home. The
loop closes on three statements, each made by the party entitled to make
it -- the domain's exchange says where the account lives, the account
says which exchange is its home, and the origin serves that home for
that account.

### A device hints its home

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-76.*

```
Hint    | origin[32] | dom_len: u8 | domain |          POST /account/hint
Hinted  | now: u64 | pulling: u8 |
```

From any identified caller. The account is the caller's own: the device
itself where it is registered as nobody's, else the account its
registration here names (SIP-22) -- never one the caller asserts. Where
that account's Move names this exchange (SIP-59), the exchange records
`origin` among the account's hints, with `domain` where given, and wakes
its home task, which pulls from the origin at once; `pulling = 1`. Where
the account is not homed here, nothing is recorded and `pulling = 0`: a
home acts for an account on the account's signature, and this device
has not got one. A `domain` may be empty for an origin the exchange
already knows how to reach -- one on its lists, or one it holds a link
or a forwarder to.

A hint is idempotent and adds to the account's hints; it does not
replace them as a Move's `origins` do (SIP-59). An exchange MUST bound
the hints it keeps per account at `MAX_HINTS`, dropping the oldest.

### The home hints itself

An exchange that carries a `create_at` to an origin for an account whose
Move names it (§Where a direct message lives) MUST, on the
origin's `200`, record that origin among the account's hints as if the
account had hinted it, and wake its home task. It knew the origin before
the origin could tell it; §Telling the home remains, and is what informs
a home that carried nothing.

### A client hints

A client SHOULD hint its home:

- after a `create_at` it made, naming the origin it named there --
  belt beside the braces above, for an exchange from before
  §The home hints itself (0.94.0);
- when it learns, from `/channel/home` (SIP-43), that a channel it is a
  member of lives at an exchange other than its own, once per origin;
- when a person tells it where a channel lives, by hand.

A client that opened a direct message elsewhere (§Where a direct message
lives) and is waiting
for the copy SHOULD hint before it waits.

### A client says where it lives

A client holding the account key, on connecting to its exchange, asks
`/account/home` for itself; where the answer is `not_found` or says this
exchange with `since = 0` -- no Move on record -- it signs a Move naming
this exchange (SIP-59) and presents it, once. A linked device does not:
it cannot sign for the account, and the account either did or will. A
Move naming the exchange the client is already at moves nothing and is
what lets the home act when an origin tells it.

*Amended 2026-09-20 (was SIP-82): only under §When a client presents a
Move unasked.*

### When a client presents a Move unasked

*Added 2026-09-20, was SIP-82.*

A client holding the account key, on connecting to an exchange `E`,
MAY sign and present a Move naming `E` without being told to, only
where **both** hold:

1. `E` has no home on record for the account (`/account/home` answers
   nothing, or a record with `since = 0`), as §A client says where it lives requires; and
2. the client's store is filed under `E`, or under no exchange yet.

Where the store is filed under another exchange `H`, the client is a
**visitor** at `E`: it presents no Move, reads and writes at `E` as
whatever `E` lets a member of channels ordered there do, and tells the
person that the account lives at `H` and that this is `E`. A visitor
moves only by an explicit act (SIP-59's Move as the client's `move`
command performs it), which names `H` and every origin the client
knows, as it does today.

A store filed under no exchange is a first run, and the Move it
presents is the account's first "this is my home". A client that can
be told a home some other way -- a restore (SIP-48), a domain the
person gave it -- SHOULD prefer that to the exchange it happens to be
pointed at.

### What an unasked Move names

A Move a client presents on its own (rule 2's first case: the store's
own home has lost the record) names as origins what `move` would name:
the exchange the store was filed under, and every origin of every
channel the client holds. That exchange is `E` itself here, and the
list is what lets `E` gather what §Telling the home promised.

### The residue of a Move by accident

A visitor at an exchange `E` whose record names **`E` itself** as the
account's home finds one of two parties behind: `E`, holding a Move a
client from before §When a client presents a Move unasked presented there and never displaced
because nothing told it of the later one; or the store, because
another of the account's devices moved the account to `E` since the
store was last filed. The two look the same from the store alone, and
acting in the second case would move the account back over the other
device's Move. So the client keeps, with the store, the `issued` of
the Move that filed it -- every Move it makes or presents -- and:

- where `E`'s record is **earlier** than that, `E` is behind: the
  client signs a Move naming `H`, the store's home, and presents it at
  `E`, which names nowhere the account is not. `E` then records the
  account as away at `H` and serves it as a former home does (SIP-59,
  SIP-81). The client says it did this;
- otherwise the client does nothing and says the store may be behind
  -- the account may have been moved here from another device -- and
  leaves the person to `move` or to follow. A store from before this
  document kept no `issued` and reads as behind.

*(The first release told `E` in both cases; the live run below found
the second.)*

A home told of a later Move naming another exchange -- by the client,
or by that exchange over `/peer/moved` -- records it and answers
`moved` for the account's home-only routes (SIP-59), and answers the
home's device list (SIP-81), as before; what these rules add is that a
client never causes the case where it is *not* told.

### Where a direct message lives

A direct message's identifier is derived from its two accounts (SIP-16),
so two people who each open it at their own exchange make two channels
with one name, and SIP-16's arithmetic cannot say which is the
conversation. This document does: **a direct message lives at the home
of the lower key** -- the `first` of SIP-16's derivation -- as its home
is known when the message is opened. The party at that home creates it
there as always. The other party creates it *there* too, from where they
are:

```
CreateAt | origin[32] | Create |
```

`POST /channel/create_at` names the origin the `Create` is for; the
request is signed under `origin` as SIP-43 signs every act made through
a copy, and the exchange forwards it as a `ForwardAction` with path
`/channel/create`, which SIP-43's list of honoured paths gains -- along
with `/channel/key/put`, a rotation, since the first message of a
sealed channel opened elsewhere is one. The
origin honours it under the acts-for gate for the creating account (the
forwarding exchange carries that account's Move first, as SIP-59 has it
carry every act), creates the channel as it would for a directly
connected creator -- SIP-16's rules for a returning party and for a
squatter apply unchanged -- and, the creator's home being another
exchange, tells it as above; the forwarding exchange pulls the channel
and the creator reads and writes it there. A client that finds the other
party's home is its own, or cannot locate them, creates at its own
exchange as before.

Groups live where they are made. A group's members are invited by an
admin who is where the group is, and the telling above is what reaches
them.

## Rationale

**Why the exchange locates and the client does not.** The client could
discover the domain itself and ask Bob's exchange; SIP-39's motivation
is the answer, and the whitelist is its enforcement: Bob's exchange
admits the exchanges it federates with, not every stranger's client.
One connection, to your own exchange, is the shape, and locating is the
first thing on this path that the exchange does for the client rather
than the client for itself.

**Why a learned home is not a signed one.** Because it is not: the
domain's exchange said it, and a domain's exchange can say what it
likes. What a learned home may cause is a proxied take and a message to
a home -- one prekey served once to a member who was going to be sealed
to anyway, and a notice the home acts on only under its account's own
signature. Nothing served to anybody rests on it.

**Why the home carries the Move rather than the origin asking for it.**
The origin does not know whether the account ever signed one, and asking
would make `/peer/invited` an oracle for it. The home knows, and acting
is its business: an account that never said "this is my home" has not
asked its exchange to gather anything for it.

**Why a client presents a Move naming the exchange it is at.** Without
one, the home can be told and cannot act; with one, everything an
origin tells it about becomes a copy it holds. Signing it on first
connection costs a signature and changes nothing that day, which is why
it is the client's default and not a step to remember.

**Why the lower key.** Any deterministic rule would do; this one is the
derivation's own ordering, already computed to make the identifier, so
neither client learns anything new to apply it. The alternative -- let
both create, then settle the fork as SIP-53 settles one -- strands
somebody's first message every time two people write at once, which is
exactly when a direct message starts.

**Why a forwarded create and not a create at the other exchange.** Alice
is not admitted at Bob's exchange and should not have to be; her
exchange is, on her behalf, by the peer list. The create is her own
signed act, carried as SIP-43 carries her posts, and Bob's exchange
verifies it under her key exactly as if she were connected.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-76:*

**Why the device's word is enough.** The hint causes one thing: the
home asks an origin what channels the account is in there (`/peer/mine`)
and pulls what the origin serves for that account. The origin serves
only what the account may read; the home holds only what it was already
entitled to hold under the account's Move. A device that lies about an
origin makes its home ask an exchange that will answer with nothing.

**Why the home hints itself.** §Telling the home put the telling at the origin
because the origin is the party that writes the membership. But a
`create_at` is the home writing the membership *through* the origin,
and it holds the origin's key in the request. Waiting for the origin to
say what the home just did was the round trip the laptop could never
complete.

**Why not a Move.** A Move carries hints (SIP-59) and replaces them; it
is signed by the account key, which a linked device does not hold; and
signing a new Move to say *also pull from there* is a heavy instrument
for a light fact.

**What was rejected.** Having the client pull the channel itself from
the origin: it cannot connect there, which is the case the hint
exists for. Having the origin retry the telling: it should, and §Telling the
home says nothing against it, but an origin that can never reach the home
retries forever.

*Added 2026-09-20, was SIP-82:*

**Why the store's word and not the exchange's.** The exchange can only
say whether *it* has a record. The store says where the account lives,
because SIP-59 re-files it on every Move the account makes from this
client, and SIP-42 keeps a second device's store in step. An
exchange with no record is either a stranger or a home that lost its
state, and only the store can tell which.

**Why a visitor is allowed at all.** A person pointing a client at
another exchange may mean to: to read a public channel there, to ask
where somebody lives (§Locating), to admit themselves (SIP-24). None of
those is a Move, and refusing to connect would make the client unable
to do the thing §Locating exists for. Saying so is enough.

**Why the first run still moves.** A fresh store at an exchange the
person typed is the person choosing a home, and there is no other
source to prefer where none was given. It is stated as the case these
rules do not close: a fresh store pointed at the wrong exchange
moves there, as it did before, and the person sees the home named on
the first screen.

## Compatibility

Additive: three client routes, one peering message, one honoured forward
path, and one proxied behaviour on two existing routes. It amends one
line of SIP-59: `/prekey/take` for a device of an account that moved
away is no longer refused `moved` but answered from the home, which is
what a sender wanted from it. An exchange from
before this document refuses `/account/locate` and `/channel/create_at`
with `not_found`, answers `/prekey/take` for a device it does not hold
with `no_prekey` as it always did, and refuses `/peer/invited` as an
unknown peering message -- so a home from before it is never told, and
its members find channels at other exchanges by connecting there, as
they did. A client from before it opens a direct message at its own
exchange; a client from after it, finding the other party's home is
older, does the same thing.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-76.* Additive: one client route and one rule on an existing one. An exchange
from before §A device hints its home (0.94.0) refuses `/account/hint` with `not_found`; a
client that sent it falls back to waiting, as it did. A home from before
that does not hint itself, and is told by the origin as before.

*Added 2026-09-20, was SIP-82.* The visitor rule is client only: no wire change and nothing at the
exchange. A client from before it (0.99.1) goes on presenting Moves wherever it is pointed;
an account moved that way is recovered as today, by a Move back. The
reference client's `ensure_home` answers which of the three cases it
found, and prints the visitor case.

## Security considerations

**A learned home is a hint, and everything that acts on it says so.**
Stated above and repeated because it is the load-bearing distinction:
the acts-for gate opens on a Move and on nothing else.

**What locating discloses.** To the home, that somebody at the locating
exchange asked after the account -- what a name resolution disclosed
before this document -- and to the locating exchange, the account's
devices and credentials, which SIP-22 publishes to anybody. The
locating exchange keeps the learned home, which is one more row in a
graph it already holds.

**A proxied prekey is one prekey.** The home serves it once and the
locating exchange keeps nothing; the envelope sealed to it is the
member's and lands in a channel the home pulls. An exchange that took
prekeys it did not need would drain a pool, which SIP-23's rate limits
bound and its recipient notices as a pool that empties.

**`/peer/invited` from a peer that lies.** The home records a hint and
carries a Move to an exchange that will refuse it if the account is not
there. The cost is a request; a peer on the list could make it anyway.

**The forwarded create is the creator's signed act.** The origin checks
it under the creator's key and admits it under the acts-for gate for
that account, which the creator's own Move opened. A forwarding exchange
cannot create in anyone's name but that of an account it has been
signed for.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-76.* **Authority.** A hint names an origin and nothing else, for the caller's
own account, as SIP-22 attributes the caller. It grants the home no
entitlement it did not have.

**Disclosure.** The home learns that its account may have channels at
`origin`, which the account's own client is telling it. The origin
learns, from the pull, that the home acts for the account, which the
Move the home carries already says (SIP-59).

**Denial of service.** Bounded per account by `MAX_HINTS`; each hint
costs the home one peering request per home-task cycle, which SIP-56's
peering limits already bound at the origin.

*Added 2026-09-20, was SIP-82.* **A Move is the account's word.** Nothing here changes who may move an
account: the key holder, by signature. It changes when the reference
client uses the key on the person's behalf, which is the only party
the visitor rule binds.

**A stranger exchange learns less.** Under the rule as first written an exchange
learned, from any connection by an unfamiliar account, a signed Move
it could present anywhere as the account's current home until a later
one displaced it. A visitor gives it a connection and nothing signed.

## Reference implementation

`sqex` 0.77.0; 0.94.0, 0.94.1 (hints); 0.99.1, 0.100.1 (the unasked
Move, client only). `sqex-proto::locate`, `peer::PeerInvited`,
`channel::CreateAt`. `sqexd`: `/account/locate` through the relay's
finder (name resolution, `/account/home` followed once, `/device/list`),
a `learned_home` table beside the signed `home` one and one `where_is`
that reads the signed record first; `/prekey/take` and `/device/list`
proxied to the home; `tell_home` after every membership written for an
away account (create invitees, invites, forwarded creates), off the
request path; `/peer/invited` at the home, which keeps the hint and
pokes the SIP-59 home task; `/channel/create_at`, carrying the creator's
Move before the create; a `domain` in the exchange's own config, which
is what it tells a home to find it by. `sqex-chat`: `ensure_home` on
every start, `locate`, and `open_dm` at the lower key's home; `^N
name@domain` and `sqex home locate`. Tests: an invitation reaches the
invitee's home and a prekey comes through the inviter's exchange from
the home's pool, once each; a direct message created from the other
side lives at the lower key's home; and a sealed direct message between
two clients at two exchanges, in both key orders.

Building it turned up one more path SIP-43 had left at the origin: a
**rotation**. A member reading a sealed channel through a copy could
not re-key it, and the first message of a direct message opened
elsewhere is a rotation -- so `/channel/key/put` is now carried as a
post is, the member's own signed action with the envelopes it sealed,
and a client that rotates through a copy waits for the copy to report
the epoch before it posts under it. And an account asking its own
exchange where it lives is known there by the asking, which SIP-59's
`/account/home` now says.

**Hints** (§A device hints its home to §A client hints, sqex 0.94.0).
`sqex-proto::home::{Hint, Hinted}`. `sqexd`:
`/account/hint` on the registry's account for the caller, through
`Registry::add_home_origin`, which now bounds an account's hints at
`MAX_HINTS`; the `create_at` route records the origin for the creator on
the origin's `200`, where the creator's Move names this exchange, and
wakes the home task. `sqex-chat`: `Chat::hint_home`, once per origin per
run, after a `create_at` and on learning from `/channel/home` that a
channel lives elsewhere; `homer hint <origin> [domain]` for a person.

Tests: two exchanges of which one cannot find the other, so the origin's
telling never lands; a direct message created at the unreachable side
through the home still arrives at the home, because the home hinted
itself -- checked by removing the self-hint and watching the copy never
come; a device of an account not homed here is told its hint pulled
nothing; and a group made at the far side for an account the home was
never told about arrives once that account's device hints by hand.

Run live on 2026-09-19 (`sqex` 0.94.1): the case in the motivation, a
direct message living at `trunk.exchange` opened from an exchange on a
laptop nobody could reach. The laptop hinted itself on the create it

sqex 0.99.1, client only). `Store::filed_under` reads the exchange the

## Abstract

SIP-53 moves a channel's origin, and where two exchanges ordered the
same positions during a partition, one ordering wins and the entries
the other ordered are **stranded**: kept for their authors, never served
as the conversation, and nothing puts them back. This document says
what a client does with them. A client that learns a fork it was on the
losing side of files what it holds above the fork as stranded where its
own, drops the rest, reads the winning history from the fork, continues
its SIP-31 chain from its last entry the winner holds, and offers the
person their stranded posts to send again -- each posted as a fresh
entry carrying, in a new SIP-19 part, when it was first said.

## Motivation

The SIP-53 live run ended with one entry stranded at the old origin and
its author's client showing it as the conversation, above a cursor the
winning history would never move past. Three things were wrong, and the
document had named only the third.

- **The client did not know.** It had posted at its exchange, the
  exchange had ordered the post, and the receipt was good. That the
  position was later taken by another exchange's entry is a fact the
  client learns only by reading the winning log from the fork -- which,
  with its cursor above the fork, it never does.
- **The client could not post again even if it knew.** Its SIP-31 chain
  had advanced over the stranded entry. The winning origin holds no such
  entry, so the client's next signature links to a hash the origin has
  never seen, and SIP-31's rule that a client never takes a lower
  position from an exchange -- written to stop an exchange forking a
  client's chain -- here stops the client mending its own.
- **Nothing offered to post it again**, which is what SIP-53 says the
  client SHOULD do.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian. **Fork**, **stranded**, the
**origin history**, `Home.former` with its `ended` positions, and
`/channel/stranded` are SIP-53's; **receipt** is SIP-34's; **chain** is
SIP-31's; **part** is SIP-19's.

### Learning the fork

A client holds a channel's origin history beside it (SIP-53 §What
readers verify). It learns a fork when the history gains a former
origin whose regime `ended` at a position **below the highest entry the
client holds** -- from a `Home` answer, from a rehome entry it reads, or
by rehoming the channel itself at a replica (below). Position `ended`
is the fork; what the client holds above it was ordered by the former
origin, and the client checks that: an entry above the fork whose
receipt verifies under the former origin and not under the origin that
took over is of the losing regime.

A client SHOULD ask `/channel/home` again whenever an entry's receipt
verifies under no origin it knows: a receipt from a new origin is how a
client with its cursor above the fork first meets it. A client that
carries a rehome to an exchange (SIP-53 `/channel/rehomed`) asks that
exchange again at once: it has just changed what the exchange answers.

A client that reads a channel through more than one exchange holds a
view per connection (SIP-43). The losing side is held in the view that
read it -- the connection to the origin that lost -- and is found there,
when that connection next learns the fork; a stranded post, once found,
is a fact about this device's words and not about a connection, and is
offered and sent from whichever connection can post.

### What is done with the losing regime

Of the entries above the fork that are of the losing regime:

- the client's **own** posts (SIP-19 `Post` bodies signed by this
  device) are kept aside as **stranded**, with the time each was first
  posted and its body as this client wrote it;
- everything else -- other members' entries, and the client's own
  reactions, edits and redactions, whose targets were positions in a
  history that no longer exists -- is dropped from what the client
  shows and holds as the conversation.

They MUST NOT be shown as the conversation, and MUST NOT be kept under
their sequence numbers, which the winning history now uses. A client
that holds none of its own above the fork -- a fresh store -- asks
`/channel/stranded` at the former origin where that is the exchange it
is at, and files what comes back the same way.

The client's fetch cursor goes back to the fork, and the winning history
is read from there as SIP-53 has a client read across a move: the
former origin's key verifies what came before the fork, the new one what
comes after.

### The chain

After the fork, the client's SIP-31 chain for the channel continues
from its **last entry the winning origin holds**: the position and head
the origin reports in `Channel` (`my_chain_seq`, `my_chain_head`) are
taken *in place of* the client's own where the client's own is higher
and every entry between is one it filed as stranded. This is the one
case in which a client takes a lower position from an exchange than it
remembers, and it is permitted because the client has verified for
itself that the entries between are of a regime that ended: an exchange
that under-reported here would have to show the client receipts under
the former origin for entries it never made.

A replica that has not yet become the origin reports no chain (SIP-53:
a replica tracked none, and rebuilds from its entries before it orders)
-- or, since SIP-43 §A copy reports what it holds, the chain as its
entries show it, which a client
takes as the origin's report.
The client then rebuilds its own the same way, from the entries it
holds up to the fork: a post of its own links over the entry's terms,
which it can recompute; an **action** of its own -- a system entry
naming its device, whose signing input the exchange never transmits --
links over a head only the device that signed it knows. A client MUST
therefore keep every chain head it computes, by position, for as long
as it keeps the channel; a client that kept only the latest cannot step
back over an action it signed.

A client rehoming a channel at a replica whose origin is gone (SIP-53
§Posted at a replica) strands, by that act, everything above the
replica's last position -- including its own. It MUST file those and
rewind its chain as above **before** it signs the rehome, or the rehome
entry itself links to a hash the replica has never seen.

### Posting again

Each stranded post is **offered to the person**, in the order it was
first posted, and sent again only on their say: it is a new entry, and
the person may have said it since, or no longer mean it. A post sent
again is posted as any post is (SIP-16; SIP-43 through a copy), with
one part added:

```
Part | kind: u8 = 0x07 | len: u32 = 8 | said: u64 |
```

`Said` (at most one per post) is when the poster first posted this,
the `posted` of the stranded entry, as the poster's own word about the
poster's own act, signed and sealed with the rest of the body. A
reader that understands it shows the message **at `said`** and marks
it as posted again; a reader that does not skips the part (SIP-19) and
shows the message at its `posted`, which is when it landed the second
time. A `Said` later than the entry's own `posted` is ignored: nothing
was first said after it was posted. The entry's `posted` remains what
orders the log; `Said` is how it reads.

Stranded posts that were replies (SIP-19 `Reply`) keep their `Reply`
part where its target is below the fork, and lose it otherwise: the
message it answered was stranded too. Attachments are kept: a blob is
referred to by its id and is not stranded with the entry (SIP-18),
though a client SHOULD check it is still held before offering.

### Limits

| Limit | Value |
|---|---|
| `Said` per post | 1 |
| Stranded posts kept per channel | 256, the oldest dropped past that |

## Rationale

**Why the client decides what was stranded, and not the exchange.** The
former origin keeps stranded entries for their authors (SIP-53), but the
author's client already holds them, in the clear, and holds the
receipts that prove which regime ordered them. Asking the exchange is
the fallback for a client that lost its store; the rule is written so
that a client with one needs no round trip to know.

**Why others' entries are dropped and not kept as history.** They are
not history: they are one side of a fork, and their authors are the
ones who can put them back. Keeping them under their numbers would show
two messages at one position; keeping them aside would be keeping
somebody else's stranded entries, which is the former origin's job and
is bounded there. SIP-71's earlier copies are different -- a whole
incarnation that ended -- and are kept because nobody can post them
again.

**Why a lower chain position is taken here and nowhere else.** SIP-31
forbids it because an exchange that reported a lower position could
make a client sign twice at one position, and the fork that follows
reads as the client's misconduct. Here the client signs at positions
the winning origin has never seen used, and has checked that the
entries it is stepping back over were ordered by an origin that lost
them. The evidence is the client's own; the exchange's report is only
taken once it is consistent with that evidence.

**Why `Said` and not a re-dated entry.** `posted` is the exchange's
stamp on the position it ordered, and SIP-34 receipts it. A client
cannot set it, and should not: the log's order is the exchange's. What
the client can say is when it said the thing, which is its own fact,
carried the way SIP-43 carries `Via` -- signed by the poster, shown by
the reader as the poster's claim.

**Why offered and not sent.** Time has passed. The person may have
answered again on the winning side, and a message sent twice reads as
a stammer; or the moment has gone. The client knows what was stranded;
only the person knows whether it still wants saying.

**What was rejected.** Re-posting with the original `posted` in place
of a fresh one: a receipt over a position carries the time the origin
ordered it, and a client-chosen time there is a forged receipt. Having
the new origin merge the stranded side: SIP-53 rejected it, since
renumbered entries are receipted entries with the wrong receipts.

## Compatibility

Additive: one SIP-19 part kind, which readers from before this
document skip; and client rules. Nothing changes on any exchange. A
client from before this document goes on showing its stranded entries
as the conversation, as it did.

SIP-31's client-side rule -- never take a lower chain position than
remembered -- is amended by the one case above.

## Security considerations

**`Said` is the poster's claim.** A poster can post anything with any
`Said`; a reader shows it marked as posted again, under the poster's
name, ordered where the exchange put it. It changes how a message
reads and not where it sits, and it is signed by the same key as the
words.

**The chain rewind cannot be induced.** A client steps back only over
entries it holds with receipts under an origin that its origin history
says ended -- an exchange that wants a client to step back has to show
it a fork that happened, and a fork that happened is exactly when
stepping back is right.

**Stranded bodies are the poster's own.** They were sealed to the
channel's members when first posted and are sealed again when posted
again, under the epoch in force then, which may have moved on; a
member removed in between does not receive the second sending.

## Reference implementation

`sqex` 0.92.0, in `sqex-proto` (`message::Part::Said`, `PART_SAID`,
`Post::said`, one per post) and `sqex-chat`. `store`: `stranded`
(unscoped on read, as `dm_alias`), `chain_log` (every head, by
position) with `head_after`, `reset_chain`, `truncate_above`,
`entries_anywhere`. `client`: `fork_check` / `fork_found` in the
document's order -- losing side by receipt, own posts kept, the rest
dropped, cursor back, chain back -- called from a `Home` answer's
`former` (the fork being the position before `ended`), from a rehome
entry read (after the batch), from a repudiated receipt (the home asked
again), and from `rehome` at a replica before it signs; `chain_from_held`
rebuilding the chain from own posts and own actions' logged heads where
the origin reports none; `stranded_posts`, `post_again`, `forget_stranded`.
The TUI shows how many posts are stranded, `/repost` and `/unstrand`,
and a message with `Said` at that time, marked *posted again*.

Building it found three things the first draft had not said, all now in
the specification: that a replica-turned-origin reports no chain and the
client rebuilds from what it holds; that an action's link cannot be
rebuilt from the entry the exchange serves, so every head a device
computes is kept; and that a client reading through two exchanges finds
the losing side in the view that read it. Two things beside them were
wrong before this document: a client carrying a rehome looked for the
entry only in the view it was connected through, which never held it
(the entry was read at the new origin); and a client with entries of its
own above a fork could not post again at all -- its chain linked to a
hash the winner had never seen, and the rule against taking a lower
position from an exchange, right everywhere else, stopped it mending.

Tests: an origin with a replica, partitioned by coming back where the
replica cannot find it, orders a post the replica never sees; the client
rehomes at the replica -- chaining on from what the replica holds, which
the first draft could not -- and posts on; when the partition heals and
the client carries the rehome, its view through the old origin keeps the
post aside as stranded, shows nothing of the losing side, and the post
is sent again from the winning side, read back saying when it was first
said, with nothing forged anywhere. Checked by never keeping a post
aside and watching the client hold nothing to send.

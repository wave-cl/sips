## Abstract

One identity signs a statement about another and lodges it with an exchange.
Others may read the statements about an identity and decide for themselves what
to make of them.

## Motivation

Every identity in this ecosystem is self-asserted. A key proves it is itself
and nothing more: that a base58 string operates a particular service, or was
audited, or has behaved well, is not something the key can establish alone.

Attestation lets identities make claims about each other, so a consumer with an
existing trust relationship can extend it.

## Specification

*Sketch. Not ready to implement from.*

A signed statement, lodged by its author, retrievable by subject:

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| context | domain separator, `sqex-attestation-v1` |
| subject | Ed25519 identity the claim is about |
| issuer | Ed25519 identity making it |
| claim | the assertion itself |
| issued_at, expires_at | validity window |
| signature | by `issuer`, over the above |

Open questions this SIP must settle before Proposed:

- **Claim format.** Free text is unprocessable; a fixed enumeration cannot
  anticipate uses. Probably a small registry of typed claims with an escape
  hatch, and the registry is most of the work.
- **Revocation.** An attestation outlives the connection that delivered it, so
  withdrawal needs its own signed statement — and a consumer that never sees it
  keeps trusting a retracted claim. Expiry is the only guarantee.
- **Whether the exchange validates anything** beyond signature and format. It
  cannot know whether a claim is true.

## Rationale

**These need signatures, unlike everything else here.** SIP-4, SIP-5 and SIP-25
lean on the transport: the connection proves who is speaking, and the claim
dies with it. An attestation is meant to be repeated to third parties who never
saw the connection, so it must carry its own proof. This is the one exchange
feature where transport authentication is insufficient, and saying so sharpens
what the rest are for.

The exchange is a convenient place to lodge them, not an authority over them. A
signed attestation is equally valid handed over on a USB stick.

## Compatibility

New protocol. Requires SIP-2.

## Security considerations

**No consensus and no scores.** There is no global view of who is trustworthy,
and this SIP must not grow one. Statements are inputs to a consumer's own
policy. Computing a reputation number would manufacture authority the data does
not support.

**Sybil.** Anyone can generate identities and have them vouch for each other. A
count of attestations is meaningless; only attestations from issuers a consumer
already trusts carry weight, and consumers MUST be able to filter by issuer.

**Attestations are public and permanent in practice.** Once read they can be
retained and replayed by anyone. Nothing lodged should be treated as
retractable, whatever the revocation mechanism says.

**Negative claims are dangerous** in a way positive ones are not: an
unaccountable assertion that an identity misbehaved is a defamation vector with
no adjudicator. Whether to support them at all is an open question, not a
detail.

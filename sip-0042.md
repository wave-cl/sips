## Abstract

Two devices of one account give each other the channel history each holds:
the signed entries, the epoch keys that open them, and the blobs they name,
over a session between the two devices that the exchange carries but cannot
read. A device admits only a sibling -- a device the same account has
credentialled -- and takes from it only what it can verify exactly as a
replica verifies an origin: by the entries' own signatures and receipts. The
exchange's retention window stops being the only copy of a conversation.

## Motivation

A second device today gets the exchange's retention window and nothing
older. The first device's disk is the only copy of the rest, and SIP-23
makes that copy unrepeatable: an epoch key's envelope is served once, so a
message the exchange has pruned and one device has opened exists in one
place. Lose the device and the conversation is gone; buy a phone and it
starts on a Tuesday.

The exchange cannot help. It prunes on a schedule the channel chose (SIP-16),
and a copy at another exchange (SIP-35) prunes on its own. What holds the
history is the device that read it, and what is missing is a way for it to
hand that history to the account's other devices -- which are, by SIP-20 and
SIP-22, the same person.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian. "Sibling" means a device holding a
SIP-20 credential from the same account, scope `sqex-chat`, current at the
time of the session.

### The session

Two siblings meet in a SIP-12 relayed session, each opening it naming the
other's **device** key. Both devices are online and each knows the other from
SIP-22's `/device/list`, so a device MAY keep an `open` outstanding toward
each sibling and sync whenever one appears; consent is SIP-12's, mutual by
construction. A device MAY instead, or afterwards, take the session direct
by SIP-25; the frames below are the same on either.

Every frame is sealed with the session key (SIP-12 framing, one frame per
`seq`) and carried on the session's reliable path. The exchange relays
ciphertext.

```
Frame   | type: u8 | body |

Hello   | type = 0x01 | account[32] | credential (SIP-20, self-contained) |
Have    | type = 0x02 | count: u16 | count × Held |
Held    | channel[32] | instance[32] | first: u64 | last: u64 | epochs: u16 |
Want    | type = 0x03 | channel[32] | since: u64 |
Entries | type = 0x04 | channel[32] | instance[32] | count: u16 | count × Entry(receipted) |
Keys    | type = 0x05 | channel[32] | count: u16 | count × (epoch: u32 | key[32]) |
Blob    | type = 0x06 | blob[32] | chunk: u32 | of: u32 | bytes |
Done    | type = 0x07 |
```

`Entry(receipted)` is SIP-16's entry as SIP-35 serves it: with SIP-31's
signature block and SIP-34's `head` and `receipt`.

### The exchange, in order

1. Each side sends `Hello`. A side MUST check that the credential's
   `account` is its own account, that its `delegate` is the peer's transport
   identity (the key the session was opened to), and that it verifies for
   scope `sqex-chat` now. Anything else ends the session; nothing further is
   sent or read.
2. Each side sends `Have`: every channel it holds signed entries for, with
   the channel's `instance`, the range held, and how many epoch keys it
   holds for it.
3. Each side sends `Want` for each channel the other holds beyond what it
   has: `since` is the highest `seq` it holds, or 0. A channel whose
   `instance` differs from the one this side knows is a different
   incarnation (SIP-16 §A reset sequence space); a side MUST NOT merge it
   and SHOULD want it from 0 only after resetting as it would on a fetch.
4. A side answers each `Want` with `Keys` for every epoch it holds of that
   channel, then `Entries` in ascending `seq` in pages of at most 256 and
   1 MiB, then `Blob` for each blob its entries name that it holds, in
   chunks as stored (SIP-18's sealed chunks, as served).
5. `Done` when a side has nothing more to send. The session ends when both
   have sent it.

### What a device MUST verify before storing

Exactly SIP-35 §"What a replica MUST verify", with the roles renamed: the
importing device is the replica, the *exchange the channel lives at* is the
origin, and the sibling is a courier whose word counts for nothing.

- Each entry: SIP-31's signature under the device that signed it; that
  device's SIP-20 credential from the entry's account (fetched from
  `/device/list` at the exchange, or already held); the device chain; and
  SIP-34's receipt under the exchange's pinned key, with the head linking to
  the previous stored entry where one is held. An entry failing signature,
  credential or receipt MUST be refused. A gap is stored; a fork is stored
  with its pair, as evidence.
- Each key: an epoch key is opaque; a device stores it and finds out whether
  it opens anything when it opens something. A key that opens no entry it
  holds is kept anyway -- a later entry may need it.
- Each blob: `blob_id` over the chunks equals the id named (SIP-18).

An entry the device already holds is not stored again. A device MUST NOT
move its fetch cursor on account of anything imported: the cursor is where
the exchange has got to, and the sibling is not the exchange.

### What a device sends

Only what it holds **signed**: entries it fetched with receipts and kept as
served. Entries a device opened before it kept signatures are not offered.
This is deliberate -- see Rationale -- and it means a device starts keeping
receipted entries from the day it implements this document, and syncs
those.

Epoch keys are sent in the clear inside the sealed frame. The session is
between two devices of one account, authenticated both ways by credential
and transport identity; a key the receiver's account already holds is not
a disclosure to it.

### Limits

As SIP-35's: pages of 256 entries and 1 MiB; blobs in their stored chunks. A
device SHOULD bound how much it accepts in one session and continue in the
next; a sync is idempotent and resumable by construction, since each `Want`
carries where the wanting side has got to.

## Rationale

**Why signed entries only, and not the plaintext a device already has.** The
sibling is the same person, and a device could simply be told what its
sibling read. Two things argue against it. A device that accepts its
sibling's word has no way to tell a compromised sibling's inventions from
history, and a compromised sibling is exactly the device one wants to limit;
signatures make the history checkable by anybody who holds it, later,
including the person reading it. And the plaintext copy is the *result* of a
verification the first device did and threw the evidence of; carrying the
result without the evidence would make the second copy weaker than the
first. The cost is that history from before a device kept signatures does
not sync. That cost is paid once.

**Why the exchange is the origin, not the sibling.** SIP-35 verifies a
copy against the party that ordered it, because that is the only party
whose signature says where an entry went. Here nothing changes: the entries
carry the exchange's receipts, the importing device holds the exchange's
key, and the sibling is the wire.

**Why a relayed session and not the exchange's own routes.** The exchange
has pruned what is being synced; that is the whole case. Sending history
through a store-and-forward at the exchange would ask it to hold what it
chose not to. SIP-12 relays without holding, and cannot read what it
relays, which is the property a channel's members were promised.

**Why keys go in the clear.** Sealing them per key, as SIP-17 does across
the exchange, protects against the exchange reading the envelope. The
session is already sealed under a key the exchange cannot complete, to a
device the account itself credentialled; a second layer would protect the
key from the party it is for.

**Why consent is per session and mutual.** A device that could pull from a
sibling that had not opened toward it could pull from a sibling that was
not there; the `open` from both sides is the presence check and the consent
at once, which is what SIP-12 was for.

## Compatibility

Nothing on any exchange changes. A device from before this document does
not open sessions toward siblings and is never synced with; one that does
finds nobody there and loses nothing. The frame types are this document's
own, inside SIP-12's opaque payload.

## Security considerations

**The credential is the door.** A session opened by a key that presents no
credential from this account, or an expired one, or one for another device,
is ended before a byte of history moves. The transport identity is the
device key SIP-3 carried on the connection to the exchange, which the
exchange named the session by; a credential naming a different delegate is
somebody else's.

**A compromised sibling learns nothing new.** It already holds the account's
keys and can fetch and open everything within retention; what it gains is
history the exchange pruned, which is history the account's other device
holds and would show its owner. What it cannot do is make the receiving
device *store* anything the exchange did not sign for.

**Revocation.** A device revoked at the exchange (SIP-22) still holds what it
synced, as any device holds what it read; SIP-22 says as much. What it can
no longer do is present a credential that verifies, so no sibling syncs with
it again -- provided the sibling checks against the exchange's current
device list rather than one it remembers, which this document requires.

**Storage.** A device now keeps two copies of each entry: the opened message
and the signed entry it came in. Retention is the device's own choice, as it
always was.

## Reference implementation

Planned: `sqex-chat` keeps receipted entries, its credential, and every
epoch key; `sqex_chat::sync` is the frames, the checks and a step-wise
runner over any sealed link; a relayed link over `/session/send` and
`/session/recv`; sigil keeps an `open` toward each sibling and syncs when
one appears, saying how much arrived.

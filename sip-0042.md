## Abstract

Two devices of one account give each other the channel history each holds:
the signed entries, the epoch keys that open them, and the blobs they name,
over a session between the two devices that the exchange carries but cannot
read. A device admits only a sibling -- a device the same account has
credentialled -- and takes from it only what it can verify exactly as a
replica verifies an origin: by the entries' own signatures and receipts. The
exchange's retention window stops being the only copy of a conversation.

An ended incarnation of a channel -- one destroyed and rebuilt (SIP-16),
a stray folded into the conversation (SIP-71) -- is kept by a device as
a **generation**: its signed entries and epoch keys beside what they
opened, never merged into the live one. Siblings offer, want and serve
generations over the same session, under the same verification, so an
earlier copy read on one device reaches the other as history.

## Motivation

A second device today gets the exchange's retention window and nothing
older. The first device's disk is the only copy of the rest, and SIP-23
makes that copy unrepeatable: an epoch key's envelope is served once, so a
message the exchange has pruned and one device has opened exists in one
place. Lose the device and the conversation is gone; buy a phone and it
starts on a Tuesday.

The exchange cannot help. It prunes on a schedule the channel chose (SIP-16),
and a copy at another exchange (SIP-35) prunes on its own. What holds the
history is the device that read it, and what is missing is a way for it to
hand that history to the account's other devices -- which are, by SIP-20 and
SIP-22, the same person.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-72.* On 2026-09-18, after three moves and three handovers, an account's two
devices tried to trade and one ended the session with *the sibling's
entries are from an incarnation the exchange does not serve*. The
sibling was not wrong to hold them: it had read that conversation. The
device was not wrong to refuse: §What a device MUST verify before storing
says the instance an `Entries` names must be the one the exchange states
today, because merging two
incarnations under one identifier is the fault SIP-16 warns of. What
was wrong was that the only two outcomes were *merge* and *refuse*.

SIP-71 added the third. A device keeps what it read of an ended
incarnation as an earlier copy, shown above the conversation that
continues and never merged into it. That settles what a device does
with its own history; these sections settle what two devices do with
each other's. Without it a phone bought after a fold sees the
conversation from the fold on, and the laptop's earlier copy stays on
the laptop -- which is the situation this document was written to end.

And a device that resets, today, keeps the plaintext it read and
discards the signed entries and epoch keys under them. A sibling can
verify only signed entries (§What a device sends), so the
discarded half is the half that could have travelled.

## Specification

All integers are unsigned big-endian. "Sibling" means a device holding a
SIP-20 credential from the same account, scope `sqex-chat`, current at the
time of the session. A *generation* is one ended incarnation
of a channel as a device keeps it.

### The session

Two siblings meet in a SIP-12 relayed session, each opening it naming the
other's **device** key. Both devices are online and each knows the other from
SIP-22's `/device/list`, so a device MAY keep an `open` outstanding toward
each sibling and sync whenever one appears; consent is SIP-12's, mutual by
construction. A device MAY instead, or afterwards, take the session direct
by SIP-25; the frames below are the same on either.

The session's reliable path is a byte stream: SIP-12 frames of at most 32
KiB, each sealed with the session key under its own `seq`, delivered in
order. The messages below are written onto that stream, each prefixed by
its length, and cut into frames wherever a frame is full -- a page or a
blob chunk larger than a frame spans several. The exchange relays
ciphertext.

```
Message | len: u32 | type: u8 | body |

Hello   | type = 0x01 | account[32] | credential (SIP-20, self-contained) |
Have    | type = 0x02 | count: u16 | count × Held |
Held    | channel[32] | instance[32] | first: u64 | last: u64 | epochs: u16 |
Want    | type = 0x03 | count: u16 | count × (channel[32] | since: u64) |
Entries | type = 0x04 | channel[32] | instance[32] | count: u16 | count × Entry(receipted) |
Keys    | type = 0x05 | channel[32] | count: u16 | count × (epoch: u32 | key[32]) |
Blob    | type = 0x06 | blob[32] | chunk: u32 | of: u32 | bytes |
Done    | type = 0x07 |
```

`Entry(receipted)` is SIP-16's entry as SIP-35 serves it: with SIP-31's
signature block and SIP-34's `head` and `receipt`. An `Entries` message is
at most 256 entries and 1 MiB; a `Blob` is one stored chunk. An
account with no linked device (SIP-22) is its own device, and its `Hello`
carries no credential.

### The exchange, in order

1. Each side sends `Hello`. A side MUST check that the credential's
   `account` is its own account, that its `delegate` is the peer's transport
   identity (the key the session was opened to), and that it verifies for
   scope `sqex-chat` now. Anything else ends the session; nothing further is
   sent or read.
2. Each side sends `Have`: every channel it holds signed entries for, with
   the channel's `instance`, the range held, and how many epoch keys it
   holds for it.
3. Each side sends one `Want`, listing each channel the other holds beyond
   what it has -- more entries, or more epoch keys -- with `since` the
   `seq` below which it lacks nothing of theirs: its own highest where its
   copy starts at or before the other's `first`, and one below the other's
   `first` otherwise. (A device that fetched only what the exchange still
   served holds the top of a channel and none of what came before; its
   highest would say it needs nothing.) One message, possibly empty, so
   that its arrival is the whole of what that side wants. A channel whose
   `instance` differs from the one this side knows is a different
   incarnation (SIP-16 §A reset sequence space); a side MUST NOT merge it
   and SHOULD want it from 0 only after resetting as it would on a fetch.
   A channel this side knows no incarnation of is wanted as offered.
4. A side answers each wanted channel with `Keys` for every epoch it holds
   of it, then `Entries` in ascending `seq` in pages of at most 256 and
   1 MiB, then `Blob` for each blob the page's entries name that it holds,
   in chunks as stored (SIP-18's sealed chunks, as served).
5. `Done` once the other's `Want` has arrived and every channel in it has
   been served. The session ends when both have sent it; a side MUST keep
   reading until it has both sent and received `Done`, since the other's
   history may still be in flight after its own.

### Generations

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-72.* When a device resets a channel's sequence space (SIP-16, SIP-71) it
MUST keep, as a generation: the incarnation's `instance` (SIP-31); the
exchange that ordered it -- the channel's origin as the device knew it
at the reset, SIP-43 -- as the generation's **origin**; every signed
entry it holds of it; every epoch key it holds for it; and what it
read, as SIP-71 already requires. The live tables start empty. Signed
entries of an ended incarnation MUST NOT remain among the live ones: a
sequence number belongs to one incarnation, and an entry left behind
is offered to a sibling as the conversation's.

Generations are shown oldest first, ordered by the time of the first
entry each holds; their numbering is local and never travels.

### Offering, wanting and serving a generation

After `Hello` and **before** `Have`, a device that holds generations
sends

```
Earlier     | type = 0x09 | count: u16 | count × Generation |
Generation  | channel[32] | instance[32] | origin[32] | first: u64 | last: u64 | epochs: u16 |
```

one row per generation, in the shape of `Held` with the origin added.
A device holding no generation sends nothing: `Earlier` is optional,
and a sibling that never sends it holds none or predates §Generations
(0.90.0).
It comes before `Have` so that a device answering `Have` already has it
in hand: step 3 above has each side send `Want` on receiving the other's
`Have`, and a device that had to wait for a possible `Earlier` after it
could not tell a sibling that holds none from one that has not spoken
yet -- and two devices each waiting on the other would never trade.

**Wanting.** A device that received `Earlier` decides per row, and sends its answer
**before** `Want`, so that `Want`'s arrival is still the whole of what
it wants (step 3 above):

```
WantEarlier | type = 0x0a | count: u16 | count × (channel[32] | instance[32] | since: u64) |
```

- A row whose `instance` is the one this device holds **live** is not
  wanted: the sibling has noticed a change this device has not, and the
  device's next poll settles it (SIP-71).
- A row whose `instance` this device holds as a generation is wanted
  from that generation's highest entry, where the row's `last` is
  beyond it or its `epochs` exceed the keys held.
- A row whose `instance` this device does not hold is wanted from 0.
- A row whose `origin` this device does not **know** is not wanted, and
  is never asked for: see Security. A device knows an exchange it has
  connected to, an exchange a channel of its lives at or lived at
  (SIP-43, SIP-53), and their earlier keys (SIP-40, SIP-64).

`WantEarlier` is sent only in answer to an `Earlier`, and only when it
is not empty.

**Serving.** A wanted generation is answered as step 4 above answers a wanted
channel, with its keys named for the incarnation they belong to:

```
KeysOf      | type = 0x0b | channel[32] | instance[32] | count: u16 | count × (epoch: u32 | key[32]) |
```

then `Entries` (`0x04`), whose `instance` field names the generation, in
pages as step 4 has them, then `Blob` for what the pages
name. `Done` is sent once `Want` has arrived and everything in it, and
in any `WantEarlier` that came before it, has been served.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-75 §Between devices.* That is where a
generation's files travel, and the requirement is explicit: a device
serving a generation MUST serve the blobs its entries name that it
holds, and a device importing one MUST keep them, exactly as for the
live incarnation. A device that never fetched a file before the fold
fetches it from the exchange that folded, while the folded attachment
stands (SIP-71 §Folded attachments), or from a sibling that did.

### What a device MUST verify before storing

Exactly SIP-35 §"What a replica MUST verify", with the roles renamed: the
importing device is the replica, the *exchange the channel lives at* is the
origin, and the sibling is a courier whose word counts for nothing.

- Each entry: SIP-31's signature under the device that signed it; that
  device's SIP-20 credential from the entry's account (fetched from
  `/device/list` at the exchange, or already held); the device chain; and
  SIP-34's receipt under the exchange's pinned key, with the head linking to
  the previous stored entry where one is held. An entry failing signature,
  credential or receipt MUST be refused. A gap is stored; a fork is stored
  with its pair, as evidence. The `instance` an `Entries` message names
  MUST be the one the exchange states for the channel today; a device
  that knows none records it, as it would on first signing.
- Each key: an epoch key is opaque; a device stores it and finds out whether
  it opens anything when it opens something. A key that opens no entry it
  holds is kept anyway -- a later entry may need it.
- Each blob: `blob_id` over the chunks equals the id named (SIP-18).

An entry the device already holds is not stored again. A device MUST NOT
move its fetch cursor forward on account of anything imported: the cursor
is where the exchange has got to, and the sibling is not the exchange. It
SHOULD move it *back* when a key it lacked arrives and it holds entries it
fetched and could not open -- nothing else revisits them (2026-09-19: a
device whose envelope never came read nothing of a conversation even after
its sibling handed it the key).

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-72.* **A generation.** Exactly the rules above, with two substitutions: the
incarnation the `Entries` names MUST be one this device asked for by
`WantEarlier`, in place of the one the exchange states today; and the
receipts verify under the generation's **origin** as this device knows
it, and that origin's earlier keys, in place of the exchange the channel
lives at. Signatures, credentials and chains are checked as they are for
the live incarnation; an entry failing any is refused. A `KeysOf` for an
incarnation not asked for is ignored. `Entries` for an incarnation
neither live nor asked for are refused, as the rules above refuse them.

What verifies is kept as a generation -- signed entries, keys, and what
the keys open -- and shown as SIP-71 shows an earlier copy. A generation
imported for an incarnation the device already holds one of is merged
into it by sequence number, which is safe because both are of one
incarnation. Nothing here moves a fetch cursor or touches the live
incarnation.

### What a device sends

Only what it holds **signed**: entries it fetched with receipts and kept as
served. Entries a device opened before it kept signatures are not offered.
This is deliberate -- see Rationale -- and it means a device starts keeping
receipted entries from the day it implements this document, and syncs
those.

Epoch keys are sent in the clear inside the sealed frame. The session is
between two devices of one account, authenticated both ways by credential
and transport identity; a key the receiver's account already holds is not
a disclosure to it.

### Limits

As SIP-35's: pages of 256 entries and 1 MiB; blobs in their stored chunks. A
device SHOULD bound how much it accepts in one session and continue in the
next; a sync is idempotent and resumable by construction, since each `Want`
carries where the wanting side has got to. A device SHOULD bound the
generations it keeps per channel and drop the oldest past the bound; no
number is set here.

## Rationale

**Why signed entries only, and not the plaintext a device already has.** The
sibling is the same person, and a device could simply be told what its
sibling read. Two things argue against it. A device that accepts its
sibling's word has no way to tell a compromised sibling's inventions from
history, and a compromised sibling is exactly the device one wants to limit;
signatures make the history checkable by anybody who holds it, later,
including the person reading it. And the plaintext copy is the *result* of a
verification the first device did and threw the evidence of; carrying the
result without the evidence would make the second copy weaker than the
first. The cost is that history from before a device kept signatures does
not sync. That cost is paid once.

**Why the exchange is the origin, not the sibling.** SIP-35 verifies a
copy against the party that ordered it, because that is the only party
whose signature says where an entry went. Here nothing changes: the entries
carry the exchange's receipts, the importing device holds the exchange's
key, and the sibling is the wire.

**Why a relayed session and not the exchange's own routes.** The exchange
has pruned what is being synced; that is the whole case. Sending history
through a store-and-forward at the exchange would ask it to hold what it
chose not to. SIP-12 relays without holding, and cannot read what it
relays, which is the property a channel's members were promised.

**Why keys go in the clear.** Sealing them per key, as SIP-17 does across
the exchange, protects against the exchange reading the envelope. The
session is already sealed under a key the exchange cannot complete, to a
device the account itself credentialled; a second layer would protect the
key from the party it is for.

**Why consent is per session and mutual.** A device that could pull from a
sibling that had not opened toward it could pull from a sibling that was
not there; the `open` from both sides is the presence check and the consent
at once, which is what SIP-12 was for.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-72:*

**Why a generation and not a second channel.** An earlier incarnation
is not a different conversation; it is the same two people before
something reset the numbering. SIP-71 chose to show it above the
conversation for that reason, and a sibling should receive it as the
same thing.

**Why the origin travels with the row.** A folded stray was ordered by
the exchange that folded it, which need not be the exchange the
conversation lives at now or the one either device talks to. Receipts
verify under the key that made them, and the importing device has to be
told which. The sibling's word for it is checked against what the
device already knows, not taken.

**Why `Earlier` comes before `Have`.** The other order deadlocks; see
the specification. Putting it first costs an older sibling the same
ended session either way, and costs a newer one nothing.

**Why `WantEarlier` comes before `Want`.** Step 5 closes the list on
`Want`'s arrival, and a serving side sends `Done` once everything in it
is served. A request arriving after that would be answered by nothing.
Sending it first costs nothing and keeps the end condition as it
was, which is why `Done` needed no change.

**Why a device that noticed nothing does not take the sibling's word.**
A sibling offering as *earlier* what this device holds *live* is ahead
of it, not wrong; but resetting on a sibling's say-so is exactly what
the rules above forbid for the live incarnation, and the exchange will say the
same thing on the next poll.

**What was rejected.** Bumping `Have` to carry origins and several
incarnations per channel: a device from before §Generations would
refuse nothing and merge nothing, but would want an earlier incarnation
as if it were live (step 3, *a channel this side knows no incarnation
of is wanted as offered*) and store it under the live tables. Separate
messages keep the old path exactly as it was.

## Compatibility

Nothing on any exchange changes. A device from before this document does
not open sessions toward siblings and is never synced with; one that does
finds nobody there and loses nothing. The frame types are this document's
own, inside SIP-12's opaque payload.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-72.* Additive: three message types. A device from before §Generations
(0.90.0) that receives `Earlier` ends the session as §What a device
sends says of an unknown type, which is the situation SIP-44 §Compatibility states for `Key`: a device that finds the
session ended after sending one learns the sibling is older, and says
so. A device that holds no generation never sends it and trades with an
older sibling as before. `WantEarlier` and `KeysOf` are only ever sent
to a sibling that sent `Earlier` or `WantEarlier`, which an older one
never does.

The rule *the instance an `Entries` names MUST be the one the exchange
states today* is amended by §What a device MUST verify before storing:
or one this device asked for by `WantEarlier`.

## Security considerations

**The credential is the door.** A session opened by a key that presents no
credential from this account, or an expired one, or one for another device,
is ended before a byte of history moves. The transport identity is the
device key SIP-3 carried on the connection to the exchange, which the
exchange named the session by; a credential naming a different delegate is
somebody else's. The credential is judged by the exchange's clock, read
from `/device/list` in the same breath as the device's standing, not by
the device's own.

**A compromised sibling learns nothing new.** It already holds the account's
keys and can fetch and open everything within retention; what it gains is
history the exchange pruned, which is history the account's other device
holds and would show its owner. What it cannot do is make the receiving
device *store* anything the exchange did not sign for.

**Revocation.** A device revoked at the exchange (SIP-22) still holds what it
synced, as any device holds what it read; SIP-22 says as much. What it can
no longer do is present a credential that verifies, so no sibling syncs with
it again -- provided the sibling checks against the exchange's current
device list rather than one it remembers, which this document requires.

**Storage.** A device now keeps two copies of each entry: the opened message
and the signed entry it came in. Retention is the device's own choice, as it
always was.

*Added 2026-09-19, was SIP-72.* **The sibling is still a courier.** Nothing is stored on its word:
every entry carries its author's signature and the origin's receipt,
and both are checked. What the sibling adds is the name of the origin,
and a sibling that named a key it controls could sign receipts under
it -- so the name is accepted only for an exchange the device already
knows. A compromised sibling can therefore not manufacture an earlier
copy of a conversation under an exchange the device never met, and
under one it has met it would need that exchange's key.

**Keys of a dead incarnation.** They open nothing that can still be
written to, and travel as the live keys do: in the clear inside the
session, to a device of the same account.

**Disclosure.** As above: two devices of one person, and the history
that person read.

**Denial of service.** A generation is bounded as a channel is; a
sibling offering many is bounded by the device's own limit on what it
keeps.

## Reference implementation

`sqex-chat` (the live trade); 0.90.0 (generations). `sqex-chat` keeps
receipted entries, its credential, and every epoch key;
`sqex_chat::sync` is the messages, the checks and a step-wise runner
(`Sync`) over any sealed link (`Link`); `Relayed` is the link over
`/session/send` and `/session/recv`, met by `Chat::meet_sibling`;
`sqex-chat device sync` runs one from the command line. sigil keeps an
`open` toward each sibling and syncs when one appears, saying how much
arrived.

**Generations** (§Generations and after, sqex 0.90.0), all of it in
`sqex-chat`. `store`: `generation`,
`entry_history` and `channel_key_history` beside SIP-71's
`message_history`; `archive_incarnation` at the one place every reset
passes (`reset_sequence_space`), which now also clears the live `entry`
table -- before 0.90.0 a reset left the signed entries where they
were, numbered against the new incarnation, and a sibling would have
been offered them as the conversation's; `generations`,
`history_entries_after`, `history_keys` and their `put_` halves.
`sync`: `Earlier` (`0x09`), `WantEarlier` (`0x0a`), `KeysOf` (`0x0b`);
`Owed` carries a generation; `serve` reads either table. `client`:
`held_generations`, `knows_exchange`, `import_earlier` -- the live
checks with the origin named in place of the exchange -- and `earlier`
ordering generations by the time of the first thing each holds.

Tests: a direct message destroyed and rebuilt, the phone keeping the
first incarnation as a generation with its signed entries and keys and
the live tables holding only the new; the laptop, which never saw the
first, receiving it as an earlier copy over a trade, verified, with the
conversation that continues untouched and a second trade carrying
nothing; a courier offering a generation under an origin the device
never met is not asked for, and one under the exchange it knows has its
altered entry refused and the rest kept. Checked by silencing the offer
and watching the laptop hold no earlier copy.

Run live on 2026-09-19 between two devices of one account at
`trunk.exchange`, the pair whose trade had failed on the 18th (which,
on 0.90.0, now trades cleanly: 30 entries one way, nothing refused). A
direct message with a third account was read on one device, destroyed
by both parties and reopened; that device's next poll kept the first
incarnation as a generation -- five signed entries, one key, two
messages -- and a trade carried it to the other device, which had never
read it: the same instance and origin, the two messages shown as an
earlier copy above the one message of the conversation that continues.
